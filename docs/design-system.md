# Design system

The two token layers of `@pbgo/ui` (platform and game), the palette, and the rules for components. The page `/design` shows every token and component: check it before building a screen.

## Design system (`@pbgo/ui`)
Black and white, dark theme only, Geist Sans in semibold with tight line-height and letter-spacing. No accent colour (but the presence of a friend: green online, blue in a game, orange-yellow inactive, red do not disturb: `--presence-online`, `--presence-playing`, `--presence-inactive` and `--presence-dnd`; and the brand of a sign-in provider on its button: `--brand-facebook` and `--brand-discord`), and a single gradient, neutral too: the backdrop of the home's hero (`bg-linear-to-b from-card to-background`), a little lighter at the top and black where the games start. Apart from those, game art is the only colour on the platform. Every token and component is shown on the internal page `/design` (`(hub)/design/page.tsx`, noindex, not in the sitemap): it is the reference, check it before building a screen.
- Two layers share the token names (`--background`, `--foreground`, `--primary`, `--muted`, `--border`, `--radius`, `--font-body`…):
  - Platform layer (hub, account, legal, admin, 404): values in `packages/ui/src/styles/platform.css`, scoped to the class `.platform`. The class sits on `<html>` (hub and 404, through `PlatformDocument`), on the admin wrapper and on the root of every portal (select, dropdown, dialog, sheet, tooltip, toast), which React renders outside the wrapper. Components: `@pbgo/ui/platform/*`
  - Game layer: the `:root` values of `packages/core/styles/game.css`, overridden at runtime by the game's Sanity skin (inline style on `<html>`, `themeStyle()`). Components: `@pbgo/ui/game/*`. A game changes its look by changing token values, never a component
- Boundary: the platform never sets a value on `:root` and a game never sets one on `.platform`. In `platform.css`, `@theme` only defines new names (`shadow-surface|float|overlay`, `duration-fast|base|slow`, `ease-standard`, `bg-scrim`, `max-w-page|reading`, `px-gutter`, `py-section`, `h-navbar|pt-navbar|scroll-mt-navbar`); a name Tailwind already has (type-scale line-heights, `--tracking-tight`, `--default-transition-*`, `--font-weight-*`) gets its platform value inside `.platform`, or every game would inherit it
- Rounding: the taller a box, the rounder its corner, up to the menus' step (the base `--radius` is 12px and every step a multiple of it). A corner is rounded by about two fifths of the height of what it rounds at most and never by half of it, so nothing is a capsule (but the highlight of a menu row, which shows under the pointer only and is the menus' own value, `rounded-2xl`): 31px (`rounded-4xl`) for a surface, any container from 6rem high (menu, popover, select, dialog, panels, card, game card, cookie banner, login card, an empty state); 26px (`rounded-3xl`) for a bar of 4rem, the home's search bar, which at `rounded-4xl` would be a pill; 22px (`rounded-2xl`) for a control or an island 3rem to 3.5rem high; 17px (`rounded-xl`) for one of 2.25rem to 2.5rem (button, field, tab bar, chat bubble); 12px (`rounded-lg`) for 2rem; 10px (`rounded-md`) for a chip, and `rounded-sm` for a badge. What sits in a padded container takes the container's radius less its padding, as near as the step and its own height allow. Only the picture of a person is a circle (`Avatar`, `rounded-full`, and its dot; the tracks and thumbs of a switch, a slider and a progress bar are the other exceptions). A game, its icon and its card are rounded like anything else, never a circle. Corners are smoothed a touch: every one is a superellipse (`corner-shape: superellipse(var(--corner-smoothing))`, `--corner-smoothing: 1.2` on `.platform`, where 1 is the arc and 2 the squircle; 1.5 squared the corners too much), except a `rounded-full`, which keeps its arc. `/design` (section `radius`) measures the scale and shows the smoothing, and `platform.test.ts` keeps the rules
- Text selection: the platform is an app, not a document, so its texts cannot be selected (`user-select: none` on `.platform`, in `platform.css`: the hub, the legal pages, the design system and the gamemaster; a game's page has its own root and its own rules). A field, a code sample (`pre`, `code`) and whatever carries `data-selectable` or the `select-text` class can be (a table code to copy, for instance); Safari needs the fields said, since one inside a text that cannot be selected would not take the caret. `platform.test.ts` keeps both rules
- In-game UI: not part of the platform layer. It is drawn in the game's own look (its skin tokens `--foreground`, `--surface`, `--accent-game`, `--font-title`, `--font-accent`, set by `themeStyle()`), tuned by `UI_THEME` (see `docs/game-ui.md`); never `@pbgo/ui/platform`, Geist or the `.platform` class. `/design › In-game UI` is the one place where the hub shows it: two games (Courtisans and the template) side by side, each inside a `GameScope` (`_components/game-scope.tsx`) that resets the platform's values (Geist, weight, line-heights, tracking, radius) and sets the game's tokens and font stacks on its own wrapper, never on `:root`. The hub does not load `game.css`: `globals.css` points `@source` at the four pure components (`seat-label`, `players-strip-view`, `announcement-view`, `game-over-panel`) and maps the utilities only games have (`font-display`, `bg-surface`, `bg-surface-dark`, `text-accent-game`) with `@theme inline`, which emits no variable. A new pure component drawn on `/design` needs its `@source` there. The font files of a game are not loaded on the hub, so its families fall to the next of the stack (Georgia); the colours, layout and motion are the real ones. Use `data-look`, never `data-game` (analytics counts the hub links that carry it)
- Palette: Tailwind's `neutral` scale (chroma 0), `destructive`, the four colours of a friend's presence and the two of a sign-in provider's button (below), nothing else. One font weight (600): `font-medium`, `font-bold`… resolve to it, so don't write them. The exceptions are the display faces, the title of the home and the finest print (`fine-print`), which each set their own weight (below). Depth: `shadow-surface|float|overlay` and `ring-1 ring-foreground/10`. Motion: `duration-fast|base|slow`, `ease-standard`. Shapes: `rounded-*`, scaled from `--radius`
- Motion (section `motion` of `/design`): three durations (`duration-fast`, 100 ms: overlays entering and leaving; `duration-base`, 150 ms: hover and focus, the default of `transition`; `duration-slow`, 250 ms: things that travel), one easing (`ease-standard`, `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`: quick to leave and slow to settle) and one spring, tokens of `platform.css`. Plain `transition` and `transition-colors` already use the base duration and the standard easing, so a component writes neither, and the guard test rejects `duration-200`. What CSS cannot do (a layout animation, a sequence, a spring) is done with `motion/react` (motion.dev, a dependency of `@pbgo/ui` and of `@pbgo/core`), which reads the same numbers from `@pbgo/ui/motion`: `DURATION` in seconds, `EASE_STANDARD` as the four numbers of the bezier, and `SPRING_TRAVEL` (stiffness 520, damping 42: a damping ratio of about 0.9, no overshoot worth seeing, settled in about a quarter of a second) for something that moves to a place it was not, the fill that glides between the navbar's tabs. `motion.test.ts` reads `platform.css` and fails when a copy has drifted. The games' UI shares the easing (`EASE_STANDARD` of `@pbgo/core/lib/motion-preference`) and has its own timings (`useMotion()`, `docs/game-ui.md`). Less motion is honoured everywhere: in CSS the three durations become 0ms under `prefers-reduced-motion`, in JavaScript `Navbar` mounts `MotionConfig reducedMotion=user` (the fill jumps) and `FaceCycle` does not rotate. The platform moves in JavaScript in two places only, the navbar's fill and the title's word; an animation answers an action or says that something changed, uses the tokens, and never has a duration or an easing written in a component
- Popovers (select, dropdown menu, popover) are translucent (`bg-popover/70`) and carry their blur on the popover element itself (`backdrop-blur-2xl backdrop-saturate-150`), never on a `::before`. A descendant with a blend mode (the `Avatar` ring is `mix-blend-lighten`) makes the popover an isolated group in Chromium, and a pseudo-element backdrop then blurs nothing: the page shows through the list, sharp. The game switcher (an `Avatar` in each of its rows) is the live check of this rule
- `PLATFORM_THEME_COLOR` (`@pbgo/ui/theme`) is the browser colour of platform pages; `theme.test.ts` keeps it equal to `--background`. `platform.test.ts` rejects `dark:`, palette and hard-coded colours, arbitrary sizes and the old `admin` names in `packages/ui/src/platform/*`
- The hub's game card is `GameCard` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/game-card`), built on `GameCardVisual`, its picture: the game's logo over its background on the `bg-muted` frame, no layer between the two (no scrim: the art shows as it is), the frame alone when there is neither. The page of a game on the hub draws the same `GameCardVisual`, larger, so the card and the page cannot drift apart. `/design` (section `game-card`) shows the card of a live game and of a game to come, then the picture alone
- The hub's navbar is `Navbar` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/navbar`; `NavbarIsland` is a glass island on its own, for what hangs on the bar beside the two containers), with `Logo` and `SearchBar` beside it: hand-written, not from shadcn, shown on `/design` (sections `logo`, `navbar`, `search-bar`). `Navbar` is two containers kept apart over a transparent bar, `h-navbar` high: `NavbarStart` on the left (the brand, then the tabs) and `NavbarEnd` on the right (the account: an avatar of `size-10`, or Sign in and Let's play as `lg` buttons). Each is a glass island, a rounded square (`h-14 rounded-2xl border bg-background/80 shadow-surface backdrop-blur-md`, its content `h-10` and `rounded-lg`: the island's radius less its `p-2`, so the corners stay concentric; `NavbarEnd` makes the buttons in it `rounded-lg` too, a button being a pill elsewhere) that takes the pointer back, while the bar between the two lets the page and the pointer through. The bar spans the window, not the page's `max-w-page`: the islands sit on its edges, `1.5rem` in from the top and from each side (the bar's `p-6`, with the same `1.5rem` left under them: with their own `3.5rem` that is the `6.5rem` of `h-navbar`). The brand is the home's button, a `size-10` square around the `Logo` (`h-6`, the size of the tabs' text): `current` marks it on the front page, where no tab is, the way a tab is marked on its own page: the bar's fill (`bg-muted`) behind it, with the logo as white as ever. That fill is one element for the whole bar (`layoutId` of `motion/react`, `SPRING_TRAVEL` of `@pbgo/ui/motion`): when the current item changes it glides to the next instead of jumping. It can only do that if the bar stays mounted from one page to the next, so the hub's layout mounts it once (`SiteHeader` of `apps/web`, a client component that reads the address to know what is current) and the pages do not draw it. `Navbar` mounts `MotionConfig reducedMotion=user` and a `LayoutGroup` of its own, so a visitor who asks for less motion gets the jump and two bars on one page (the specimens of `/design`) do not trade the fill The bar is pinned (`fixed inset-x-0 top-0 z-40`) and the page leaves `pt-navbar` under it or lets the backdrop of its first screen run under the islands (the home's hero does), and gives an anchor that lands below it `scroll-mt-navbar`; the tabs leave the first island under `md`. `Logo` is an inline SVG on `currentColor`, since `apps/web/public` holds `favicon.ico` only. `SearchBar` draws the search bar and nothing else: the page owns the text and what it finds (the home lays the bar over the bottom of its title, in the hero: its top edge sits on the baseline of the last line, so the letters stand on it and the tails of the script letters pass behind it)
- The hub's search bar is one glass bar, rounded by the step of a bar of 4rem and not a pill (`SearchBar` and its parts, `@pbgo/ui/platform/search-bar`, `h-16 rounded-3xl border bg-background/60 backdrop-blur-xl`, 8px of padding, so what is in it is `rounded-xl` and the magnifier's button `rounded-lg`, `bg-background/60` and `backdrop-blur-xl` so whatever is behind it shows through, blurred) with the magnifier at its start (`SearchBarSubmit`: a plain icon that is the button that sends the search, a ghost button, with no round button at the other end), the field, a clear button once there is a text and, at its end, the category (`SearchBarSegment`: an icon and what is picked, dimmed while nothing is, no label, a `Popover` whose panel holds a `Combobox`, `@pbgo/ui/platform/combobox`: a list with a search field above it, each option with its icon at the start of its row (`categoryIcon` of `apps/web`, a placeholder until Notion has a table of the categories with an icon, a colour and a description for each) and the check of a pick at the other end, the rows going from one edge of the panel to the other, case and accents ignored, the arrow keys, Enter and a click pick or unpick, any number can be picked so the list stays open, the list has no scrollbar (it scrolls all the same), the field has the focus the moment the panel opens (`autoFocus`), and the panel is `w-52` with no padding (`p-0`), its title and its Clear button 8px from the top; with nothing picked its value is left out under 48rem of room so the field keeps its room on a phone, and with picks (`picks`) the segment draws up to three of them with their icons, and their names from 48rem of room, then how many more there are, `+2`: under 28rem of room a phone shows two icons and the count; the panel opens to the end of the bar). The other filters are not in the bar: they stand under it (`SearchBarFilters`), centred, on a line of their own that wraps when the room runs out, each in a container of its own, a place of its own that is only a place (56px high, a rem of room on each side, no background, no outline, no shadow, the buttons in it `rounded-lg`; only the age, which is a button that opens a menu, goes gray under the pointer): the number of players as a stepper that starts on 4 (`SearchBarStepper`: a minus button, the number with its icon just after it, a plus button, no label, the two buttons outlined), the duration as the hourglass and the limit written after it (`SearchBarIcon` and `SearchBarValue`: the minutes and their mark, `30′`, with no unit word, or `1h+` for no limit) then a slider with one thumb (`Slider`, `@pbgo/ui/platform/slider`: twelve steps, "5" to "55" five minutes at a time and "1h+", the last being no limit; it starts on 20′, and its track is `w-16` with no dots, only the fill; the handle is a white vertical capsule 8px wide and 20px high; `mx-1` around it, since the handle sticks out of the track by half its width), the complexity as its icon and five bars of the same height that fill up to the level pressed, with nothing written beside them (`Meter`, `@pbgo/ui/platform/meter`, not `clearable` here: it starts on 3 and there is always a level, from 1 to 5), and the age as a menu that starts on 8+ (`DropdownMenu`, a `SearchBarSegment` that is `boxed`: "6+" to "18+", one choice, no "any"; it goes gray under the pointer and while its menu is open, like the category, and the menu is as wide as the button). Like the navbar they are hand-written, not from shadcn, and shown on `/design` (sections `search-bar`, `combobox`, `slider`, `meter`, `popover`). The panel is portaled, so like every portal it carries the `platform` class itself, and it sits at `z-50`, above the navbar's `z-40`. A modal overlay (a menu, a select, a dialog, a sheet) locks the scroll of the page, and `react-remove-scroll` hides the scrollbar and adds a margin of its width to the body, which made whatever is fixed, the navbar, jump by that width. The page keeps the place of its scrollbar instead, the way ui.shadcn.com does: `html.platform` has `scrollbar-gutter: stable` (which also keeps a page from changing width between a short one and a long one), so that while the lock is on the browser leaves an empty, inactive track where the scrollbar was, and the lock's margin is taken off the body (`html.platform body[data-scroll-locked]`, in `platform.css`). `platform.test.ts` keeps both rules from coming undone. Nothing in the row is filled in or outlined to say what is picked, since the filters are always at work: the number of a stepper is plain text that is never empty (it starts on 4 players, `DEFAULT_PLAYERS` of `@pbgo/catalog/filter`, the duration on 20′, `DEFAULT_DURATION`, the complexity on level 3, `DEFAULT_COMPLEXITY`, and the age on 8+, `DEFAULT_AGE`), and each press moves it one place along the options (`stepOption`), down to the first or up to the last, which the buttons stop at; the slider and the bars show what they are set to. The category segment is always drawn: with no category yet its panel says so. The filters' icons are the ones the game's card and page already use: `UserMultipleIcon` for the players, `HourglassIcon` for the duration, `Brain02Icon` for the complexity, `ChildIcon` for the age (and `GridViewIcon` for the category). The home's field says what the bar is for, "Search games, tables & tournaments...": only the games can be searched today (tables and tournaments have no list yet), and it is small and dim like a placeholder (`text-muted-foreground`, `text-base` on a touch screen since a smaller text makes iOS zoom the page in on focus, `text-sm` where the pointer is fine) while its placeholder, which iOS does not zoom for, is `text-sm` and, under 24rem of room, `text-xs`, so the sentence fits a phone from 360 px wide. The bar draws no ring or border around itself when the field has the focus, the caret says where the text goes
- The title of the home is three lines of capitals in Geist Black, all the same size (`text-display`): `display-title` (`platform.css`), weight 900 (the one weight the platform sets on its own for text: Geist's variable font has it, everything else is 600), a line-height of 0.9, the tight tracking of the headings (set on each line too: an `em` is inherited as a length, so a line bigger than the title would keep the title's) and the full colour of the text at the top, fading to 60% at the bottom (a mask over the whole title, `mask-image` from `foreground` to `foreground` at 60%, so that the backdrop shows through a little more towards the foot). BOARD GAMES, W/ FRIENDS and & RIVALS are not the same width, on purpose: 7.30em, 5.85em and 4.41em in that face, so the title narrows as it goes down. The size follows the window (`7.7vw + 0.5rem` up to 7.5rem: the first line takes 71% of a 390 px window and 60% of a 1440 px one). The search bar stands 32px under the title (`mt-8`) and covers none of it; it used to be pulled up over the foot of the last line by a token, `-mt-title-overlap`, which is gone. The hero's backdrop is `Tabletop` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/tabletop`, section `tabletop` of `/design`): a plain table seen from above at a low angle, in perspective, under a lamp, with the pattern of a game on it, made of CSS alone (the utilities `tabletop`, `tabletop-pool`, `tabletop-floor`, `tabletop-pattern`, `tabletop-lamp` and `tabletop-grain` of `platform.css`) and of `foreground` at a few percent, so no image and no colour of its own. The pattern is Courtisans' for now, the diamonds of its table (a lozenge, a dashed one inside it and a dot at every node), drawn as an SVG tile used as a mask over `foreground`, laid on a plane tilted back around its bottom edge (`perspective()` and `rotateX()`, its far end faded) and picked out of the dark by a pool of light (a radial mask); then come the glow of the lamp at the top and a fine grain (an SVG noise used the same way). The tile is `--tabletop-tile` where the table is nearest, 6rem on a phone and growing with the window up to 10rem, and the perspective and the height of the plane are measured in tiles, so it is the same drawing at every width. One day each game's own pattern may take its turn, one every six seconds, the way the word of the title did: that is not done. It is `absolute inset-0` in a `relative` section whose content is `relative` too, and fades out at its foot, where the black of the page takes over. Before it the hero had a grid of thin lines (`pattern-grid` in `platform.css`: lines of one pixel, 3rem apart, in a faint `foreground`, on a layer of its own faded out towards the foot by a mask) and, before it, a faint backdrop of game pieces (`BoardPattern`, `@pbgo/ui/platform/board-pattern`, section `board-pattern`); both stay, and putting either back is one layer in the hero (`<div aria-hidden="true" className="pattern-grid pointer-events-none absolute inset-0 mask-b-from-30%" />` in a `relative overflow-hidden` section whose content is `relative`). The face is meant to be able to change: it is `display-title`, the size (it follows the widths of the face's lines) and, for a face that is not Geist, the import of its font, nowhere else. `FaceCycle` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/face-cycle`, section `face-cycle` of `/design`) is the other way to set a title, kept for when the rotation comes back: one word of a line changes between four display faces every six seconds (`display-unkempt`, `display-jersey`, `display-fraktur`, `display-birthstone`, listed in `faces.ts`), out and in with `DURATION.slow` and `EASE_STANDARD`, the rest of its line gliding when it changes width; with `prefers-reduced-motion` it does not rotate. The display faces are the typefaces the logo's four letters are drawn in; their fonts are Fontsource packages (`@fontsource/*`, the latin subset) that `apps/web/src/app/globals.css` imports, so a game page never carries them. Each sets its own weight, since the platform's 600 on a face that has none would be a fake bold, and a scale of the parent's size that gives it the cap height of Geist's capitals (measured, not guessed). A title stays one text, written as it is (the capitals are CSS), for a screen reader and a search engine. It is for a title from `text-3xl` up
- The blocks of the home are `Section` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/section`, section `section` of `/design`): `Section` (as wide as the window between its `px-gutter` margins, 64px on a desktop, with no `max-w-page`, so the cards of a list fill a desktop screen, and `pb-section` below it, so a stack of them needs no spacing of its own), `SectionPreview` (a list that has more to show than the section has room for: what it holds fades out at the bottom, a `mask-b-from-60%` on the content alone, so it is opacity and no colour of the page, and its `action` sits over the fade, centred) and `SectionAction` (the way to the page of the tab: a link drawn as a button, bigger than a control of a page, `h-12` and `text-base`, since it is the one thing a visitor is asked to do there; the framework's link through `asChild`). A section has no visible title, what it shows says what it is, and a screen reader gets an `sr-only` heading. The home cuts its grid of popular games to three rows at every width (the cards beyond them are `hidden`, so they are neither drawn nor reached by the keyboard), and a section with nothing to list yet is a compact `Empty` (`p-6`) with the same button under it. `cn` knows the platform's own spacing names (`gutter`, `section`, `navbar`), so a `px-0` replaces a `px-gutter` the way it would a `px-4`; before that tailwind-merge kept both and the order of the generated CSS decided, which `utils.test.ts` now keeps from coming back
- The footer's backdrop is `Wordmark` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/wordmark`, section `wordmark`): the letters PBGO as SVG text in the four faces the logo is drawn in (the P `display-fraktur`, the B `display-unkempt`, the G `display-jersey`, the O `display-birthstone`), scaled by its viewBox, so always the width of its container. Each letter is placed by hand from its measured ink (a canvas `measureText` at 360 units, with the faces' own scales; measure again when a face changes), not from the advance of its glyph, which left the P and the B almost touching and a wide gap between the B and the G: the boxes of the ink of two letters are 20 units apart, except the P and the B: the P leaves much air at its lower right, so the B is 50 units closer, which makes the closest row of their shapes 19.5 units apart, like the other pairs (measured on the letters as drawn), the ink of the P starts at the left edge of the viewBox and the ink of the O ends at its right edge, and the letters are aligned on the middle of their ink, since the P and the O have a tail under the baseline that the B and the G do not. It fades in a straight line from white (`--foreground`) at 16% at the top of the letters to nothing at 92% of their height (an SVG gradient in the units of the viewBox with two stops, so nothing is left on the last 8%), a soft veil, and is `aria-hidden`: a drawing behind the text. `SiteFooter` (`apps/web`) is as wide as the site and draws, from the top, one row with the copyright and who built the site on the left (`COPYRIGHT_YEAR`, `COPYRIGHT_NAME` and `AUTHOR` of `@/site`, the author's name a link to their site) and the Privacy Policy, Cookie Policy (the cookies section of the legal notice) and Terms of Use links on the right (the first and the last are `LegalStubPage`s until their text is written; the Cookie settings link is there where analytics are on), then the wordmark from edge to edge, with no line anywhere, and, alone and centred under it, what the site is not (an unofficial adaptation: the games belong to their authors and publisher), the finest print of the page (`fine-print`: `text-xs`, a regular weight (400) and `foreground` at 20%, so dark that it is hard to read), `mt-1` (and its own `p-1`) under the letters and with `pb-5` under it; each text of the footer has 4px of padding of its own (`p-1`: the copyright, each legal link, the fine print) and the row has 20px (`p-5`), so the texts are 24px from the edges of the window, like the navbar's, never more
- The friends of a signed-in visitor are on the navbar of every page of the hub, the way the Riot Client shows them, in four parts (sections `friends-panel`, `friends-bar` and `social-panel` of `/design`), drawn from the friends, conversations and requests they are given and deciding nothing about them (where those come from, PLT-23, and what a button does are the page's; a button whose handler is missing is not drawn). A status is a shape, a colour and a word, never the colour alone: the dot on the corner of a picture (`PresenceDot`, five shapes: a green disc for a friend who is online, i.e. active; a blue disc with a play knocked out of it, an even-odd path over the dot's own `bg-background` so that the play is the colour of the page whatever the picture under the dot is, for one in a game; an orange-yellow moon for one who is inactive; a red disc with a bar knocked out of it for one who does not want to be disturbed; a thick grey ring for one who is away, its outline a quarter of the dot: 3px, 2px on a small picture, 4px on a large one) and, under their name, a line of quiet text (`text-muted-foreground`): only the dot is coloured, never the text. The four colours are tokens of `platform.css` (`--presence-online`, `--presence-playing`, `--presence-inactive`, `--presence-dnd`, utilities `bg-presence-*` and `text-presence-*`), the only hues of the platform besides `destructive`; sections `friends-panel` of `/design` draws the five dots. `FriendsBar` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/friends-bar`) is an island of the navbar (`NavbarIsland`), in a row with `NavbarEnd` on its left: a pill for each game some friends are in (its picture, `GameIcon`, and how many of them, up to four, the most crowded first, fewer on a narrow screen), which takes the fill of the navbar's current item and a line under it under the pointer, and opens a `Popover` anchored on the bar with that game's friends in two lists, `Online` (waiting in its lobby; those at one table together under it, `Table K7Q · 2/4`) and `Playing`; with no friend in a game it is the button that invites one. `SocialPanel` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/social-panel`) is what the account's picture opens, a column on the right (`fixed`, from the top to the bottom of the window, over the navbar's islands) with the visitor at the top (their picture and name are the trigger of the account's menu, `AccountMenu` of `apps/web`) and a button that folds it, then three icon tabs: the friends and what they are doing (`FriendsRoster`), the conversations (the last message, how long ago, how many are not read) and the way to add a friend with the requests; a tab that has something for the visitor, and the picture on the navbar, carry a count. `ChatWindow` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/chat-window`) is the conversation a press on a friend or a conversation opens, a card at the bottom of the page next to the panel. `FriendsPanel` is the same list as a panel for a page (header, search, sections, invite button), used by the page of the friends (`/me/friends`); it fills the height of its parent and scrolls its list. The pure part is `@pbgo/ui/friends` (`sectionsOf`, `gamesOf`, `tablesOf`, `activityLine`, `initials`) and `@pbgo/ui/social` (`sortedConversations`, `unreadOf`, `timeAgo`), tested without React. The hub mounts them once in the navbar (`SiteSocial`, `docs/accounts.md`)
- The sign-in's buttons are `ProviderButton` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/provider-button`, section `provider-button` of `/design`): one per provider, side by side in a grid of equal columns (the login card has four, `grid-cols-4 gap-3`), each a tile `h-10 rounded-xl`, wider than it is tall (about two to one in a row of four), with nothing on it but the provider's small mark (`BrandMark`, 20px, `@pbgo/ui/brands`: the brands' own marks, Simple Icons' (CC0) for Facebook, Apple and Discord and, for Google, the four colours of the G that its own sign-in script ships, never redrawn; that file is the one place of the design system where a colour literal is allowed, since the colours of a brand are the brand's), named `Continue with <name>` for a screen reader and in a tooltip, and showing a spinner in place of the mark while its sign-in is on its way. The tile is the brand's colour where the brand has one to give, a token each (`--brand-facebook`, `#1877f2`, and `--brand-discord`, `#5865f2`, utilities `bg-brand-*`, the only two the platform takes from a brand); Google's is white (`foreground`, with its four colours) and Apple's black (`background`, outlined, since the page is dark). A new provider adds its mark, its tile and its name (`PROVIDERS` and `PROVIDER_NAMES` of `@pbgo/accounts`), and `platform.test.ts` checks that each has a mark and a tile.
