# Catalog

The list of games the hub shows: the live games of the registry, then the games to come of the platform's own Sanity project (`docs/platform-sanity.md`), joined by `@pbgo/catalog`. Every game on the hub, a live one as much as one to come, shows the values of its `game` in that project; the registry only says which of them can be played. Part of PLT-56 ("the hub catalog shows every game"); PLT-83 made one row the hub's source for every game; PLT-90 moved that source from the Notion Games database to the platform's Sanity project.

## What exists
- Step 1 (PLT-71): the reader and the join, in this package. Step 2 (PLT-72): the route that serves the pictures of the games, `/api/catalog/<slug>/<logo|icon|background>` (below), and the order of the games to come. Steps 3 and 4 (PLT-73, PLT-74): the hub reads the catalog and lists every game in one list, and each card opens the game's own page on the hub, `/game/<slug>`, whether the game is live or to come, and the page ends with the games related to it (below). `[game]`, the game's own site, still serves the registry's slugs only
- Step 5 (PLT-75): the SEO of the game pages, `@pbgo/catalog/seo`: which of them search engines may index, their metadata and share image, their JSON-LD and the sitemap (below)
- PLT-83 (2026-10-27): the hub shows the values of one row for every game, Courtisans included, and a game's own Sanity project serves only once one enters the game (`/<slug>`, its table, its admin). The hub reads no game's own project. The registry's `live` stays the one switch between a game to play and a greyed "Coming soon" (the cards and the page read `entry.live` for it, and `entry.indexable` for search engines); the ruling holds "for now", so the switch is kept small on purpose
- PLT-86 (2026-10-28): the filters of the home move into its search bar: the categories at its end, in a panel with a list and a search field, and under it, bare (no container is drawn), the players as a stepper that starts on 4 (a minus button, the number, a plus button), the duration as a slider with one thumb that starts on 20′ (up to 5, 10 … 55 minutes, five at a time, or 1h+, which is no limit), the complexity as bars that fill and start on 3 (never below 1), and the age as a menu that starts on 8+. The categories, the minimum age and the complexity (the weight BoardGameGeek gives the game) are new values of a game; the availability filter and `?show=` are gone (below)
- PLT-19 (2026-10-30): the platform's own Sanity project, `playboardgamesonline`, is made and seeded with the catalog (`docs/platform-sanity.md`)
- PLT-90 (2026-10-30): the hub reads that project in place of the Notion Games database. `readSanityGames` (`src/sanity.ts`) is the reader, the pictures come from Sanity's image CDN, the order of the games to come is the `order` of the Studio, a game's credits are one line per role of BoardGameGeek, and the descriptions are English only until the translations are plugged in. Notion stays the team's internal tracker and is read by the platform's seed alone (`platform/content/notion/`): the site no longer has a `NOTION_API_KEY`. This file describes what has landed; each step updates it

## Where a game comes from
- Every value the hub shows of a game is a field of its published `game` in the platform's Sanity project (`mhgqs6fe`, dataset `production`, public), a live game's as much as one to come's: the name, the pictures (logo, icon, background), the description (English), the players, the duration, the age, the complexity, the categories, the people credited with what each of them did, the publishers and the rulebooks. Courtisans' card and page are its game of the Studio: where it and the registry disagree on a figure (the players, the duration) the hub shows the Studio's
- The hub reads nothing from a game's own Sanity project: that project serves once one enters the game (`/<slug>`, its table and its pages, another root layout)
- The registry decides what the Studio does not: `live` (a game to play or a greyed "Coming soon"), `indexable`, the slug, and the order of the live games (registry order, before all the others). A **live** game (`live: true` in `packages/games/src/registry.ts`) takes its game of the Studio; with none, or with the project down, it takes `registryRow(game)`: its name, its players and its duration when the registry has one figure, nothing else (a plain grey frame, a bare page), still listed and still enterable. There is no value-by-value fallback: a field the game leaves empty stays empty, the registry never fills it
- A **planned** game: a published `game` of the Studio. A game of the registry that is not live takes its game too, and the Studio's name with it; with none it is not listed. Only published documents are read (`perspective=published`): a draft is not on the hub until it is published
- `template` is never listed (`UNLISTED_SLUGS`), and neither are the expansions: a game's `kind` is `game` (the default when it has none), `reimplementation` or `expansion`. An `expansion` is played with another game, so it has no page: it is counted and left out (never reported), as the expansions of the Notion database were. A `reimplementation` (what Notion called a standalone expansion, a new edition: Flip 7 Vengeance and Time Bomb Evolution today) stands on its own and is listed like a game. The seed imports no expansion, so none is left out today
- No sync: the hub reads the published content of the Studio, never a copy of it. The registry stays the source of the slug, `live`, `indexable` and a game's own Sanity project

## The package (`packages/catalog`, `@pbgo/catalog`)
- `.` (`src/catalog.ts`, pure: no I/O, no framework) — the types (`CatalogGame`, `Creator`, `Publisher`, `CreditLine`, `CatalogEntry` = `{ slug, live, indexable, game, related }` where `game` is the game of the Studio, `Catalog` = `{ entries, source }`), `buildCatalog(games, rows)`, `registryRow(game)` (the row of a live game that has none), `compareGames`, `UNLISTED_SLUGS`, `pickLocalized(values, locale)`, `ART_SLOTS`, `pictureUrl(game, slot)` (the address of a picture in Sanity's image CDN, sized for the page), `cardPictureUrl(game)` (the logo of a card, else its icon), `sharePicture(game)` (the picture a page shares, below), `iconVersion(website)`, `publisherIconUrl(game, index)` (the address of the icon of a publisher, below), `creditLines(game)` (the credits of a game's page: the labels and the lines, and for a publisher its link and its icon)
- `./server` (`src/server.ts`, server only) — `getCatalog()`, `CATALOG_TAG`, `CATALOG_REVALIDATE_SECONDS`, `reportOnce(message)`. `getCatalog` is the one call a page makes
- `./filter` (`src/filter.ts`, pure like `.`, and the one that the browser imports) — the search and the filters of the hub's list: `GameFilter`, `NO_FILTER` (no pick at all), `DEFAULT_FILTER` (where the bar starts: 4 players and 8+), `GameIndex`, `indexEntry(entry)`, `filterGames(games, filter)`, `categoriesOf(games)`, `isFiltered`, `optionOf`, `stepOption` / `canStep` (what the stepper of the bar does), `complexityLevel` (the level a weight rounds to), `filterFromParams(params, offered)` / `filterToParams`, `normalize`, `words`, and the options it offers (`PLAYER_COUNTS`, `MANY_PLAYERS`, `DURATION_LIMITS`, `COMPLEXITY_LEVELS`, `AGE_LIMITS`, `DEFAULT_PLAYERS`, `DEFAULT_AGE`, `MAX_QUERY_LENGTH`). See "Search and filters", below
- `./route` (`src/route.ts`, server only) — `pictureRoute()`, the handler of the picture route: it serves the icon of a publisher's website, and sends the old addresses of the games' pictures to Sanity's image CDN; `apps/web/src/app/api/catalog/[slug]/[slot]/route.ts` is `export const GET = pictureRoute()`
- `./seo` (`src/seo.ts`, pure apart from Next's `Metadata` types) — `isIndexable(entry)`, `metaDescription(text)`, `gameSeo(entry, description)` (the `GameSeo` of a page), `gameMetadata`, `gameJsonLd`, `serializeJsonLd`, `sitemapItems` (below)
- `./picture` (`src/picture.ts`) — `fetchPicture(link)`: downloads a picture from a link and checks it (below); `download(link, kind)` is the download itself, which the page of a publisher's website is read with too. The platform's seed (`platform/content`) downloads the pictures of its Notion rows with it
- `src/sanity.ts` — the reader, `readSanityGames({ fetch?, init?, timeoutMs?, projectId?, dataset? })` (below), `CATALOG_QUERY`, `PLATFORM_PROJECT_ID`, `PLATFORM_DATASET`, `SANITY_API_VERSION`, `SanityError`
- `src/favicon.ts` — `fetchFavicon(site)`: the icon of a publisher's website, found from its page and downloaded (below), `iconLinks(html, base)` (the icons a page names) and `rank(link)` (which one is best)
- `scripts/list.ts` — `pnpm catalog:list`; `src/testing.ts` — the fixtures of the tests (a game as Sanity answers, a fake Sanity, fake picture hosts and publishers' websites), not exported
- Order of the list: the live games (registry order), then the planned ones by the `order` of the Studio, then by name (`compareGames`, below). A live game is never ordered by it: it is the registry's, wherever its `order` would put it
- `pickLocalized` gives a text for a visitor: their language, else English, else French, else any other; it says which one it took, so a page can mark a text that is not in the visitor's language. Every description is English today, so it always takes that one

## The Sanity read (`src/sanity.ts`)
- One `GET` of the project's public API through its CDN, `https://mhgqs6fe.apicdn.sanity.io/v2025-02-19/data/query/production?perspective=published&query=…`: a plain `fetch`, no SDK, no framework, and **no token**. The dataset is public: its published documents can be read by anyone, so there is nothing to configure, not even in a fresh clone. The caller hands in the cache options (`server.ts` gives Next's) and may hand in its own `fetch`, a project and a dataset (a test does). It answers `{ games, skipped, expansions, warnings }`; a failure is a `SanityError` (`status`, 0 when Sanity could not be reached; the message never holds an address)
- The query (`CATALOG_QUERY`, GROQ) takes the published `game`s that have a slug, oldest first (`_createdAt`: two games with one slug are settled the same way every time), and follows each reference to the document it points at in the same call, so one request is the whole catalog: the names of the categories, the creators with their roles, the publishers with their websites, the slugs of the related games and of the base game, the address of the file of each picture. A reference to a document that is not published comes back `null`, which the reader drops
- Read of a game: `name`, `slug`, `kind`, `order`, `bggId`, `releaseDate`, `playersMin`, `playersMax`, `duration`, `minAge`, `complexity`, `description`, `rulesFr|En|Es|De`, `categories`, `creators`, `publishers`, `related`, `baseGame`, `logo`, `icon`, `background`, and the document's last edit (`updatedAt`). Not read: `tagline`, `cover` and `boxFront`, a creator's photo, website and BGG id, a publisher's logo and BGG id, a category's icon, color and description, and the editorial documents: nothing on the hub shows them yet
- A game needs a name (one line of text that passes `nameProblem` of `@pbgo/games/slug`) and a slug that passes `slugProblem` (lower-case, 40 characters at most, not a route of the site). One that has not is reported in `skipped` with its reason, never guessed at, and the others are read; two games with one slug: the older one wins and the other is reported. A text is trimmed and its control characters removed (it goes to a terminal and to the logs), the names of people and publishers included
- A value the Studio lets through and the hub cannot use leaves its field empty, a game is never refused for it: a `playersMax` under the `playersMin` (empty means "N or more"), a `complexity` outside 1 to 5, a `releaseDate` that is no day, a rulebook or a website that is not an http(s) link, a picture that is not on the image CDN of the project that was read (the hub points a visitor nowhere else)
- The people credited: `creators` is one line per person with all they did on that game (`{ name, roles }`); the roles are BoardGameGeek's, `CREATOR_ROLES` of `@pbgo/studio-kit/constants`, the list the Studio's form shares, read in the order of that list. A line with no role of the list, or with no name (its person is not published), is dropped
- The links between games: `related` holds the slugs of the games its `related` list names, then its base game, each once, never its own slug and only games the reader kept (an unpublished game, an expansion or a skipped one is not a link). It is one side of a link as the Studio has it; `buildCatalog` makes it go both ways (below)
- The descriptions: `descriptions` is `{ en }` (or empty). The Studio has one English description per game for now, and the French, Spanish and German ones of the Notion rows were not imported: the hub shows English until the translations are plugged in (`docs/platform-sanity.md`)
- The project having no published game at all is not a failure: the hub then lists the live games of the registry alone, and says so (`warnings`)

## The order of the games to come
- The `order` field of the game in the Studio, lowest first, a game with none after those that have one, then the name (case-insensitive, accents aside, numbers in order, so `6 nimmt!`, `7 Wonders`, `10 Minutes`, `Bang`): `compareGames`. The seed numbered the games 10, 20, 30… in the order the hub listed them when it was seeded, which was the one of the Games Development database of Notion (Priority, then Status, then Effort, then the name), so one can be slipped between two. Nothing else orders the list: no priority and no status, and no card shows `order`
- A live game is never ordered by it: it is the registry's, in registry order, before all the others, whatever its `order` says

## The hub (`apps/web/src/app/(hub)/page.tsx`, and `games/page.tsx` for `/games`)
- Two pages read `getCatalog()` and give every entry, in the order the catalog gives (the live games, then the games to come), to `GameSearch`, which holds the search and the filters (below): the home (`(hub)/page.tsx`) and the list of games (`(hub)/games/page.tsx`, `/games`: what the home used to be, a title, the search bar and `GameList`, which draws the games that match in one grid; indexable, in the sitemap, `canonical` `/games`). The search bar of the header (`HeroSearch`) types and picks into it; its field says "Search games, tables & tournaments...", but only the games can be searched today. The home lays the glass bar over the foot of its title and draws the sections of the platform under it until a search is started (`HomeBody`). None has a visible title (what it shows says what it is; each has an `sr-only` heading). *Popular games* (`PopularGames`) is the grid of `/games` cut to two rows at every width, whose bottom fades out, with "See all games" over the fade, centred, to `/games` (`SectionPreview`, `SectionAction`); there is no count of plays to rank them by yet, one game being live and the rest to come, so they are the first games of the catalog in its order, and `PopularGames` is the one place that ranks the day there is a count. Then *Tables in progress*, *Tournaments in progress* and *What's new* (`EmptySection`: an empty state each and its button, "See all tables", "See all tournaments", "See what's new", to the page of its tab, until the list of open tables PLT-21, the tournaments PLT-31 and the release notes PLT-20 exist; the rows of tables will take the place of the empty state, with the same fade and button as the games; their words are `COMING_SOON`, `apps/web/src/lib/coming-soon.ts`, shared with the pages of the tabs). The page never sorts or splits the games again, and `order` only sorts, no card shows it. With the Studio's project unavailable the list is the live games alone, each one a `registryRow`: its name and its facts under a plain frame.
- Every card is the same tile, `GameCard` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/game-card`, drawn in `docs/design/03-components/04-surfaces.md`): the game's logo, contained and not cropped, over its background picture on a grey 4:3 frame (`bg-muted`, a platform token), with no layer over the art, then its name, on one line and cut with an ellipsis when it does not fit, and two facts, its players and its duration (`UserMultipleIcon` and `HourglassIcon`), and nothing else: no tagline, no placeholder. A picture the game does not have leaves the frame plain; with neither, the frame is a plain grey and the name sits under it
- A live game is `variant="play"`, a game to come `variant="soon"` with a small "Coming soon" badge at the top right of the card. `catalogCardProps(entry)` (`apps/web/src/lib/game-card-props.ts`) is the one place that makes a card's props, and the only thing it reads of the registry is `entry.live`, for that variant. Every card takes the same values from the game's game of the Studio: its logo is `cardPictureUrl(game)` (the logo, else the icon), its background `pictureUrl(game, "background")`, both addresses in Sanity's image CDN (below), and its facts are `playersLabel` and `durationLabel`. So Courtisans' card shows the pictures of its game in the platform's Studio, not the ones of its own project: a change in the platform's Studio reaches the hub once the catalog has read it again (below), a change in the game's own Studio does not
- Every card is a plain `<a>` to the game's page on the hub, `/game/<slug>` (below), the games to come as much as the live ones. `GameCard` requires an `href`, so a card that goes nowhere cannot be drawn; `catalogCardProps` holds that one rule
- Only the card of a live game carries `data-game`: analytics counts the games opened from the hub (`game_opened_from_hub`), and a game to come is not one. The click on a live card now leads to the game's page on the hub rather than into the game (`docs/analytics.md`)
- The picture of a card is `GameCardVisual` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/game-card`), which the page of a game draws too, larger (below). `gamePicture(game)` (`apps/web/src/lib/game-card-props.ts`) chooses its two pictures for the card and for the page alike, and `catalogCardProps(entry)` makes the props of the cards of the home and of the related cards of a game's page. Those related cards carry no `data-game` either: the listener counts `/` and `/games` only

## Search and filters (`GameSearch`, `HeroSearch`, `SearchCategory`, `SearchMoreFilters`, `HomeBody`, `GameList`)
- The home and `/games` search in client components of `apps/web/src/components/`, with one state between them. `GameSearch` is the provider that holds the search, the picks and the categories the games have. `HeroSearch` is the search bar of the header (`SearchBar` of `@pbgo/ui`, drawn in `docs/design/03-components/02-navigation.md`): one pill with the magnifier at its start, which sends the search and scrolls to `#results` like Enter, the field (with a clear button while there is a text) and, at its end, the category (`SearchCategory`), then, under it, centred on a line of their own and each in a container of its own, the other filters (`SearchMoreFilters`). The category is a segment that opens a `Popover` with a title, a Clear button and a list with a search field (`Combobox`: several at once, so the panel stays open; the segment is always drawn, and with no category yet its panel says so). The number of players is a stepper (`NumberStepper`: a minus button, the number with its icon just after it and a plus button, no panel and no label) that starts on 4 (`DEFAULT_PLAYERS`) and whose buttons move it one place along its options, the duration the hourglass, the limit written next to it (its minutes and the minute mark, "30′", or "1h+" for no limit), then a slider with one thumb on twelve steps (`DurationFilter`: from 5′ to 55′, five minutes at a time, and "1h+", the last being no limit; it starts on 20′, `DEFAULT_DURATION`, and the fill runs from the first step to the thumb; a dot marks each quarter hour), the complexity its icon and bars that fill up to the level pressed, with nothing written beside them (`ComplexityFilter`: a `Meter` that is not `clearable`: it starts on 3, `DEFAULT_COMPLEXITY`, and pressing the picked level keeps it, so there is always a level, from 1 to 5), the age a menu of "6+" to "18+" that starts on 8+ (`AgeFilter`, `DEFAULT_AGE`), as wide as its button. The number of players, the complexity and the age are never empty, and the duration has a step for no limit, "1h+", but never none, so the filter is always at work on all four, and there is no "any" for the players, the complexity or the age; nothing in the row is filled in or outlined to say what is picked: what the filters are set to is read from the control (the age, a menu, goes gray under the pointer, like the category). `CategoryFilter` and `MoreFilters` draw them from the picks they are given (the design docs draw them with picks of their own). `HomeBody` is what the home draws under its bar: the sections of the platform (its children, which stay server-rendered) until the visitor types or picks a filter (`isFiltered`), then the games that match. `GameList` is the list of games, the results of the home and all of `/games`: the cards of the games that match, or "No game matches" with a button that clears the search, and a count ("31 games", "5 of 31 games") that only screen readers get (`role="status"`). The rules are `@pbgo/catalog/filter` (`src/filter.ts`, pure and tested); the components only draw them. Their texts are English constants: the platform speaks English
- It all happens in the browser. The page stays static and hands `GameSearch` every game as `{ ...indexEntry(entry), props: catalogCardProps(entry) }`: what the filter reads of the game (`GameIndex`: slug, `live`, the normalised text to search, players, duration, minimum age, categories; no description, no picture) beside the props of its card. The HTML of the page lists every game whatever the link holds, so a crawler or a visitor without JavaScript gets the whole list. Nothing is asked of the server when a visitor types
- Filters only remove: the cards keep the order of the catalog (the live games, then the games to come), and there is no sort control
- The search: every game, a live one included, is searched by its name, the people credited on it (whatever they did: designers, artists, developers…) and its publishers, never by its descriptions. Case, accents and apostrophes do not count (`normalize`: "herve" finds "Hervé", "millers" finds "Miller's"; œ æ ß ø ł are spelled out). Every word of the query must be somewhere in the text of the game, a part of a word is enough, in any order. 60 characters at most (`MAX_QUERY_LENGTH`)
- The filters, all of which must hold: categories (any of the ones picked, spelled as the Studio has them: they are the names of the `category` documents a game points to, the panel offers the ones the games have, in alphabetical order, each with the icon that `apps/web/src/lib/category-icons.ts` gives its name (a placeholder: a `category` document has an icon, a color and a description, which the catalog does not read yet, and the name of a category will find them there), and a game with no category is left out as soon as one is picked; "Play now" and "Coming soon" are not categories and no longer a filter, the card says "Coming soon" itself); players (1 to 7, 8+, 4 where the bar starts: a game stays when its range holds the number, a game with no upper limit ("3+") seats any number from its lower limit up, and "8+" keeps the games that seat 8 or more); duration (up to 5, 10 … 55 minutes, five at a time, 20 where the bar starts, or no limit, the slider's twelfth step "1h+", which a link writes `duration=any`; a game counts by the one figure of its game in the Studio, a live game with none by the registry's when that is a single figure, not a range); complexity (a level from 1 to 5, 3 where the bar starts: a game stays when its level, the weight of its game rounded to the nearest whole number, is at most the one picked, so 2.49 is level 2 and 2.5 level 3; the weight is BoardGameGeek's, from the `complexity` field of the game); age (6, 8, 10, 12, 14 or 18, 8 where the bar starts: the age of the youngest player, so a game stays when its minimum age is at most that, and the menu reads "8+"). A game that does not say its players, its duration, its complexity or its minimum age is left out as soon as the filter asks for it, except that the four picks the bar starts on, 4 players, 20′, level 3 and 8+, leave out only the games that say they do not fit: a starting point is no demand, and the list would be empty wherever the games have no weight or no age yet. `isFiltered` is false where the bar starts: the home keeps its sections until the visitor types or changes a pick, and "Reset filters" puts the picks back there
- The address: the picks are written into it in place (`history.replaceState`: the list as the visitor has it is not a page to come back to), as `/?category=Cards&category=Party&players=6&duration=30&complexity=2&age=10`, a `category` for each one picked, each pick left out when it is where the bar starts (the number of players and the age, 4 and 8, and the others empty), so the list as it opens is `/` (or `/games`). A link that holds picks opens the list cut. A value the filters do not offer (`players=04`, `age=7`, `duration=60`, a category no game has) is ignored, and the pick goes back to where the bar starts, and so is the `?show=` of the availability filter that was. The typed text is never in the address, neither written nor read: the address goes to analytics with every event, and what a visitor types is theirs while analytics events carry no personal data (`docs/analytics.md`). A search that can be shared (`?q=`) would first need a rule in `@pbgo/analytics` that scrubs it
- The home and `/games` are static, so the server cannot read the query string. On the server and on the first render of the browser `GameSearch` sees none (`useSyncExternalStore`: the same markup, no hydration mismatch), then the real one. A link that holds picks therefore shows the sections of the home (the whole list on `/games`) for a moment before the results appear. A typed text is lost on reload
- Analytics: no event for the search or the filters. The count of `game_opened_from_hub` is unchanged: it listens on the document for `a[data-game]` at the moment of the click, so the cards that a filter brings back count as before, only the live ones carry `data-game`, and `usePathname` ignores the query string, so `/?category=Cards` is still the hub
- A new criterion = a field of `GameFilter` and of `GameIndex`, its rule in `filterGames`, its parameter in `filterFromParams` and `filterToParams` (left out when it is the default), a test in `filter.test.ts`, and its segment in `SearchCategory`, `SearchMoreFilters`
- Tests: `filter.test.ts` runs the model (`pnpm --filter @pbgo/catalog test`). `apps/web` has no test suite, so `GameSearch`, `HeroSearch`, `SearchCategory`, `SearchMoreFilters`, `HomeBody` and `GameList` are checked in the browser: typing, the panel of each filter (a pick, a second click on it, Clear, Escape, the keyboard), the empty state and its button, a shared link, a phone, a tablet and a desktop width

## The page of a game (`apps/web/src/app/(hub)/game/[slug]/page.tsx`)
- Every game of the catalog has one, live or to come, at `/game/<slug>`, in the hub layout and the platform look. `getGamePage(slug)` (`apps/web/src/lib/game-page.ts`) finds the entry in `getCatalog()` and builds what the page draws; a slug the catalog does not list answers 404 (`(hub)/not-found.tsx`). So a game of the Studio has a page only while the Studio's project answers: with it down, the catalog is the live games alone (each a bare `registryRow`) and every other slug is a 404
- It shows the name, a line of figures under it (the players and the duration, worded like the card, then the age: "Ages 8+"), the description, the credits, the rulebooks and a picture. Every one of those values is in the game's game of the Studio, a live game's as much as one to come's (the description, the age, the people credited, the publishers and the rulebooks). A field with no value is not drawn and no text is written in its place, so a live game with no game in the Studio shows its name and its figures alone; the registry's tagline is not shown. The picture, next to the text on a wide screen, is the one of the game's card drawn larger (`GameCardVisual`): the logo over the background on the grey frame, with no layer between the two, and a plain frame when the game has neither, no placeholder. Both pictures come from Sanity's image CDN. The description goes through `pickLocalized` (the visitor's language, else English, French, any other) and is marked with the `lang` it was found in
- Under the text, the credits follow one another, one line each: a line for each role of BoardGameGeek that somebody has on the game, in this order, Designer, Solo designer, Developer, Artist, Graphic designer, Editor, Sculptor, Writer, Insert designer (`CREATOR_ROLES`), then Publisher (`creditLines`; a label is in the plural when there are several, a line that nobody fills is not drawn, and a person who did several things is on each of their lines). A publisher that has a website is a link to it that opens in a new tab, with the website's icon at the left of its name, the two being one link (`CreditLink`, `@pbgo/ui/platform/credit-link`, drawn in `docs/design/03-components/04-surfaces.md`; the icon is the route below); a publisher with no website is a name. The rulebooks end the page, over the related games, each in its own section with a heading, in a grid that follows the width: one link per language the game has a rulebook in, the visitor's language first and the others in `LOCALES` order (`rulebooks` in `game-page.ts`). Each one is a bordered, button-like link (`Item`) that opens the PDF in a new tab: the language's code (EN, FR, ES, DE) on a grey chip (`Badge`, hidden from screen readers, the link carries the `lang`), the title in that language with the game's name ("Règles du jeu - Flip 7", "Rulebook - Flip 7"), then an arrow up-right. The words before the name (`RULEBOOK_TITLES`) are a platform text, in code: the hub reads no game's own Sanity project
- The games related to it end the page, under a "Related games" heading, as cards like the home's (`catalogCardProps`: a live card or a card to come, with no `data-game`) in a grid that follows the width; a game with none has no such section. They are the `related` of its catalog entry, built from the `related` and `baseGame` links of the games of the Studio, whichever game holds the link (above): the base game of a reimplementation and that reimplementation show on each other's page, and a live game shows the planned games related to it. Those links are the game's family, which comes first; `rankRelated` (`src/catalog.ts`) adds after it the games that look like it, at most `RELATED_MAX` (12) in all: the share of the categories that the two games have in common (the Jaccard index of their categories) plus, when it is known, the share of the game's players who also play the other (`alsoPlayed`, an option of `buildCatalog`: counts of distinct players per pair of games, never who; nothing feeds it yet, since no account has a history to count). So a game that has both signals comes before one that has only one, a game that has neither is not recommended, and a tie keeps the catalog's order, which puts the live games first
- The page, top to bottom: the head (the name, the figures, the description, the credits, the picture), the zone to create a game with the tables being created, the tournaments, the rulebooks, the related games. Where to buy the game, in a partner shop near the visitor or online, is an idea of Oré's and is not built: it needs a partnership and a place for the data, and until a shop exists the page says nothing about it.
- The zone to create a game (`GamePlay`, `apps/web/src/components/game-play.tsx`) is under the head, before everything else, and has no button of its own: a live game's zone is a plain `<a href="/<slug>">` to the game's own site (another root layout, loaded whole): the table is created there, not from the hub, which knows nothing of the game's options (they are in its own Sanity project). Next to it a card, "Tables being created", lists the tables that wait for players (`getOpenTables`, `apps/web/src/lib/game-activity.ts`): there is no list of open tables yet (PLT-21), so it says so. A game to come has the zone half transparent and `aria-disabled`, "Coming soon", and no card of tables.
- The tournaments of the game (`GameTournaments`, `getTournaments`) come under it: the ones to come, with the places taken and the places there are, the ones in progress and the finished ones, each group under its own heading and only the groups that have a tournament. There are none yet (PLT-31), so one empty state says what will be listed. The dates are written in UTC, the same on the server and in the browser. "Live" is the registry's flag (`CatalogEntry.live`, pure data), not `isPlayable(game)`, which needs the loaded binding and is also true outside production for every game with an engine: the hub says the same in production and in a preview
- Search engines: `robots.txt` has no rule for `/game/<slug>` (`/*/game/` needs a segment before `game`: it keeps crawlers off the tables), so the page says itself whether it may be indexed, in its `robots` meta, and carries its metadata, its JSON-LD and its line of the sitemap from `@pbgo/catalog/seo` (next section)
- `game` was already a reserved slug, being a segment of the table's address (`/<slug>/game/<code>`). `/game/<slug>` has two segments and a table three, and the static `game` wins over `[game]`, so the two never meet

## The SEO of the game pages (`packages/catalog/src/seo.ts`, `@pbgo/catalog/seo`)
- One rule, `isIndexable(entry)`, behind the three things below. A live game follows the registry's `indexable` flag, like its own home (`/<slug>`): `/game/courtisans` may be indexed, the page of a live game whose flag is off may not, whatever its game in the Studio says. A game to come may be indexed once its game in the Studio has a description (English, for now): until then the page has nothing to say and stays out of the results (`noindex`, no JSON-LD, not in the sitemap). A live game's page on the hub and its home are two pages of one game, both indexable when the flag is on, each with its own canonical
- Pure functions, tested without Next (`seo.test.ts`); `apps/web` hands them what the page loaded. `getGamePage` makes the page's `seo` (`GameSeo`, by `gameSeo(entry, description)`: the description is the one the page shows, from the game's game of the Studio, live or not; a game with none has none in its metadata and its JSON-LD, nothing stands in for it), `generateMetadata` is `gameMetadata(game.seo, { url: SITE_URL, name: SITE_NAME })`, the page writes its JSON-LD `<script>` only when `seo.indexable`, and `sitemap.ts` is `sitemapItems(SITE_URL, GAMES, entries)`
- The metadata: the title is the game's name (the layout's template adds the site's); the description is the text the page shows, on one line and cut at 160 characters between two words (`metaDescription`); `alternates.canonical` is the page's own absolute URL; `robots` is `index, follow` (with the `googleBot` large-image and snippet hints) or `noindex, follow`, a page that may not be indexed still letting its links be followed; Open Graph (`type: website`, `siteName`, `url`, `title` with the site's name, `description`, `locale` of the language the description was found in, the image) and a Twitter card (large with a wide picture, `summary` with a small one or none). Open Graph and Twitter are set on a `noindex` page too: a link to it still previews in a chat
- Next merges metadata key by key: a page's `openGraph` and `twitter` replace the layout's whole, so they repeat the type, the site name and the URL, and a key with nothing to say is left out, never set to `undefined` (Next reads a key that is present as an override, and `undefined` would wipe the layout's value: the tests walk the object for one)
- The picture a page shares, for every game: `sharePicture(game)`: its Background (wide) if the game has one, else its Logo or Icon (not wide, so the Twitter card is `summary`), else nothing. It is an absolute address in Sanity's image CDN already, which the page leaves as it is (`absoluteUrl` only completes a path). A live game's own share image, `/<slug>/opengraph-image/share`, stays the picture of its own home, not of its page on the hub. Before PLT-90 the share image was `/api/catalog/<slug>/background`, and Facebook, X and the like keep the links they have seen: `robots.txt` still opens `/api/catalog/` (`Allow` beats `Disallow: /api/`, the longest rule wins), and the route sends those links to the CDN
- The language: the description is the one the page shows (`pickLocalized`: the visitor's language, else English, French, any other). A crawler sends no language, so it reads the site's default, French, and falls back the same way; the `og:locale` is the language the text was found in, English while every description is. No `hreflang`: a page has one URL, not one per language
- The JSON-LD (`gameJsonLd`, written by `serializeJsonLd`, which escapes `<` so that a name can never close the tag): a schema.org `Game` with `name`, `url`, `description` (on one line, not cut), `image`, `numberOfPlayers`, `timeRequired` (ISO 8601, from the duration, one figure), `typicalAgeRange` (`8-`), `author` (the designers and the solo designers), `contributor` (everyone else credited on the game, whatever they did: a Game has no `illustrator`) and `publisher` (with its website as the `url`). A field with no value is absent, not empty. No `inLanguage`: the text is not always in the visitor's language
- The sitemap: the hub, the home of every indexable game of the registry, then the hub page of every game of the catalog for which `isIndexable` is true, so it lists exactly the pages that do not say `noindex`. A page is dated by its game's last edit in the Studio (`lastModified`, from `updatedAt`; none when the date does not read, so none for a live game that has no game in the Studio); a live game's page is `weekly` with priority 0.8, one to come `monthly` with 0.6. It is rebuilt every ten minutes (`revalidate = 600`, like the catalog), not once per build, so a game that gets a description is listed within twenty minutes at worst. When the catalog is the live games alone (the Studio's project unavailable) the sitemap lists those alone until the next rebuild
- To look at it: `pnpm dev`, then view-source on `/game/<slug>` of a game with a description and of one without, `/sitemap.xml` and `/robots.txt`

## Cache and failure
- `getCatalog` is wrapped in React's `cache` (one read per request); the Sanity call itself is cached by Next for ten minutes (`next: { revalidate: 600, tags: ["catalog"] }` on the `fetch`; the stale answer is served while a fresh one is fetched). `revalidateTag(CATALOG_TAG, { expire: 0 })` makes the next read ask Sanity again; no webhook of the project calls it yet, so a published change shows on the hub within about eleven minutes (Sanity's CDN keeps an answer for a minute, then Next's copy for ten). Next does not cache an answer over 2 MB: the catalog is about 65 KB for 71 games
- The Studio's project is the one optional part. When it does not answer (an outage, a timeout of 5 s, a bad query), the catalog is the live games alone, each one a `registryRow` (its name, its players and its duration, no picture, no text), `source` is `"unavailable"`, and a Studio-only slug has no page (`/game/<slug>` answers 404). In development (`NODE_ENV=development`) the catalog also lists the other games of the registry, each a `registryRow` that says "Coming soon" (`buildCatalog`'s `registryGames` option, after the live games and in registry order), so that a hub that cannot read the project can be seen with all its grid, names and figures but no picture; a deployment never does, since a game that the Studio does not announce is not announced
- What is wrong is logged once per distinct line and per server instance, as `[catalog] …` on `console.warn`: the project unavailable, no published game, a game left out and why. Nothing logged holds an address of a request

## The pictures, and the route of the old addresses (`GET /api/catalog/<slug>/<logo|icon|background>`)
- A game's pictures are files of its game in the platform's Sanity project (`logo`, `icon`, `background`). The reader hands out the address of each file in the project's image CDN (`https://cdn.sanity.io/images/mhgqs6fe/production/…`: only that CDN, a picture anywhere else is dropped), and a page draws `pictureUrl(game, slot)`: that address with `?w=<width>&fit=max&auto=format`, 800 px for a logo, 512 for an icon and 1200 for a background (never more than the file holds), `auto=format` giving WebP or AVIF to a browser that takes it, and an SVG as it is. The visitor's browser loads the picture from `cdn.sanity.io` itself: nothing runs on this site's servers for it, and Sanity's CDN keeps it. `img-src` is not restricted by this site; if the pictures ever have to go through this site's own domain, `/_next/image` already accepts `cdn.sanity.io` (`images.remotePatterns` in `next.config.ts`)
- No picture is no error page: a game that does not have one leaves a plain grey frame (`bg-muted`, a platform token: no initials, no placeholder art). The cards of the hub and the pages of the games (`/game/<slug>`) draw it (above)
- The route is what is left of the one that served the pictures of the Notion rows (Notion's links expired after about an hour, so the server downloaded each picture from a fresh link). Links to it were shared and cached before PLT-90, a page's share image among them, and a consumer that knows a game's slug only (the icon of a game in the friends' panel, in the list of a visitor's games) can still ask for `/api/catalog/<slug>/icon`. Its order, so that nothing costs more than it has to: 1) the slot is one of the three, or `publisher-<n>` (next section), else 404; 2) the catalog could not be read: 503, the game may well exist; 3) the slug is a game of the catalog, live or not, else 404; 4) a picture the game has is a 308 to its address in the CDN (`?v=` of an old link is ignored), a picture it does not have a 404

| Answer | `Cache-Control` |
| --- | --- |
| 308 to the picture in the image CDN | `public, max-age=300, s-maxage=3600` |
| 404 (not a game of the catalog, or a picture the game does not have) | `public, max-age=60, s-maxage=60` |
| 503, with `Retry-After` (the catalog cannot be read now) | `no-store` |

- What the server fetches (`picture.ts`, the one place that does it, for the icon of a publisher's website and for the seed): the visitor gives a slug and a slot, never a URL. A link must be https, with no credentials and the default port; an IP address, a name without a dot, `localhost` and the names a network keeps for itself (`.localhost`, `.local`, `.internal`, `.localdomain`, `.home.arpa`) are refused. A redirect is followed by hand, three at most, each one checked again. One 8 s limit covers the download, the size is capped at 4 MiB while the body streams (a function answers 4.5 MB at most), and the file must be a picture by its bytes (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, AVIF, SVG, and ICO, which only an icon is), never by what the host said. The logs name the host, never a link
- Limits, on purpose: the server does not resolve names, so a public name that points at a private address (`127.0.0.1.nip.io`) passes: the websites come from the Studio, trusted as far as whoever can edit it

## The icon of a publisher (`GET /api/catalog/<slug>/publisher-<n>`)
- Why it exists: a game's page shows each publisher with the icon of its website at the left of its name (`CreditLink`). The visitor's browser must not ask the publisher's website for it (this site keeps its visitors' data on its own domain, as the media proxy and the `/ingest` rewrites do), so the server looks the icon up and the CDN keeps it. The page draws it from `publisherIconUrl(game, index)`: `/api/catalog/<slug>/publisher-<index>?v=<iconVersion(website)>`, `index` being the place of the publisher in the game's list, from 0, as `creditLines` lists them, and `v` a short hash of the website's address, so that a publisher whose website changes gets a new address for its icon
- It is the picture route (`pictureRoute()`) with another kind of slot. `publisher-<n>` has no leading zero and at most two digits, so one icon has one address. The first steps are the picture's (the slug is a game of the catalog), then the game must have an n-th publisher with a website (the `website` of its `publisher` document), else 404, and `v` must be the version of that website, else a 307 to the right URL, before anything is fetched, so nobody can make the server look for an icon under a `v` of their own. Sanity is not read again: the website is the catalog's. The visitor gives a slug and a place and never an address: a `url=`, `site=` or `host=` in the query is not read, and a test sends them to prove it
- What the server does (`favicon.ts`, `fetchFavicon(site)`): it reads the page of the website as far as its first 256 KiB for the links `rel="icon"`, `shortcut icon` and `apple-touch-icon` (`iconLinks`; `data:` icons, comments and anything `allowedUrl` refuses are left out), ranks them (`rank`, the lowest first: 32 to 128 px, the nearest to 64 first; then a scalable one, an SVG or `sizes="any"`; then an apple-touch-icon; then a bigger one; then one that says no size, usually an ICO; the smallest, under 32 px, last), tries four at most (the best three, then `/favicon.ico`, once), and serves the first that is a picture by its bytes (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, AVIF, SVG or ICO) and at most 512 KiB. A page that cannot be had (down, 4xx, a bad certificate) still leaves `/favicon.ico`. An `http` address in the Studio is asked for over https. One 8 s limit covers the page and the icons together. It sends `Accept` and a `User-Agent` that names the site (`PlayBoardGamesOnline-icons/1.0 (+https://playboardgamesonline.app)`) and nothing else
- What it may fetch is what a picture may (`picture.ts`, `allowedUrl`): https only, no credentials, no port, no address, no `localhost`, no name of a local network, for the website's own address and for every link its page gives and every redirect it makes (three at most, by hand, each checked again)
- The answers: 200, `public, max-age=86400, s-maxage=86400` (a day: a favicon changes once in years); 404, `public, max-age=3600, s-maxage=3600` when the website has no icon the server can use, `public, max-age=60, s-maxage=60` when the game has no such publisher or no website; 307 to the current `v` (`public, max-age=0, s-maxage=10`); 503, `no-store`, when the website cannot be reached now (`Retry-After` 300: it is then left alone for five minutes, per server instance, so that a website that is down or has a broken certificate is not asked again by every visit to its game's page, and nobody waits for its 8 s) or the catalog cannot be read. Every 200 has the headers of a picture (`X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` and `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; style-src 'unsafe-inline'; sandbox`, like the media route) and its type comes from its bytes. A website that fails is logged once per instance, as `The icon of the publisher <n> of "<slug>" cannot be served: <host> …`, the host and never an address
- The page: `CreditLink` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/credit-link`, `docs/design/03-components/04-surfaces.md`, Credit link) keeps the 16 px place of the icon from the first paint and asks for the picture once the page is interactive, so that a 404 or a 503 is always heard and the place is then given up: a publisher with no icon is its name alone, with no broken picture and nothing that moves but that name, once. The icon is decorative (`alt=""`): the link says the publisher's name. `robots.txt` already opens `/api/catalog/`: a crawler that renders the page may fetch an icon, which is a small cached image
- Limits, on purpose: names are not resolved (as for a picture); only the first 256 KiB of a page are read, so an icon named after that is not seen; the icon is the website's favicon, as asked, and the `logo` of a `publisher` document, which the Studio has, is not read (it could stand in where a website has no icon); the icon is only as good as the `website` of the publisher (on 2026-10-30, of the 18 websites of the publishers, 16 give an icon; `https://www.catchupgames.com` has an invalid certificate for `www`, while `https://catchupgames.com` works, so its `publisher` should say that, and the link a visitor follows would be refused by their browser otherwise; the Magilano website has no icon at all, so its name stands alone); a publisher's new favicon shows within a day

## `pnpm catalog:list`
What the hub would list right now: one line per game (slug, name, kind — `live`, `live (registry)` for a live game that the Studio has no game for (listed with what the registry knows of it), or `planned` —, its `order`, which of Logo / Icon / Background and which description languages are filled, players, duration, weight (the `complexity`), age, categories, the creators with what each did on the game, publishers, the games related to it) and, under it, the games of the Studio that are not in the catalog and why (a name or a slug the site cannot use, a slug already taken), how many expansions were left out, and the warnings. ## Working on the package
- `pnpm --filter @pbgo/catalog test` and `pnpm --filter @pbgo/catalog typecheck`. The tests run the reader against a fake Sanity built from the shape the project answers (`src/testing.ts`: `sanityGame(slug, overrides)` is one document as the query returns it, `fakeSanity(answer)` the fetch that answers with a list or with a failure, `sanityFailure`); a new field = its line in `CATALOG_QUERY`, the field of `CatalogGame`, its line in `readSanityGames`, `registryRow`, `catalogGame` and `sanityGame` of `testing.ts`, a test. The tests do not run the query itself (the Studio's own queries are run by a GROQ engine, `platform/apps/studio/schemaTypes/views/games-of.test.ts`): a change to `CATALOG_QUERY` is proved against the real project with `pnpm catalog:list`
- The picture route is tested against a fake Sanity and fake picture hosts and websites of publishers (`fakeWeb`), never the network: the answers, the headers, the memo and the refused links. A change to `route.ts` or `picture.ts` is checked by breaking it on purpose and seeing a test fail
- The SEO is tested without Next or the network (`seo.test.ts`, on the entries that `catalogEntry` of `testing.ts` builds from `registryGame` and `catalogGame`): the rule, the cut of the description, the metadata (and that no key of it is `undefined`), the JSON-LD and the lines of the sitemap
- The source is run as it is by Node (`pnpm catalog:list`, type stripping) and bundled by Next (`transpilePackages`): in `src/`, a value import from a sibling file has no extension that both accept, so a file imports only types from its siblings (`import type`) and the pieces that need each other meet in `server.ts`, `route.ts` and `scripts/list.ts`; `scripts/` import with `.ts` extensions. `seo.ts` is the exception, since Node never loads it (Next bundles it and Vitest runs it): it imports values from `./catalog` and from core (`lib/i18n`, `lib/site-url`). `picture.ts` imports nothing at all, and `favicon.ts` is another exception like `seo.ts`: it imports values from `./picture`, and Node never loads it (`route.ts` does, in Next and in Vitest). `server-only` throws under plain Node, so only `src/server.ts` imports it (the tests of `server.ts` and `route.ts` mock it). `list-script.test.ts` runs the script under plain Node to keep this true
