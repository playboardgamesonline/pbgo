# Catalog

The list of games the hub shows: the live games of the registry, then the games to come of the Notion Games database, joined by `@pbgo/catalog`. Every game on the hub, a live one as much as one to come, shows the values of its Notion row; the registry only says which of them can be played. Part of PLT-56 ("the hub catalog shows every game"); PLT-83 made the Notion row the hub's source for every game.

## What exists
- Step 1 (PLT-71): the reader and the join, in this package. Step 2 (PLT-72): the route that serves the pictures of the games, `/api/catalog/<slug>/<logo|icon|background>` (below), and the reader brought up to the Notion database as it is since 2026-10-24 (`Authors`, `Illustrators` and `Editors` relations, the Creators database, no more `Status`, `Priority` or `Effort` on the Games rows), with the coming-soon games ordered by the Games Development database (below), then by name. Steps 3 and 4 (PLT-73, PLT-74): the hub reads the catalog and lists every game in one list, and each card opens the game's own page on the hub, `/game/<slug>`, whether the game is live or to come, and the page ends with the games related to it (below). `[game]`, the game's own site, still serves the registry's slugs only
- Step 5 (PLT-75): the SEO of the game pages, `@pbgo/catalog/seo`: which of them search engines may index, their metadata and share image, their JSON-LD and the sitemap (below)
- PLT-83 (2026-10-27): the hub shows the Notion values for every game, Courtisans included, and a game's Sanity project serves only once one enters the game (`/<slug>`, its table, its admin). The hub no longer reads any Sanity project. The registry's `live` stays the one switch between a game to play and a greyed "Coming soon" (the cards and the page read `entry.live` for it, and `entry.indexable` for search engines); the ruling holds "for now", so the switch is kept small on purpose
- PLT-86 (2026-10-28): the filters of the home move into its search bar: the categories at its end, in a panel with a list and a search field, and under it, bare (no container is drawn), the players as a stepper that starts on 4 (a minus button, the number, a plus button), the duration as a slider with one thumb that starts on 20′ (up to 5, 10 … 55 minutes, five at a time, or 1h+, which is no limit), the complexity as bars that fill and start on 3 (never below 1), and the age as a menu that starts on 8+. The categories are the Notion `Tags` column, the minimum age and the complexity (the weight BoardGameGeek gives the game, a `Complexity` number column) are new; the availability filter and `?show=` are gone (below). This file describes what has landed; each step updates it

## Where a game comes from
- Every value the hub shows of a game comes from its row in the Notion Games database, a live game's as much as one to come's: the name, the pictures (logo, icon, background), the descriptions in four languages, the players, the duration, the age, the authors, the illustrators, the publishers and the rulebooks. Courtisans' card and page are its Notion row: where Notion and the registry disagree on a figure (the players, the duration) the hub shows Notion's
- The hub reads nothing from a game's Sanity project: that project serves once one enters the game (`/<slug>`, its table and its pages, another root layout)
- The registry decides what Notion does not: `live` (a game to play or a greyed "Coming soon"), `indexable`, the slug, and the order of the live games (registry order, before all the others). A **live** game (`live: true` in `packages/games/src/registry.ts`) takes its Notion row; with no row, or with Notion off or down, it takes `registryRow(game)`: its name, its players and its duration when the registry has one figure, nothing else (a plain grey frame, a bare page), still listed and still enterable. There is no value-by-value fallback: a field the row leaves empty stays empty, the registry never fills it
- A **planned** game: its row in the Notion Games database, whatever its status. A game of the registry that is not live takes its row too, and Notion's name with it; with no row it is not listed
- `template` is never listed (`UNLISTED_SLUGS`), and neither are the expansions: a row whose `Parent item` is filled is counted and left out, unless its `Type` is `Standalone expansion` (today 20 of the 51 rows are left out; the 2 standalone ones, Flip 7 Vengeance and 6 nimmt! 25 Jahre, are listed)
- No sync to Sanity: Notion is read, never copied. The registry stays the source of the slug, `live`, `indexable` and the Sanity project

## The package (`packages/catalog`, `@pbgo/catalog`)
- `.` (`src/catalog.ts`, pure: no I/O, no framework) — the types (`NotionGame`, `DevelopmentRank`, `CatalogEntry` = `{ slug, live, indexable, game, related }` where `game` is the Notion row, `Catalog`, `Publisher`), `buildCatalog(games, rows)`, `registryRow(game)` (the row of a live game that has none), `comparePlanned`, `UNLISTED_SLUGS`, `pickLocalized(values, locale)`, `ART_SLOTS`, `pictureUrl(game, slot)`, `cardPictureUrl(game)` (the logo of a card, else its icon), `sharePicture(game)` (the picture a page shares, below), `pictureVersion(editedAt)`
- `./server` (`src/server.ts`, server only) — `getCatalog()`, `CATALOG_TAG`, `CATALOG_REVALIDATE_SECONDS`, `notionToken()`, `reportOnce(message)`. `getCatalog` is the one call a page makes
- `./filter` (`src/filter.ts`, pure like `.`, and the one that the browser imports) — the search and the filters of the hub's list: `GameFilter`, `NO_FILTER` (no pick at all), `DEFAULT_FILTER` (where the bar starts: 4 players and 8+), `GameIndex`, `indexEntry(entry)`, `filterGames(games, filter)`, `categoriesOf(games)`, `isFiltered`, `optionOf`, `stepOption` / `canStep` (what the stepper of the bar does), `complexityLevel` (the level a weight rounds to), `filterFromParams(params, offered)` / `filterToParams`, `normalize`, `words`, and the options it offers (`PLAYER_COUNTS`, `MANY_PLAYERS`, `DURATION_LIMITS`, `COMPLEXITY_LEVELS`, `AGE_LIMITS`, `DEFAULT_PLAYERS`, `DEFAULT_AGE`, `MAX_QUERY_LENGTH`). See "Search and filters", below
- `./route` (`src/route.ts`, server only) — `pictureRoute()`, the handler of the picture route; `apps/web/src/app/api/catalog/[slug]/[slot]/route.ts` is `export const GET = pictureRoute()`
- `./seo` (`src/seo.ts`, pure apart from Next's `Metadata` types) — `isIndexable(entry)`, `metaDescription(text)`, `gameSeo(entry, description)` (the `GameSeo` of a page), `gameMetadata`, `gameJsonLd`, `serializeJsonLd`, `sitemapItems` (below)
- `src/notion.ts` — the reader, `readNotionGames({ token, fetch?, init?, timeoutMs? })`: a plain `fetch` against Notion's REST API (`Notion-Version: 2022-06-28`), no SDK, no framework. It reads the Games database and, alongside it, the Editors and Creators databases that the relations of a game point to, and the Games Development database that ranks the planned games. The caller hands in the token and the cache options. `readPictureLinks(options)` reads the Games database alone for the links of the pictures. A failure is a `NotionError` (`status`, `code`, and for a 429 the `retryAfter` it asks, in seconds)
- `src/picture.ts` — `fetchPicture(link)`: downloads a picture from a link and checks it (below)
- `scripts/list.ts` — `pnpm catalog:list`; `src/testing.ts` — the fixtures of the tests (a row as Notion writes it, a fake Notion, fake picture hosts), not exported
- Order of the list: the live games (registry order), then the planned ones by their rank in the Games Development database, then by name (`comparePlanned`, below). A live game is never ranked: it is the registry's, wherever its Notion row and its rank say it would be. `Hotness` is not read: nothing says what it would mean for the hub
- `pickLocalized` gives a text for a visitor: their language, else English, else French, else any other; it says which one it took, so a page can mark a text that is not in the visitor's language

## The Notion databases (`notion.ts` holds the ids and the column names)
- **Games** `3ffd1ca3-468f-80e8-a371-ed666deecc9a`, **Editors** `3ffd1ca3-468f-80e0-ba62-fb466ca8a6c8` (publishers: `Name`, `URL`) and **Creators** `5712daa0-497d-4fa7-9634-1c2320f4446d` (the people: `Name`; its `Role` is not read, the column of the game says whether a person is an author or an illustrator)
- **Games Development** `401d1ca3-468f-8159-a5c2-fbf706218640`: the tasks database, one row per game (its title is the game's slug). `Game` is the relation to the Games row; `Priority`, `Status` and `Effort` rank the game (below)
- The integration must be connected to all four databases (Connections of each one): without Editors no game has a publisher, without Creators no game has an author or an illustrator, without Games Development the coming-soon games are listed by name (the Games database alone still works, with a warning for each)
- `Slug` (text) is the slug of the page, written by hand: a row with none is skipped, never guessed. A slug must pass `slugProblem` (`@pbgo/games/slug`: not a reserved slug, lower-case, 40 characters at most); two rows with one slug: the older row wins, the other is reported
- Shown: `Name`, `Description FR|EN|ES|DE`, `Players` (`4`, `2 - 5`, `3+`), `Duration` (minutes), `Complexity` (a number from 1 to 5: the game's weight on BoardGameGeek, written as BGG writes it, `2.35`; a value outside that range, 0 included, which is BGG's own "not enough votes", is read as none), `Age` (`8+`), `Tags` (a multi-select: the categories of the game, read for the hub's category filter and drawn nowhere), `Authors` and `Illustrators` (relations to the Creators database), `Editors` (relation to the Editors database), `Rules FR|EN|ES|DE` (links, http(s) only), `Logo`, `Icon`, `Background`. A relation is read in the order Notion keeps it; a row it points to that has no name, or that the integration cannot see, is dropped without a word. A query hands back 25 related rows of a column at most, far more than a game has
- `Parent item` and `Type` tell an expansion from a game. A row whose `Parent item` is filled is an expansion of that game: counted and left out, never reported. Unless its `Type` (a select: `Base game`, `Expansion`, `Standalone expansion`) is `Standalone expansion`: it plays without its base game, so it is a game of its own, read and listed like any other (with no slug it is reported as skipped, not counted as an expansion). A row with no `Parent item` is a game whatever its `Type`. Without a `Type` column the reader says so, like for any column, and every row with a parent is an expansion again
- `Related Games` (a relation to the Games database itself, one way) and `Parent item` are the links between games, and `related` of a catalog entry is built from both: the slugs its own row points to in either column, plus those of the games whose rows point to it, so a link written on one row is enough. A link to an expansion, to a row with no slug or to a game the catalog does not list (`template`) is left out, a game is never related to itself, and the list follows the catalog's order. A live game's Notion row gives its links like any other's. So the base game of a standalone expansion is one of its related games, and the expansion one of the base game's. `Expansions`, the same link seen from the base game, and `Relation kind` (`Spin-off`, `Re-skin`) are not read
- Every other column is ignored: `Hotness`, `Development` (the relation to the database below: the join is read from the other side, the `Game` column of each Development row), `Game family` (a franchise, not a category), `Release date`, the BGG columns, the fonts, `Expansions`, `Relation kind`, `Other contributors` and the dates. The Games database has no `Status`, `Priority` or `Effort` any more (they live in Games Development now), which the first reader used to order the list
- The pictures are only flagged (`art`): a file that Notion hosts has a link that expires after about an hour, so a link is never kept in the cache. `notionPageId` finds the row again and `editedAt` changes when the row does; the picture route serves the picture from a fresh link. A link to somewhere else (`external`) counts only if it is https: a picture with any other link is not flagged
- A row of the Games database that is archived or in the trash is ignored. Text is trimmed and control characters are removed from what a row says about itself, the names of people and publishers included (it goes to a terminal and to the logs)
- A column that is missing from the database (renamed, deleted) is not an error: the field is empty for every game and the reader says so (`The Games database has no "<column>" column`). A database without `Name` or `Slug` is not the Games database: the read fails. So does a database of more than 1 000 rows (ten requests of 100, twenty times today's), which is a runaway cursor more than a catalog

## The order of the coming-soon games (the Games Development database)
- The sort is the one of that database's own "Table" view, copied into `comparePlanned`: Priority ascending (`P1 · Now` first), then Status descending (the last status of the column first, today `Live`, `Paused`, `Playtest`, `Table`, `Content`, `Engine`, `Rules`, `Not started`), then Effort ascending (`S` first), then the name (case-insensitive, accents aside, numbers in order, so `6 nimmt!`, `7 Wonders`, `10 Minutes`, `Bang`). A game with no value in a column comes after those that have one, in either direction. It is a copy of the sort, not a link to it: changing which columns the view sorts on, or the direction of one, changes nothing here until `comparePlanned` changes with it; moving an option inside a column does (next point)
- A game is ranked by the place of its option in the column's own list of options (the order the options have in Notion, not their alphabet), which is how Notion sorts a select or a status. The reader therefore asks for the schema of the database as well as its rows, and renaming, adding or moving an option in Notion moves the games. A value the column does not have any more counts as empty
- The join: the `Game` relation of a row names the row of the Games database it ranks. A row with no `Game`, or whose game is not in the catalog, ranks nothing; a row that ranks several games ranks each of them; a game with two rows takes the older one (the way a slug does); an archived or trashed row is ignored
- `NotionGame.development` is `{ priority, status, effort }`, each one `{ position, name }` or `null`, so that `pnpm catalog:list` shows what ordered the list. It is part of the cached catalog: a change in Notion shows after ten minutes like any other
- It never fails the catalog. When the database or its schema cannot be read (the integration is not connected to it: Notion answers a 404 `object_not_found`), no game is ranked, the coming-soon games are listed by name and the reader says so once. A column that is missing or of another type is left out of the order, with a warning of its own

## The hub (`apps/web/src/app/(hub)/page.tsx`, and `games/page.tsx` for `/games`)
- Two pages read `getCatalog()` and give every entry, in the order the catalog gives (the live games, then the games to come), to `GameSearch`, which holds the search and the filters (below): the home (`(hub)/page.tsx`) and the list of games (`(hub)/games/page.tsx`, `/games`: what the home used to be, a title, the search bar and `GameList`, which draws the games that match in one grid; indexable, in the sitemap, `canonical` `/games`). The search bar of the header (`HeroSearch`) types and picks into it; its field says "Search games, tables & tournaments...", but only the games can be searched today. The home lays the glass bar over the foot of its title and draws the sections of the platform under it until a search is started (`HomeBody`). None has a visible title (what it shows says what it is; each has an `sr-only` heading). *Popular games* (`PopularGames`) is the grid of `/games` cut to two rows at every width, whose bottom fades out, with "See all games" over the fade, centred, to `/games` (`SectionPreview`, `SectionAction`); there is no count of plays to rank them by yet, one game being live and the rest to come, so they are the first games of the catalog in its order, and `PopularGames` is the one place that ranks the day there is a count. Then *Tables in progress*, *Tournaments in progress* and *What's new* (`EmptySection`: an empty state each and its button, "See all tables", "See all tournaments", "See what's new", to the page of its tab, until the list of open tables PLT-21, the tournaments PLT-31 and the release notes PLT-20 exist; the rows of tables will take the place of the empty state, with the same fade and button as the games; their words are `COMING_SOON`, `apps/web/src/lib/coming-soon.ts`, shared with the pages of the tabs). The page never sorts or splits the games again, and status and priority only sort, no card shows them. With no Notion row (no key, Notion unavailable) the list is the live games alone, each one a `registryRow`: its name and its facts under a plain frame.
- Every card is the same tile, `GameCard` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/game-card`, drawn on `/design`): the game's logo, contained and not cropped, over its background picture on a grey 4:3 frame (`bg-muted`, a platform token), with no layer over the art, then its name, on one line and cut with an ellipsis when it does not fit, and two facts, its players and its duration (`UserMultipleIcon` and `HourglassIcon`), and nothing else: no tagline, no placeholder. A picture the game does not have leaves the frame plain; with neither, the frame is a plain grey and the name sits under it
- A live game is `variant="play"`, a game to come `variant="soon"` with a small "Coming soon" badge at the top right of the card. `catalogCardProps(entry)` (`apps/web/src/lib/game-card-props.ts`) is the one place that makes a card's props, and the only thing it reads of the registry is `entry.live`, for that variant. Every card takes the same values from the game's Notion row: its logo is `cardPictureUrl(game)` (the logo, else the icon), its background `pictureUrl(game, "background")`, both from the picture route, and its facts are `playersLabel` and `durationLabel`. So Courtisans' card shows its Notion pictures, not the ones of its Sanity project: a change in the Studio no longer reaches the hub, an edit in Notion does, once the catalog has read the row again (below)
- Every card is a plain `<a>` to the game's page on the hub, `/game/<slug>` (below), the games to come as much as the live ones. `GameCard` requires an `href`, so a card that goes nowhere cannot be drawn; `catalogCardProps` holds that one rule
- Only the card of a live game carries `data-game`: analytics counts the games opened from the hub (`game_opened_from_hub`), and a game to come is not one. The click on a live card now leads to the game's page on the hub rather than into the game (`docs/analytics.md`)
- The picture of a card is `GameCardVisual` (`@pbgo/ui/platform/game-card`), which the page of a game draws too, larger (below). `gamePicture(game)` (`apps/web/src/lib/game-card-props.ts`) chooses its two pictures for the card and for the page alike, and `catalogCardProps(entry)` makes the props of the cards of the home and of the related cards of a game's page. Those related cards carry no `data-game` either: the listener counts `/` and `/games` only

## Search and filters (`GameSearch`, `HeroSearch`, `SearchCategory`, `SearchMoreFilters`, `HomeBody`, `GameList`)
- The home and `/games` search in client components of `apps/web/src/components/`, with one state between them. `GameSearch` is the provider that holds the search, the picks and the categories the games have. `HeroSearch` is the search bar of the header (`SearchBar` of `@pbgo/ui`, drawn on `/design`): one pill with the magnifier at its start, which sends the search and scrolls to `#results` like Enter, the field (with a clear button while there is a text) and, at its end, the category (`SearchCategory`), then, under it, centred on a line of their own and each in a container of its own, the other filters (`SearchMoreFilters`). The category is a segment that opens a `Popover` with a title, a Clear button and a list with a search field (`Combobox`: several at once, so the panel stays open; the segment is always drawn, and with no category yet its panel says so). The number of players is a stepper (`NumberStepper`: a minus button, the number with its icon just after it and a plus button, no panel and no label) that starts on 4 (`DEFAULT_PLAYERS`) and whose buttons move it one place along its options, the duration the hourglass, the limit written next to it (its minutes and the minute mark, "30′", or "1h+" for no limit), then a slider with one thumb on twelve steps (`DurationFilter`: from 5′ to 55′, five minutes at a time, and "1h+", the last being no limit; it starts on 20′, `DEFAULT_DURATION`, and the fill runs from the first step to the thumb; a dot marks each quarter hour), the complexity its icon and bars that fill up to the level pressed, with nothing written beside them (`ComplexityFilter`: a `Meter` that is not `clearable`: it starts on 3, `DEFAULT_COMPLEXITY`, and pressing the picked level keeps it, so there is always a level, from 1 to 5), the age a menu of "6+" to "18+" that starts on 8+ (`AgeFilter`, `DEFAULT_AGE`), as wide as its button. The number of players, the complexity and the age are never empty, and the duration has a step for no limit, "1h+", but never none, so the filter is always at work on all four, and there is no "any" for the players, the complexity or the age; nothing in the row is filled in or outlined to say what is picked: what the filters are set to is read from the control (the age, a menu, goes gray under the pointer, like the category). `CategoryFilter` and `MoreFilters` draw them from the picks they are given (`/design` draws them with picks of its own). `HomeBody` is what the home draws under its bar: the sections of the platform (its children, which stay server-rendered) until the visitor types or picks a filter (`isFiltered`), then the games that match. `GameList` is the list of games, the results of the home and all of `/games`: the cards of the games that match, or "No game matches" with a button that clears the search, and a count ("31 games", "5 of 31 games") that only screen readers get (`role="status"`). The rules are `@pbgo/catalog/filter` (`src/filter.ts`, pure and tested); the components only draw them. Their texts are English constants: the platform speaks English
- It all happens in the browser. The page stays static and hands `GameSearch` every game as `{ ...indexEntry(entry), props: catalogCardProps(entry) }`: what the filter reads of the game (`GameIndex`: slug, `live`, the normalised text to search, players, duration, minimum age, categories; no description, no picture) beside the props of its card. The HTML of the page lists every game whatever the link holds, so a crawler or a visitor without JavaScript gets the whole list. Nothing is asked of the server when a visitor types
- Filters only remove: the cards keep the order of the catalog (the live games, then the games to come), and there is no sort control
- The search: every game, a live one included, is searched by its name, its authors, its illustrators and its publishers (the ones of its Notion row), never by its descriptions. Case, accents and apostrophes do not count (`normalize`: "herve" finds "Hervé", "millers" finds "Miller's"; œ æ ß ø ł are spelled out). Every word of the query must be somewhere in the text of the game, a part of a word is enough, in any order. 60 characters at most (`MAX_QUERY_LENGTH`)
- The filters, all of which must hold: categories (any of the ones picked, spelled as Notion has them: they are the options of the `Tags` column, the panel offers the ones the games have, in alphabetical order, each with the icon that `apps/web/src/lib/category-icons.ts` gives its name (a placeholder: Notion is to have a table of the categories, with an icon, a colour and a description for each, and the name of a category will find them there), and a game with no category is left out as soon as one is picked; "Play now" and "Coming soon" are not categories and no longer a filter, the card says "Coming soon" itself); players (1 to 7, 8+, 4 where the bar starts: a game stays when its range holds the number, a game with no upper limit ("3+") seats any number from its lower limit up, and "8+" keeps the games that seat 8 or more); duration (up to 5, 10 … 55 minutes, five at a time, 20 where the bar starts, or no limit, the slider's twelfth step "1h+", which a link writes `duration=any`; a game counts by the one figure of its Notion row, a live game with no row by the registry's when that is a single figure, not a range); complexity (a level from 1 to 5, 3 where the bar starts: a game stays when its level, the weight of its row rounded to the nearest whole number, is at most the one picked, so 2.49 is level 2 and 2.5 level 3; the weight is BoardGameGeek's, from the `Complexity` column of the row); age (6, 8, 10, 12, 14 or 18, 8 where the bar starts: the age of the youngest player, so a game stays when its minimum age is at most that, and the menu reads "8+"). A game that does not say its players, its duration, its complexity or its minimum age is left out as soon as the filter asks for it, except that the four picks the bar starts on, 4 players, 20′, level 3 and 8+, leave out only the games that say they do not fit: a starting point is no demand, and the list would be empty wherever the rows have no weight or no age yet. `isFiltered` is false where the bar starts: the home keeps its sections until the visitor types or changes a pick, and "Reset filters" puts the picks back there
- The address: the picks are written into it in place (`history.replaceState`: the list as the visitor has it is not a page to come back to), as `/?category=Cards&category=Party&players=6&duration=30&complexity=2&age=10`, a `category` for each one picked, each pick left out when it is where the bar starts (the number of players and the age, 4 and 8, and the others empty), so the list as it opens is `/` (or `/games`). A link that holds picks opens the list cut. A value the filters do not offer (`players=04`, `age=7`, `duration=60`, a category no game has) is ignored, and the pick goes back to where the bar starts, and so is the `?show=` of the availability filter that was. The typed text is never in the address, neither written nor read: the address goes to analytics with every event, and what a visitor types is theirs while analytics events carry no personal data (`docs/analytics.md`). A search that can be shared (`?q=`) would first need a rule in `@pbgo/analytics` that scrubs it
- The home and `/games` are static, so the server cannot read the query string. On the server and on the first render of the browser `GameSearch` sees none (`useSyncExternalStore`: the same markup, no hydration mismatch), then the real one. A link that holds picks therefore shows the sections of the home (the whole list on `/games`) for a moment before the results appear, and the browser asks for the pictures of every card first (the picture route is cached). A typed text is lost on reload
- Analytics: no event for the search or the filters. The count of `game_opened_from_hub` is unchanged: it listens on the document for `a[data-game]` at the moment of the click, so the cards that a filter brings back count as before, only the live ones carry `data-game`, and `usePathname` ignores the query string, so `/?category=Cards` is still the hub
- A new criterion = a field of `GameFilter` and of `GameIndex`, its rule in `filterGames`, its parameter in `filterFromParams` and `filterToParams` (left out when it is the default), a test in `filter.test.ts`, and its segment in `SearchCategory`, `SearchMoreFilters`
- Tests: `filter.test.ts` runs the model (`pnpm --filter @pbgo/catalog test`). `apps/web` has no test suite, so `GameSearch`, `HeroSearch`, `SearchCategory`, `SearchMoreFilters`, `HomeBody` and `GameList` are checked in the browser: typing, the panel of each filter (a pick, a second click on it, Clear, Escape, the keyboard), the empty state and its button, a shared link, a phone, a tablet and a desktop width

## The page of a game (`apps/web/src/app/(hub)/game/[slug]/page.tsx`)
- Every game of the catalog has one, live or to come, at `/game/<slug>`, in the hub layout and the platform look. `getGamePage(slug)` (`apps/web/src/lib/game-page.ts`) finds the entry in `getCatalog()` and builds what the page draws; a slug the catalog does not list answers 404 (`(hub)/not-found.tsx`). So a game of the Notion database has a page only while Notion answers: without a key, or with Notion down, the catalog is the live games alone (each a bare `registryRow`) and every other slug is a 404
- It shows the name, a line of figures under it (the players and the duration, worded like the card, then the age: "Ages 8+"), the description, the credits, the rulebooks and a picture. Every one of those values is in the game's Notion row, a live game's as much as one to come's (the description, the age, the authors, the illustrators, the publishers, each a link when the row has their URL, and the rulebooks). A field with no value is not drawn and no text is written in its place, so a live game with no Notion row shows its name and its figures alone; the registry's tagline is not shown. The picture, next to the text on a wide screen, is the one of the game's card drawn larger (`GameCardVisual`): the logo over the background on the grey frame, with no layer between the two, and a plain frame when the game has neither, no placeholder. Both pictures come from the picture route. The description goes through `pickLocalized` (the visitor's language, else English, French, any other) and is marked with the `lang` it was found in
- Under the text, the credits follow one another, one line each (Author, Illustrator, Publisher), then the rulebooks have an area of their own: one link per language the game has a rulebook in, the visitor's language first and the others in `LOCALES` order (`rulebooks` in `game-page.ts`). Each one is a bordered, button-like link (`Item`) that opens the PDF in a new tab: the language's code (EN, FR, ES, DE) on a grey chip (`Badge`, hidden from screen readers, the link carries the `lang`), the title in that language with the game's name ("Règles du jeu - Flip 7", "Rulebook - Flip 7"), then an arrow up-right. The words before the name (`RULEBOOK_TITLES`) are a platform text, in code: the hub reads no game's Sanity project
- The games related to it end the page, under a "Related games" heading, as cards like the home's (`catalogCardProps`: a live card or a card to come, with no `data-game`) in a grid that follows the width; a game with none has no such section. They are the `related` of its catalog entry, built from the `Related Games` and `Parent item` links of the Notion rows, whichever row holds the link (above): the base game of a standalone expansion and that expansion show on each other's page, and a live game shows the planned games related to it
- The button: a live game has "Create a game", a plain `<a href="/<slug>">` to the game's own site (another root layout, loaded whole): the table is created there, not from the hub. A game to come has the same button, disabled, labelled "Coming soon". "Live" is the registry's flag (`CatalogEntry.live`, pure data), not `isPlayable(game)`, which needs the loaded binding and is also true outside production for every game with an engine: the hub says the same in production and in a preview
- Search engines: `robots.txt` has no rule for `/game/<slug>` (`/*/game/` needs a segment before `game`: it keeps crawlers off the tables), so the page says itself whether it may be indexed, in its `robots` meta, and carries its metadata, its JSON-LD and its line of the sitemap from `@pbgo/catalog/seo` (next section)
- `game` was already a reserved slug, being a segment of the table's address (`/<slug>/game/<code>`). `/game/<slug>` has two segments and a table three, and the static `game` wins over `[game]`, so the two never meet

## The SEO of the game pages (`packages/catalog/src/seo.ts`, `@pbgo/catalog/seo`)
- One rule, `isIndexable(entry)`, behind the three things below. A live game follows the registry's `indexable` flag, like its own home (`/<slug>`): `/game/courtisans` may be indexed, the page of a live game whose flag is off may not, whatever its Notion row says. A game to come may be indexed once its Notion row has a description, in any language and whichever one the visitor is shown: until then the page has nothing to say and stays out of the results (`noindex`, no JSON-LD, not in the sitemap). A live game's page on the hub and its home are two pages of one game, both indexable when the flag is on, each with its own canonical
- Pure functions, tested without Next (`seo.test.ts`); `apps/web` hands them what the page loaded. `getGamePage` makes the page's `seo` (`GameSeo`, by `gameSeo(entry, description)`: the description is the one the page shows, from the game's Notion row, live or not; a game with none has none in its metadata and its JSON-LD, nothing stands in for it), `generateMetadata` is `gameMetadata(game.seo, { url: SITE_URL, name: SITE_NAME })`, the page writes its JSON-LD `<script>` only when `seo.indexable`, and `sitemap.ts` is `sitemapItems(SITE_URL, GAMES, entries)`
- The metadata: the title is the game's name (the layout's template adds the site's); the description is the text the page shows, on one line and cut at 160 characters between two words (`metaDescription`); `alternates.canonical` is the page's own absolute URL; `robots` is `index, follow` (with the `googleBot` large-image and snippet hints) or `noindex, follow`, a page that may not be indexed still letting its links be followed; Open Graph (`type: website`, `siteName`, `url`, `title` with the site's name, `description`, `locale` of the language the description was found in, the image) and a Twitter card (large with a wide picture, `summary` with a small one or none). Open Graph and Twitter are set on a `noindex` page too: a link to it still previews in a chat
- Next merges metadata key by key: a page's `openGraph` and `twitter` replace the layout's whole, so they repeat the type, the site name and the URL, and a key with nothing to say is left out, never set to `undefined` (Next reads a key that is present as an override, and `undefined` would wipe the layout's value: the tests walk the object for one)
- The picture a page shares, for every game: `sharePicture(game)`: its Background (wide) if the row has one, else its Logo or Icon (not wide, so the Twitter card is `summary`), else nothing; the picture route's path is made absolute. A live game's own share image, `/<slug>/opengraph-image/share`, stays the picture of its own home, not of its page on the hub. `robots.txt` opens `/api/catalog/` for this (`Allow` beats `Disallow: /api/`, the longest rule wins): Facebook, X and the like fetch the image as crawlers and obey it
- The language: the description is the one the page shows (`pickLocalized`: the visitor's language, else English, French, any other). A crawler sends no language, so it reads the site's default, French, and falls back the same way; the `og:locale` is the language the text was found in. No `hreflang`: a page has one URL, not one per language
- The JSON-LD (`gameJsonLd`, written by `serializeJsonLd`, which escapes `<` so that a name can never close the tag): a schema.org `Game` with `name`, `url`, `description` (on one line, not cut), `image`, `numberOfPlayers`, `timeRequired` (ISO 8601, from the Notion duration, one figure), `typicalAgeRange` (`8-`), `author`, `contributor` (the illustrators: a Game has no `illustrator`) and `publisher` (with the `url` of the Notion row). A field with no value is absent, not empty. No `inLanguage`: the text is not always in the visitor's language
- The sitemap: the hub, the home of every indexable game of the registry, then the hub page of every game of the catalog for which `isIndexable` is true, so it lists exactly the pages that do not say `noindex`. A page is dated by its Notion row's last edit (`lastModified`; none when the date does not read, so none for a live game that has no row); a live game's page is `weekly` with priority 0.8, one to come `monthly` with 0.6. It is rebuilt every ten minutes (`revalidate = 600`, like the catalog), not once per build, so a game that gets a description is listed within twenty minutes at worst. When the catalog is the live games alone (no key, Notion down) the sitemap lists those alone until the next rebuild
- To look at it: `pnpm dev`, then view-source on `/game/<slug>` of a game with a description and of one without, `/sitemap.xml` and `/robots.txt`

## Cache and failure
- `getCatalog` is wrapped in React's `cache` (one read per request); the Notion calls themselves are cached by Next for ten minutes (`next: { revalidate: 600, tags: ["catalog"] }` on the `fetch`, which is what makes a POST cacheable; the stale answer is served while a fresh one is fetched). `revalidateTag(CATALOG_TAG, { expire: 0 })` makes the next read ask Notion again. Next does not cache an answer over 2 MB (the Games database is about 370 KB)
- Notion is the one optional part. Without `NOTION_API_KEY`, or when Notion does not answer (a bad key, a database not shared, a timeout of 5 s per request, an outage), the catalog is the live games alone, each one a `registryRow` (its name, its players and its duration, no picture, no text), `notion` is `"off"` or `"unavailable"`, and a Notion-only slug has no page (`/game/<slug>` answers 404). In development (`NODE_ENV=development`) the catalog also lists the other games of the registry, each a `registryRow` that says "Coming soon" (`buildCatalog`'s `registryGames` option, after the live games and in registry order), so that a hub with no token can be seen with all its grid, names and figures but no picture; a deployment never does, since a game that Notion does not announce is not announced
- What is wrong is logged once per distinct line and per server instance, as `[catalog] …` on `console.warn`: Notion unavailable, the Editors, the Creators or the Games Development database not readable, a missing column, a row left out and why. Nothing logged holds the token

## The picture route (`GET /api/catalog/<slug>/<logo|icon|background>`)
- Why it exists: the pictures of a game are files of its Notion row, and Notion's links to them expire after about an hour, so the catalog only flags them (`art`) and this route serves them: it asks Notion for a fresh link, downloads the picture and sends it, and the CDN keeps it. A page draws a picture from `pictureUrl(game, slot)`, the route followed by `?v=<pictureVersion(editedAt)>`, the version of the row the catalog read. A live game's pictures come through here as well (the hub shows its Notion row, not its Sanity project); a live game with no Notion row flags none, so its pictures answer 404 and its frame is plain
- No picture is no error page: the answer is a 404 and the page that asked draws a plain grey frame (`bg-muted`, a platform token: no initials, no placeholder art). The cards of the hub and the pages of the games (`/game/<slug>`) draw it (above)
- The order, so that nothing costs more than it has to. 1) The slot is one of the three and the slug is a game of the catalog, live or not, whose row has that picture, else 404 (503 when Notion could not be read at all: the game may well exist). 2) `v` is the current version of the row, else a 307 to the right URL, before anything is read or downloaded, so nobody can make the server fetch a picture under a `v` of their own. 3) A fresh read of the Games database for the links, then the download

| Answer | `Cache-Control` |
| --- | --- |
| 200, the row is still the one `v` names | `public, max-age=3600, s-maxage=86400` |
| 200, the row was edited since the catalog read it | `public, max-age=60, s-maxage=60` |
| 307 to the current `?v=` | `public, max-age=0, s-maxage=10` |
| 404 | `public, max-age=60, s-maxage=60` |
| 503, with `Retry-After` (Notion, the catalog or the picture host cannot answer now) | `no-store` |

- Every 200 carries `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` and `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; style-src 'unsafe-inline'; sandbox`, like the media route. Its `Content-Type` comes from the file's first bytes (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, AVIF, SVG), never from what the host said
- One read serves a whole page: the links read from Notion are believed for 30 s, one read runs at a time however many pictures ask for it, a failed read is remembered for 5 s (or as long as Notion's `Retry-After`, when longer), and a 429 that asks for 3 s or less is waited for once. A hub of 66 pictures on a cold server is one query, not 66, which Notion's rate limit (about 3 requests a second) would not take. The memo is per server instance
- What the server fetches (`picture.ts`, the one place that does it): the visitor gives a slug and a slot, never a URL. The link comes from Notion and must be https, with no credentials and the default port; an IP address, a name without a dot, `localhost` and the names a network keeps for itself (`.localhost`, `.local`, `.internal`, `.localdomain`, `.home.arpa`) are refused. A redirect is followed by hand, three at most, each one checked again. One 8 s limit covers the download, the size is capped at 4 MiB while the body streams (a function answers 4.5 MB at most), and the file must be a picture by its bytes. Notion's token goes to Notion only: the picture host gets `Accept: image/*` and nothing else. The logs name the host, never a link (a Notion link carries a signature)
- Limits, on purpose:
  - The server does not resolve names, so a public name that points at a private address (`127.0.0.1.nip.io`) passes: the links come from the Notion workspace, trusted as far as whoever can edit it
  - A row's version is its `last_edited_time`, which has a minute's grain: two edits of one row within the same minute keep the old picture until the row is edited again
  - A picture added to a row shows once the catalog has read the row again (10 minutes) and a cached 404 has expired (1 minute)
  - `getCatalog` has no single flight across concurrent requests: a server that has just started and is asked for many pictures at once may answer a few 503s (`no-store`, nothing is kept). A page that draws the pictures has already read the catalog to build their addresses, which warms the cache

## The key (`NOTION_API_KEY`)
- A read-only internal integration (Notion › Settings › Integrations › New integration, capability "Read content" only, no user information), connected to the Games, Editors, Creators and Games Development databases (Connections of each database). Its secret starts with `ntn_`
- The secret goes in `~/.config/pbgo/secrets.env` (outside the repo) as `NOTION_API_KEY=…`, never in a chat, a commit or a pull request. `pnpm setup-env` writes it to `apps/web/.env.local` and pushes it to every Vercel target (encrypted; `--no-push` keeps it local); a deployed value applies from the next deployment. It only checks the shape of the value (`ntn_…` or `secret_…`): a value of another shape is neither written nor pushed and is not repeated in the message. Nothing is sent to Notion
- To try it before `setup-env`, put the same line in the `apps/web/.env.local` of the checkout (the script falls back on it) and run `pnpm catalog:list`
- The key stays server side: it has no `NEXT_PUBLIC_` prefix and no client component imports `@pbgo/catalog/server`

## `pnpm catalog:list`
What the hub would list right now: one line per game (slug, name, kind — `live`, `live (registry)` for a live game that Notion has no row for (listed with what the registry knows of it), or `planned` —, the Priority / Status / Effort that rank a planned game, which of Logo / Icon / Background and which description languages are filled, players, duration, weight (the `Complexity` column), age, categories, authors, illustrators, publishers, the games related to it) and, under it, the rows Notion has that are not in the catalog and why (no slug, a slug that is not valid, a slug already taken), the columns that are missing and the databases that cannot be read. It reads `NOTION_API_KEY` from the environment or `apps/web/.env.local`, queries each of the four databases once, caches nothing and never prints the token. Without a key it lists the live games, from the registry, and says so; if Notion cannot be read it prints why and exits 1. Run it after filling the Slug column of a new row: a row that is not in the list is not on the site

## Working on the package
- `pnpm --filter @pbgo/catalog test` and `pnpm --filter @pbgo/catalog typecheck`. The tests run the reader against a fake Notion built from the shapes Notion really answers (`src/testing.ts`); a new field = the column in `COLUMNS`, the field of `NotionGame`, its line in `planned()`, the fixture in `gamePage`, a test (a column that is a relation to another database goes through `readRelated`, and `fakeNotion({ games, editors, creators, development })` answers that database; `developmentSchema` replaces the schema it answers for Games Development)
- The picture route is tested against a fake Notion and fake picture hosts (`fakeWeb`), never the network: the answers, the headers, the memo and the refused links. A change to `route.ts` or `picture.ts` is checked by breaking it on purpose and seeing a test fail
- The SEO is tested without Next or the network (`seo.test.ts`, on the entries that `catalogEntry` of `testing.ts` builds from `registryGame` and `notionGame`): the rule, the cut of the description, the metadata (and that no key of it is `undefined`), the JSON-LD and the lines of the sitemap
- The source is run as it is by Node (`pnpm catalog:list`, type stripping) and bundled by Next (`transpilePackages`): in `src/`, a value import from a sibling file has no extension that both accept, so a file imports only types from its siblings (`import type`) and the pieces that need each other meet in `server.ts`, `route.ts` and `scripts/list.ts`; `scripts/` import with `.ts` extensions. `seo.ts` is the exception, since Node never loads it (Next bundles it and Vitest runs it): it imports values from `./catalog` and from core (`lib/i18n`, `lib/site-url`). `picture.ts` imports nothing at all. `server-only` throws under plain Node, so only `src/server.ts` imports it (the tests of `server.ts` and `route.ts` mock it). `list-script.test.ts` runs the script under plain Node to keep this true
