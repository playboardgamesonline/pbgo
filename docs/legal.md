# Legal pages and personal data

The Privacy Policy, the Terms of Use and the Cookie Policy (`/legal/privacy`, `/legal/terms`, `/legal/cookies`), the inventory of personal data they are written from, who receives it, how long it is kept, and the rule that keeps text and code in step (PLT-11, PLT-110). The pages describe what the site does; they do not claim that it complies with a law. The Privacy Policy and the Terms are a draft for the owner, or a lawyer, to validate: none of this is legal advice.

## The pages
- `/legal/cookies` (`(hub)/legal/cookies/page.tsx`): every cookie and storage key by name, what the site counts, what a refusal stores. The cookie banner links to it. Its entries are `Stored` (`LegalEntries` in mono)
- `/legal/privacy` (`(hub)/legal/privacy/page.tsx`): who we are, then what is collected in the order a person meets it (visit, table, sign-in, feedback, statistics), why (legal bases), who receives it, outside the EU, security, rights, changes. The sentences about PostHog follow `ANALYTICS_ENABLED`, as the Cookie Policy's do. The retention of tables, of feedback, of the presence of members, of the state of a finished game and of the seats of an unranked game, and the days a deleted account is kept (the time to reopen it with the link of the e-mail) are not typed in the page: it prints the constants of `@pbgo/core/lib/retention`, the ones the nightly clean-up reads, so the text and the job cannot drift
- `/legal/terms` (`(hub)/legal/terms/page.tsx`): the service, accounts and nicknames, playing fair, the games and other people's rights, what belongs to whom, availability and responsibility, ending (the member deletes their account in their settings, which closes it at once and gives 7 days to reopen it; the written request stays for whoever cannot), changes. The sign-in page says that continuing accepts them (`login-card.tsx`), and the sign-up page that creating an account does, with the minimum age, 13 (`signup-card.tsx`: a line under the button, no checkbox)
- `/legal` (the notice) stays as it is, unlinked
- The frame and its pieces are `apps/web/src/components/legal-page.tsx` (`LegalPage`, `LegalSection`, `LegalText`, `LegalEntries`, `LegalList`, `LegalUpdated`); the footer's list is `apps/web/src/lib/legal-pages.ts`. A page ends with `Last updated <date>`: change the date with the text
- Draft state: the two new pages keep the stub's "This page is being written" line and `noindex` until the owner (or a lawyer) has read them. Going final is a small change in each page: delete the line, drop `robots: { index: false }`, and add the page to the sitemap if the owner wants it there
- Not on the pages, because the owner chose to ship without them (Decisions 1 and 3): a governing-law and courts section, the publisher's identity beyond "oré"
- On the pages and decided by the owner: the minimum age, 13, with no parental agreement (Decisions 2). On the pages as proposals the owner has not confirmed: the retention of session replays (30 days) and of counts (12 months). Confirmed by the owner on 2026-11-06 and done by the nightly clean-up (PLT-113): tables 30 days, feedback 12 months. On the Terms as a plan that does not exist yet: the chat's banned-word filter (below)

## The rule
A change that adds, moves or removes personal data changes the legal pages **in the same pull request**, and this inventory with them:
- a cookie or a storage key (browser): the Cookie Policy's list, and "On the device" below
- an analytics event, a property, a tool or a recording rule: `packages/analytics/src/events.ts`, the Cookie Policy's "Statistics", the Privacy Policy's "Statistics" and the rows below
- a column, a table, a field or a log that holds personal data: the rows below, the Privacy Policy's section for the situation it belongs to, and its retention. A stored field needs a way to be deleted: a table or a bucket joins the nightly clean-up (`core/server/retention.ts`), with its period beside the others in `core/lib/retention.ts`. A table that points at an account (`references auth.users`) says what happens when the account goes: `on delete cascade`, or `set null` with the scrub of what names the person (the way `game_participants` does, migration 0006); `supabase/tests/account_deletion.sql` fails for a key with no action or restrict, which would block every deletion. Personal data that hangs on an account without a key (as `games.member_ids` and `games.players` do) is scrubbed by `scrub_member_from_tables` (migration 0011)
- a processor, or a request from the visitor's browser to a third party (a font, a picture, a script, a widget): the table of recipients below, the Privacy Policy's "Who receives your data" and "Outside the European Union"
- a sign-in provider, or any account data (profile, friends, ranks, history, stored chat): the Privacy Policy first. PLT-13 (profiles, ratings, tables, results) and PLT-23 (friends and conversations) both add personal data, and the policy has to say so before they ship. The results of the games, the totals, the ratings and the board of a game are in it since the recorder of PLT-13 (below, "The games played"); the profiles and the tables of the platform schema are still to come. PLT-23 also needs the French law on minors and social networks read again (Findings 11)
- a paid plan opening, or a rule of play (what is forbidden, what gets a visitor removed): the Terms

## Inventory (read from the code, 2026-11-06; the players online added on 2026-11-07; the country and two more settings on 2026-11-09; the date of birth, which the age check keeps apart, on 2026-11-09 too (PLT-125); the second step of a sign-in, PLT-129)
How to read it: *Basis* is the GDPR article 6 basis the pages give (service = contract; strictly necessary = no consent for the cookie or storage). *Kept* says what the code does; **bold** is a proposal that the code does not do yet.

### On the device
Every cookie and storage key is listed by name in the Cookie Policy. Browser-side keys written beyond it: none. The server writes the per-game player cookie, the language cookie, the sign-in cookies (and, for a few minutes, the state cookie of a sign-in with Steam) and the gamemaster's cookie; the browser writes the two display cookies (`pbgo-motion` and `pbgo-transparency`, `packages/accounts/src/display.ts`) when a member's choice is applied to a device. Nothing is written on arrival (checked on production: no `Set-Cookie` on `/`, `/login` or the API). One exception is possible: Vercel's security checkpoint (Bot Protection in Challenge mode, and Attack Mode) keeps a one-hour challenge session in the browser of a visitor it challenges, which is storing something on arrival. Both are off unless the owner switched them on (⚠ read the project's Firewall); before Challenge is switched on, its cookie is read in a browser that was challenged and added to the Cookie Policy, which then stops saying that nothing is stored on arrival (`docs/bots-and-abuse.md`).

### Nightly clean-up
Vercel Cron calls `GET /api/cron/purge` every night with `CRON_SECRET`. It deletes the tables 30 days after `updated_at`, the feedback, screenshots included, 12 months after `created_at`, and the last sight of a member on the site (`member_presence`) a day after the last beat, deletes the state of a table that is over a day after its last write when its game is recorded, and folds the seats of the unranked games older than 12 months into the totals of their players (the constants of `core/lib/retention.ts`), deletes the closed accounts whose day has passed, the feedback sent with the address of each first (`ERASE_MAX_ACCOUNTS`, 20 a night, the oldest first), and logs counts and dates only: no name, no code, no address. Production only: `pbgo-preview` is not scheduled, its data is tests (`docs/supabase-and-env.md`, Retention).

No payment data (nothing is on sale), no location, no contacts, no advertising identifier, no marketing cookie, no cross-site tracking, no profiling, no automated decision with legal effects. ## Findings of the inventory
What surprised, in the order of weight. None is fixed by PLT-110, which writes texts; each is either said in the pages as it is, or waits for a ticket.
1. **Tables and feedback were never deleted** (fixed by PLT-113, 2026-11-06). There was no sweeper, no `pg_cron`, no retention anywhere, and `games` holds nicknames and account picture addresses of every table since 12 October. The owner confirmed 30 days (tables) and 12 months (feedback), and the nightly job (`/api/cron/purge`, `docs/supabase-and-env.md`) keeps them, in production only. It could not be `pg_cron`: Supabase refuses to delete a Storage object from SQL, so the screenshot of a deleted feedback would have stayed in the bucket. The job has to have run in production before the pages go live
2. **The player id was public and also the credential** (fixed in PLT-112, 2026-11-06). Before it, the id that a table showed to whoever read it was the value of the player's own `<slug>_player` cookie, which the server took for proof of who they were. Now the cookie holds a secret that no response, row, event or log carries, and the player id is the first 128 bits of its SHA-256 (`packages/core/server/player-id.ts`): still public, and it proves nothing. A device is still one player per game for 30 days, so the player id is a stable pseudonym that a table shows to the people at it; PLT-13 replaces the cookie with a Supabase session. The old cookies (a UUID) were retired by the deploy and read as no cookie, so the values that were stored under the old scheme (the one `feedback.player_id` of production, the ids PostHog received) can no longer be used to act as anyone
3. **No way to find an account's tables** (fixed by PLT-13 for the games played, 2026-11-08, and by PLT-36 for the tables, 2026-11-09). A table records the player id (a hash of the cookie's secret), never the account, but `games.member_ids` (migration `0005`) links a seat to an account and the games that were played are written with the account id (`game_participants.user_id`), so erasure by account of the games finished is a deletion of the account. The tables that are still in the database are scrubbed by `scrub_member_from_tables` (migration `0011`, a trigger on `auth.users`): the seat becomes "Deleted player" with no picture, whatever the status of the table
4. **The picture address of a provider can hold the account id** (Discord: `cdn.discordapp.com/avatars/<user id>/<hash>`; Twitch: a file name on its own CDN, not checked for the account id; Steam: a hash of the picture on Valve's CDN, not checked for the account id), and it is written into `games.players` and shown to everyone at the table
5. **A third party sees every visitor's IP address before any choice**: Sanity's CDN, on every hub page. Not a cookie, and the Privacy Policy says so
6. **Supabase Auth keeps the IP address** of each session (`auth.sessions.ip`) and, by default, of each event in a table of the project's database that nothing empties (the lines about an account go when the account is deleted, PLT-36; the sessions go with it)
7. **The chat channel is public**: `core/lib/chat.ts` says the publishable key can listen to `chat:<slug>:<code>`. A table code is the secret
8. **The Cookie Policy missed two keys**: `pbgo:queue` (session storage, "Play ranked") and `game:debug` (local storage, Shift+D). Added in PLT-110. **`game:debug` left the policy in PLT-68**: the debugging tools never run on the production deployment (`debugAllowed` in `packages/core/components/game/debug-rule.ts`, read from `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_ENV`), which then neither reads nor writes the key. A preview deployment and a machine in development still use it, and only the team opens those
9. **A deletion by account cannot find the PostHog data** (PLT-36, 2026-11-09): the server counts use the player id as their distinct id (a hash of the secret of one game's cookie on one device) and `identify(<player id>)` is the only call the browser makes after "Accept"; no account id and no e-mail address ever reaches PostHog (`packages/analytics`, `core/server/analytics.ts`), and the only link from an account to its player ids is `games.member_ids`, which lives as long as the table. So the older wording of PLT-36, "PostHog person data deleted", is not achievable by account, and the Privacy Policy says so ("Statistics": the counts go with their retention). **The site's PostHog project is not the one the tools reach.** `docs/analytics.md` says the project is `pbgo`; the one reachable is `aurelienlouvel-space` (only `$pageview` events in 30 days). The IP discard and the retention that the pages state are therefore not verified
10. Server-side counts run whatever the banner answer, keyed on the player id (a hash of the player cookie's secret). The pages call it legitimate interest, which a lawyer should validate
11. **Friends, profiles and conversations may make the site a "social network" in the French sense.** Parliament adopted a ban of social networks for under-15s on 21 July 2026 and the Conseil constitutionnel struck it down on 14 August 2026 (decision 2026-911 DC); the government plans a new text for spring 2027. A game is not in itself a social network and a chat between the players of a table is outside the definition, but profiles, friend lists and conversations (PLT-23) are the features that could bring the site in. Read the law again when they ship
12. **A sign-up that is never confirmed leaves an address in `auth.users`** (PLT-117). `signUp` creates the user at once, unconfirmed, with the address, the password hash and the display name, and Supabase's documentation says nothing of deleting such users; the nightly clean-up (`/api/cron/purge`) does not touch `auth.users`. A typo, or someone else's address typed on purpose, stays until it is deleted by hand. Proposal for a ticket: delete the users still unconfirmed after a few days from the nightly job, with the period beside the others in `core/lib/retention.ts`, and say it in the Privacy Policy (the machinery of PLT-36 would carry it: `erase_account` deletes any account, and the job already lists and deletes them one by one)
13. **Anyone can make Supabase e-mail any address.** The confirmation e-mail goes to the address typed, before anything proves it is the typist's; the brakes are Supabase's (one e-mail a minute to an address, 30 e-mails an hour to the whole project with a custom SMTP, the rate limits by IP) and Resend's Free plan stops at 100 e-mails a day, which a flood can use up for everybody. A CAPTCHA (Authentication › Attack Protection) is the usual extra brake; the sign-up is shaped for one (`captchaToken`) and draws none (PLT-117)
14. **A date of birth kept in `user_metadata` cannot gate anything by itself** (PLT-111, then PLT-125). `user_metadata` is the free object of the account, and Supabase Auth lets a signed-in member write it with `updateUser({ data })` from their own browser, so any value there is the member's own word and never a permission. **Done in PLT-125**: the date of birth is not kept there but declared once to the server and kept in `member_birthdates`, a table the member cannot write, which is what the age check of the tables reads (`docs/accounts.md`, "The age check"); the country stays in `user_metadata` as the member's own word and gates nothing. **What stays true**: the date is a declaration, not a proof (a member can type a date that is not theirs, and the Terms ask them not to), and a real check would be a provider of identity or age checks, which is another processor and another budget: the owner has not asked for it
15. **A member who signed in with Steam has no e-mail address** (the stand-in one is made up and unreadable), so the way the Privacy Policy gives to find an account, "the e-mail address of the account", does not fit them: the owner finds the member by SteamID64 (`public.external_identities.subject`) and deletes the user (the table goes with it), and the policy now says that a Steam member gives the link of their Steam profile. The link of a Steam profile is public: it names the account and proves nothing, so before the owner deletes or exports a Steam member's data they ask for proof that the account is the asker's (the policy says so: "We may ask for proof that the data is yours"; for instance a change to the profile's summary that the owner can see, or the member signing in during a call). The self-service deletion (PLT-36) removes the need. Such a member also cannot be sent an e-mail, which is fine today and a limit for a password reset or an e-mail notification later
16. **Hiding the Twitch button leaves a member whose only way in is Twitch with none** (2026-11-09, the owner's choice: Twitch only links to an account). There is no password reset yet, so such a member cannot get back in unless Twitch is a sign-in again (`SIGN_IN_PROVIDERS`).
