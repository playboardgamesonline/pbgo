# Analytics

PostHog through `@pbgo/analytics`: typed events, the consent banner, recordings, masking and the scrubbing of table codes.

## Analytics (PostHog, `@pbgo/analytics`)
Optional: without `NEXT_PUBLIC_POSTHOG_KEY` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_POSTHOG_HOST` nothing runs (no request, no banner, no "Cookies" link). Vercel's `<Analytics />` is a separate, cookie-free measure and is unchanged. Every event carries `env` (`production`, `preview` or `development`: `VERCEL_ENV`, copied to `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_ENV` by `next.config.ts`), the property that tells a real player from a test
- Events are typed in `packages/analytics/src/events.ts` (snake_case, `game` = the slug on every game event) and hold no personal data: never a nickname, chat text or table code. A new event = one entry there, then one line at the call site. `language_changed` (`from`, `to`: any of the platform's thirteen languages) is sent by the games' language pickers and by the Language row of `/me`, once the change has happened. The profile events (PLT-39) are browser events with no name and no tag: `profile_viewed` (`own`, `hidden`, `from`: `seat`, `leaderboard`, `history`, `account` or `direct`, from the `?from=` of the address), `profile_updated` (`nickname`, `tag`, `pronouns`, `country`, `city`, `favorites`, `bio`: which fields changed, never their values: a bio and a nickname are text that a player typed), `profile_update_refused` (`reason`: `taken`, `invalid` or `unavailable`) and `profile_history_hidden` (`hidden`).
- Server events (counted whatever the visitor answered): `trackAfter(event, playerId, properties)` of `@pbgo/core/server/analytics`, in `packages/core/routes/api/games/**`, after `updateGame` returns and never inside its modifier (that runs again when it loses a race). The id is the player id of the game, a hash of the secret in its `<slug>_player` cookie (the secret never leaves the browser and the server), so nothing more is stored on the device; `identity_stable: false` until accounts exist (PLT-13), no person profile, no geolocation. `game_finished` is sent when the returned row is `over`; its `duration_s` and `moves_count` are approximations (`updated_at − created_at`, `version`) flagged `approximate: true`. Not tracked yet: `game_abandoned` (needs a sweeper)
- The search of a visitor who presses Play (PLT-21, `POST /api/hub/play`, `core/server/quick-play.ts`): `quick_play_started` (`mode`, `games`: the slugs, sent on the press and not on the calls that keep the search alive), `quick_play_matched` (`game`, `mode`, `how`: `joined` or `opened`, `wait_s`: the seconds waited, `players`: sent when the answer to a call says a table has begun and the browser says how long it waited) and `quick_play_cancelled` (`mode`, `games`, `wait_s` when known). Counts, slugs and enums only: no nickname, no account, no table code. The id is the player id of the first game of the search, as for the other server events.
- Browser: `AnalyticsProvider` is mounted by the hub, `[game]` and `global-not-found` root layouts, never by the gamemaster. The SDK talks to `/ingest` (rewritten to PostHog's EU hosts, `next.config.ts`; `skipTrailingSlashRedirect` is global because PostHog's endpoints end with a slash, and every canonical URL and the sitemap are explicit strings without one). Before the visitor answers it runs with `persistence: 'memory'` (nothing stored: each page load is a new anonymous visitor, so unique-visitor counts before consent are inflated). "Accept all" switches to `localStorage+cookie`, calls `identify(<player id>)` at a table (`useIdentifyPlayer`) and starts session recording; "Reject optional" or a withdrawal goes back to memory and sweeps the SDK's keys (`keys.ts`). `game_opened_from_hub` is counted on `/` and `/games` (the hub's two lists of games) from the links that carry `data-game="<slug>"` (the cards of the live games, which lead to the game's page on the hub: a new link from the hub to a game needs the attribute). The card of a game to come has none, and neither have the "Create a table" button and the cards of the related games of `/game/<slug>`, which the listener (mounted on `/` only) does not see
- The answer is `pbgo-consent` in `localStorage` (choice, marketing and date, with the revision), asked again after 13 months (`consent.ts`). The banner (`CookieBanner` of `@pbgo/ui/platform`) is a wide card that floats over the bottom of the screen, centred and 896 px wide at most (the width of the screen less its margin below that): a title (`texts.label`, also the name of the dialog) and one sentence that ends with a "here" link to the Cookie Policy, `/legal/cookies` (the banner puts the full stop after the link), and three buttons laid out the way Claude's are, Customize (`secondary`, the grey one) over Reject optional (`secondary`) and Accept all, the same size and the same shape (the navbar's Sign in and Let's play: the `lg` of the Button with their corners, 2.5rem high, `rounded-lg`, `text-sm`), so refusing is as easy as accepting (the words are in `texts.ts` and in English for everyone: the site is in English only for now, so `banner.tsx` no longer follows the visitor's language, and the FR/ES/DE drafts and `locale.ts` wait there for the language pass, PLT-32; the English of the card is the owner's; `texts.test.ts` caps the sentence and the words). From `lg` the words and the buttons are side by side, like a bar, the block of buttons as wide as its longest label (`w-max`, so a long label of another language widens it instead of overflowing) and centred on the card's height; under it the buttons are below the words and take the width of the card. Customize swaps the content of the card for the choices (the card holds its `view`, `bar` or `preferences`): Necessary (always on), Analytics (the identifier kept on the device, and the recordings that hang on it) and Marketing (a switch like the other, off: the site has no marketing cookie yet, but the owner wants the choice there, and its answer kept, for the day it has one), then Back and Save preferences; Escape goes back. The stored answer is `choice` (analytics, `accepted` or `refused`: the only one the SDK follows), `marketing` (a boolean that no tool follows yet) and a date, with a `rev` beside them in the storage (`CONSENT_REVISION`): Accept all turns both switches on, Reject optional both off (`ACCEPT_ALL`, `REJECT_OPTIONAL`, and a `Choice` stands for them in `consentStore.choose`), Save keeps each as it is set, and the switches open on the stored answer (`answerOf`, `current` of `ConsentBanner`). The `cookie_consent` event carries both. The day a tool needs marketing, raise `CONSENT_REVISION`: every answer given under the old one is asked again, so nobody is held to a yes given for something that did not exist (the Cookie Policy promises it). The three-state version with the analytics and the recordings as two switches was built and dropped on 2026-10-30; the recordings are part of Analytics now. The card is about 136 px high on a desktop (the owner's English sentence takes three lines, as high as the two rows of buttons), so with its margin, 160 px, it keeps 18% of a 1440×900 screen free (21% of a 1024×768 one), 234 px in a tablet 768 px wide, and 273 px on a phone, which is 32% of a 390×844 screen: a shorter sentence is the lever. The choices are taller, 352 px with the margin on a desktop and 586 px on a phone, and the card never grows taller than the window. A visitor who already answered and reopens it gets a close button at the top right of the card (Escape closes it too) that keeps the answer, and the focus on the card. The full disclosure (every cookie and storage key, what is counted either way, what a refusal stores) lives in `/legal/cookies`, not in the card. The banner and the "Cookies" links that reopen it (`ConsentLink`: the Cookie Policy page, the game home footer; the settings menu row) are hidden on pages that do not report: `/gamemaster`, `/docs`, `/<slug>/preview/*` and `/auth/reopen-account`, the page the link of the e-mail opens after a member deleted their account, whose address holds a token (`isExcludedPath`). The banner is `ph-no-capture` (its clicks are reported once, by `cookie_consent`, never by autocapture under the identity being given up; `banner.test.ts` keeps the class). What the visitor is told lives in `texts.ts` and in `/legal/cookies` (`(hub)/legal/cookies/page.tsx`, which lists every cookie and storage key by name): change them together with the behaviour, and the Privacy Policy's Statistics section (`/legal/privacy`) with them when what is counted, stored or recorded changes (the inventory is `docs/legal.md`)
- The banner keeps its room free (`reserve.ts`, `reserveSpace`): it measures its outer box, the card plus the margin around it (a `ResizeObserver`), and publishes that height as `--consent-h` on `<body>`, with an inline `padding-bottom` of the same height, and removes both when it goes away (0px without a banner). Never on `:root`: the platform sets no value there, or every game would inherit it. A page that scrolls simply ends above the card; a screen as tall as the viewport and an element fixed to the bottom do the arithmetic themselves (see `docs/design/01-guidelines/04-in-game-rules.md`). On a wide screen the card is narrower than the strip it reserves, which is the price of measuring one box. The screens of a game are the exception: the layout of the games mounts the provider with `floating` (`reserveSpace` with `pad: false`), so the banner floats over the page, which is not padded, and the table's canvas keeps the whole height of the window and never resizes with the banner; the variable is still published, and the HUD and the overlays of a game stand in a layer that ends above the card, which is the part that resizes when the banner appears, is reopened from a "Cookies" link or closes (the chat of the table is not in it: it stays against the bottom edge, under the card)
- Recordings mask every input; the chat is `ph-no-capture`, and so are the QR code and the setup key of multi-factor authentication (PLT-129), while the digits of a one-time code (`InputOTP` of the platform: the sign-up's and the second step's) are `ph-mask`, since the slots draw them as text, which the masking of inputs does not reach; nicknames printed by core are `ph-mask` (the tables draw theirs in a canvas, which is not recorded), and so are those of the hub's table components of `@pbgo/ui` (the seats of a lobby and of a row, the host's name, the card of a player, and the hidden title and description of a lobby, which name the host and spell the table's code; PLT-106) and those of the friends (PLT-23): the rows and the card of the friends' panel and the bar's list of a game, the requests, the page of the friends, the windows that ask before a friend is removed or a player blocked, the toasts that name a member and the blocked players of the settings. The chat window is not masked yet: only the demo account opens it, and the PR of the messages does it. The names in an `aria-label` or a `title` of those components are attributes, which the class does not reach (the menu of the account has always had one). A game that prints a nickname in its own DOM adds `ph-mask` there, and `ph-no-capture` as well when it sits inside a button or a link: autocapture sends the text of a clicked `a` / `button` (`$el_text`), nested text included. Never set `maskTextFn` in `options.ts`: it replaces the stars of every masked node and would show the nicknames
- Table codes (`/<slug>/game/<CODE>`, `/game/<slug>/<CODE>` on the hub, `/api/<slug>/games/<CODE>/…`, and `Table #XXX-XXX` of the tab title) are scrubbed by `scrub.ts`: from every property of an event (`before_send`), from the page address, the links and the network timings of a replay (`maskCapturedNetworkRequestFn`, `maskAttributeFn`), and the `<title>` is masked in the replay (`maskTextSelector: "title"`). The code field is `ph-mask` too. A new place that prints the code needs the same care. The address of a profile (`/player/<nickname>-<tag>`, PLT-39) holds a name and a tag. It is scrubbed to `/player/:handle` (`HANDLE_PLACEHOLDER`; `?from=` stays) from page addresses, links and event properties. The title of the page is "Player profile", never the name. Nicknames on a profile are `ph-mask`, and links that hold one are `ph-no-capture`. The pattern is any six letters or digits after `/game/` or `/games/`, so the hub's `/game/<slug>` of a six-letter slug (`/game/hanabi`) is reported as `/game/:code`: a slug is public, so nothing leaks, that page is just less precise in the reports. The client's masking options win over the project's "Privacy and masking" settings: leave the project's text and block selectors empty. The recorder compresses its larger chunks (gzip), so check a replay after gunzipping it, never by searching the raw upload
- Not in the repo, set in the PostHog project `pbgo` (EU cloud): recording enabled for the domain, exception autocapture, web vitals, IP anonymisation, the internal/test-account filter, authorised URLs

## Events and the question each answers
Three kinds of identity, by design. No event ever carries an account id, an address or a name.
- **A player id** (`trackAfter`): the table's events. The same device is the same id in one game (`identity_stable: false`).
- **No id** (`trackAnonymously`): what no player carries. A new random id is made for each event and used once, so two events are never tied together. Account steps, feedback, deleted tables, refusals.
- **The browser's id** (`trackClient`): none that outlives the page before the answer to the banner, a stored one after "Accept all", tied to the player id at a table. These events leave before the answer too, anonymous like the page views.

Every property is a boolean, a count or a word of a closed list; `game` is a slug, and `options` are the table's own options. `events.test.ts` checks it for each event: a new event needs a fixture there, and a property of free text does not compile.

| Event | From | Properties | Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `game_created` | server, player id | game, options, max_players, locale, visibility | How many tables are opened, with which options, and how many to everyone? |
| `player_joined` | server, player id | game, seat, players, visibility | How full do tables get, public or private? |
| `game_started` | server, player id | game, players, options, visibility | How many tables begin, public or private? |
| `game_finished` | server, player id | game, duration_s, players, moves_count | How long do games last, how many end? |
| `game_left`, `rematch_started`, `chat_message_sent` | server, player id | game, phase (starting, playing, over) or kind | Where do players leave (the ready check, a game in progress, the end screen's Continue), do they play again, is the chat used? |
| `takeover_vote_started`, `takeover_completed`, `turn_auto_played` | server, player id | game | How often is somebody played for? |
| `table_deleted` | server, no id | game, reason | How do tables end up empty (lobby left, ready check left, game left, nobody ready)? |
| `feedback_sent` | server, no id | game, type, with_screenshot, with_email | How much feedback, of which kind? |
| `game_route_error` | server, no id | game, route, code, status | What do the routes refuse, and how often? Not the 429, not a request that no player made; 6 a minute at most per instance |
| `signed_in` | server, no id (a provider, the e-mail link); browser (password, e-mail code) | method, new_account | How do people sign in, how many accounts are new? Counted at the first step: a member with a second step is counted once |
| `signed_out`, `account_deletion` | server, no id | none; step (requested, reopened, deleted_now, purged) | How many leave, and how many accounts go? |
| `friend_request_sent`, `friend_request_answered` | server, no id | source (form, profile); answer (accepted, declined, cancelled) | Do members add friends by typing a handle or from a profile, and how are the requests answered? Sending a request to somebody who had sent one is an answer (accepted) and not a request |
| `friend_removed`, `member_blocked` | server, no id | none; were_friends | How often are friends removed and members blocked, and is it friends who fall out? Never who: no name, no tag, no account |
| `auth_started` | browser | flow, method | Where do sign-ins and sign-ups start? |
| `signup_code_sent` | browser | resent | Do sign-ups reach the code, do codes get lost? |
| `account_changed` | browser | change, service | Is the second step used, are services linked? |
| `setting_changed` | browser | setting | Which settings are used? The key, never the value. The language, the username and the history have their own events |
| `table_setting_changed` | browser | game, setting, visibility | Do hosts open their tables to everyone? |
| `public_table_clicked` | browser | game, from | Which lists lead to a table? |
| `list_searched` | browser | list, with_text, filters, results, sort | Do visitors search, filter, sort? Do they find something? After a one second pause, never the text |
| `game_page_clicked` | browser | game, target | Do visitors create a table or buy the game from a game's page? |
| `game_opened_from_hub`, `invite_link_copied`, `rules_opened`, `language_changed`, `cookie_consent` | browser | see `events.ts` | Which cards are opened, are invitations and rules used, which languages, how many accept? |

A button of a game's page that is a link of a server component counts itself from its markup: `data-cta` and `data-cta-game`, read by the listener of `AnalyticsProvider` and checked before they are sent (`cta.ts`). A change of the account that a row of the settings keeps goes through `changing` (`apps/web/src/lib/settings-change.ts`), which counts it once it is kept and never for the demo account.

## Check once the site's project is connected
Do it on a preview, then on production. Filter on `env`.
1. Open the home and `/games`, answer the banner. Expect `$pageview`, then `cookie_consent`. Before the answer nothing is stored on the device (local storage and cookies are empty); after "Accept all" the `ph_` key is.
2. Type a word in the search of `/games`: one `list_searched` a second later, `with_text: true`, no text anywhere in the event. Press "Create" and the rules of a game's page: `game_page_clicked`, `rules_opened`.
3. Open a table: `game_created`. Change its speed: `table_setting_changed`. Sit down from a second browser: `player_joined`. Leave with both: `table_deleted` with a `distinct_id` that is new each time.
4. Sign in with a provider and out: `signed_in` (server), `signed_out`.
5. Look at the properties of every event above: no nickname, address, text or table code; no `$ip`. The recordings keep what the first section says.

Funnels worth building (dashboards are PLT-18):
1. Sign-up to first table: `auth_started` (flow signup), `signup_code_sent`, `signed_in` (new_account), `game_created` or `player_joined`. Whole only for visitors who accepted: the stored id is what ties the browser's steps to the player id at the table.
2. A table: `game_created`, `game_started`, `game_finished`. `game_finished` is sent for the player whose move ended the game, so the steps share a person only when it is the host.
3. A public table: `public_table_clicked`, `player_joined` (visibility public), `game_started` (visibility public).
4. From the hub to a table: `game_opened_from_hub`, `game_page_clicked` (create_table), `game_created`.
