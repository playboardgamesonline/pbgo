# Love Letter: rules spec

## Edition and sources

- Edition: Z-Man Games, 2025 (the year of the copyright line, p. 15; the full edition, with 21 character cards for 2 to 6 players and a cloth bag in the box)
- Rulebook: English, 16 pages, no version or date printed, file supplied (read in full, the text and every page as an image; the page numbers are the PDF's own, the cover being page 1, and the printed numbers match them, the cover and the last page printing none)
- Type: base-game
- Covers: the game as the booklet plays it for 2 to 6 players with the 21 character cards, the Spy and the Chancellor included; the classic version of p. 14 is left out, and the booklet names no other variant and no expansion
- Status: validated 2026-11-18
- FAQ: none found; the publisher's page for the game (https://www.zmangames.com/game/love-letter/, looked at on 2026-11-18) links the rulebook and no FAQ or errata

## At a glance

- Players: 2–6
- Goal: gain favor tokens by winning rounds, with the highest card or as the only player left, until a player holds the number of tokens that the number of players asks for (6 at two players, 3 at five or six).
- Shape: a game is a series of rounds; in a round the players take turns clockwise, each drawing a card and playing one of two, until the deck runs out or one player is left, and the winner of the round gains a favor token.

The 21 character cards are 2 Spies, 6 Guards, 2 Priests, 2 Barons, 2 Handmaids, 2 Princes, 2 Chancellors, 1 King, 1 Countess and 1 Princess (p. 2). A set of dots on the left of each character card shows how many copies of it are in the deck, the yellow dots being the copies that the classic version removes (p. 8). The 21 character cards share one back (the picture of p. 2). The rulebook asks for nothing that is not in the box (pp. 2–3). The cloth bag is for storage: the reference cards that are not given out go back to it at setup (p. 3), and so do the characters put away for the classic version (p. 14).

## Setup

Steps 1 to 5 are done once, and steps 2 to 4 are done again at the start of every round (p. 7).

1. Give each player a reference card and put the remaining reference cards back in the bag (p. 3).
2. Shuffle the 21 character cards into the deck and place it facedown within reach of all players, with the 13 favor tokens (p. 3).
3. Take the top card of the deck and set it aside facedown, without looking at it. At two players, take three more cards and set them aside faceup (p. 3).
4. Deal one card from the deck to each player as their starting hand (p. 3).
5. The player who most recently hand-wrote a letter takes the first turn (p. 3). At the start of a later round, the player who won the previous round takes the first turn instead, and when that round had several winners, one of them picked at random does (p. 7).

What changes with the number of players: the three cards set aside faceup, at two players only (p. 3), and the number of favor tokens that wins the game (p. 7). Counted from steps 3 and 4, the deck holds 15 cards after the deal at two players, 17 at three, 16 at four, 15 at five and 14 at six.

## How a game goes

A game is played over several rounds (p. 4). In a round the players take turns in clockwise order (p. 4), and a player who is out of the round is skipped (p. 5). Nothing is played at the same time; the only moment when everything happens together is the reveal of all the hands when the deck runs out (p. 6).

A turn has two steps, in this order (p. 4):

1. Draw the top card of the deck, which gives the player two cards in hand.
2. Choose one of the two cards and play it, resolving its effect. The played card stays faceup in the player's play area and the other card stays in the hand. The Countess can force the choice (p. 9).

A player holds one card between turns, and two from the draw to the card played (pp. 3–4); only the Chancellor makes a hand of three for a moment (p. 10).

The round ends in one of two cases (p. 6): after any turn, when the deck is empty, and immediately when only one player is left in the round. The winner of the round gains a favor token (p. 6), and the game ends when a player has enough favor tokens (p. 7). Otherwise a new round starts with steps 2 to 4 of the setup, the winner of the last round taking the first turn (p. 7).

## Rules

### The characters

The deck holds ten characters, valued 0 to 9, with the copies of each given below (p. 2). The value of a card is the number on it: the higher value wins a comparison, and the Spy, at 0, is the lowest (pp. 6, 12).

| Value | Character | Copies | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | Princess | 1 | Its player is out of the round if it is played or discarded, for any reason (p. 8) |
| 8 | Countess | 1 | No effect; it must be played when the other card in hand is the King or a Prince (p. 9) |
| 7 | King | 1 | Trade hands with another player (p. 9) |
| 6 | Chancellor | 2 | Draw two cards, keep one of the three, put the other two under the deck (p. 10) |
| 5 | Prince | 2 | Any player discards their hand and draws a new one (p. 10) |
| 4 | Handmaid | 2 | Until your next turn, the other players cannot choose you (p. 11) |
| 3 | Baron | 2 | Compare hands with another player; the lower value is out of the round (p. 12) |
| 2 | Priest | 2 | Look at another player's hand (p. 12) |
| 1 | Guard | 6 | Name a character other than the Guard; another player who holds it is out of the round (p. 12) |
| 0 | Spy | 2 | No effect; it can earn a favor token at the end of the round (p. 13) |

### Hands and play areas

The card that a player plays stays faceup in their play area (p. 4). A hand that is discarded, because its player is out of the round or because a Prince was played on them, is laid faceup (pp. 5, 10). The cards played and the cards discarded must always be visible to everyone, so that all can tell which cards have been played and which are left in the deck (p. 5).

### Choosing a player

A card that asks to choose another player can choose any player still in the round except the one who plays it and except a player protected by a Handmaid (pp. 5, 11). The Prince asks to choose any player and can also choose its own player (p. 10). A player who is out of the round cannot be chosen (p. 5).

When a player plays a card while every other player still in the round is protected by a Handmaid (p. 11):

- the Guard, the Priest, the Baron and the King are played with no effect;
- the Prince must choose its own player.

A card played with no effect is still played: it goes to the player's play area (pp. 4, 11).

### Out of the round

A player is out of the round when they play or discard the Princess (p. 8), when a Guard names the card they hold (p. 12) or when their card is the lower value in a Baron's comparison (p. 12). The player discards their hand faceup in front of them, without resolving any card effect (p. 5). Until the next round the player cannot be chosen for card effects and their turn is skipped; their reference card is flipped to the broken seal side as a reminder (p. 5). They take no part in the comparison at the end of the round, which is for the players still in the round (p. 6).

### The Princess (9)

A player who plays or discards the Princess, for any reason, is immediately out of the round (p. 8). A player whom a Prince makes discard the Princess is out of the round and does not draw a new hand (p. 10).

### The Countess (8)

The Countess has no effect when played or discarded (p. 9). A player who holds the Countess and, as the other card, the King or a Prince must play the Countess as the card of their turn (p. 9). With any other card the player can still choose to play the Countess (p. 9). The other card is not revealed when the Countess is played, so the others cannot know whether the play was forced (p. 9). The obligation does not apply to the cards drawn for another effect, such as the Chancellor's (p. 9).

### The King (7)

The player chooses another player, and the two trade hands (p. 9).

### The Chancellor (6)

The player draws two cards from the deck into their hand, which then holds three cards, keeps one of them and secretly places the other two facedown on the bottom of the deck, in any order, showing them to nobody (p. 10). With only one card in the deck, the player draws it and returns one card (p. 10). With no card left in the deck, the Chancellor is played with no effect (p. 10). The Countess's obligation does not apply to the three cards (p. 9). See the open points on the cards that go under the deck.

### The Prince (5)

The player chooses any player, themselves included (p. 10). The chosen player discards their hand faceup, without resolving its play effect, and draws a new hand (p. 10), a hand being one card as at the deal (p. 3). If the deck is empty, the chosen player draws the facedown card that was set aside at the start of the round (p. 10). If the card discarded is the Princess, the player is immediately out of the round and does not draw a new hand (p. 10).

### The Handmaid (4)

Until the start of the player's next turn, the other players cannot choose them for their card effects (p. 11). See Choosing a player for the case where every other player is protected.

### The Baron (3)

The player chooses another player, and the two secretly compare their hands (p. 12). The one with the lower-value card is out of the round; with equal values, neither is (p. 12).

### The Priest (2)

The player chooses another player and secretly looks at their hand, without showing it to anyone else (p. 12).

### The Guard (1)

The player chooses another player and names a character other than the Guard (p. 12). The chosen player announces whether they have that card in their hand, and if they do, they are out of the round (p. 12). Any of the nine other characters can be named, the Spy being a character card like the others (pp. 2, 12).

### The Spy (0)

The Spy has no effect when played or discarded (p. 13). Its only part in the game is the favor token that it can earn at the end of the round (see End and scoring). Only a Spy that has been played or discarded counts, a Spy that a Prince makes its owner discard included (pp. 10, 13): one still in a hand at the end of the round, revealed or not, has been neither (p. 13).

### Rule mistakes

The booklet names two rule mistakes: a player who is not honest when chosen for a Guard, and a player who does not play the Countess when required (p. 13). It gives no remedy for them (p. 13).

## End and scoring

A round ends in one of two ways (p. 6):

- After any turn, if the deck is empty: all the players still in the round reveal their hands and compare them. The highest value wins the round; with a tie, all the tied players win it (p. 6).
- When only one player is left in the round, the round ends immediately and that player wins it (p. 6).

Each winner of the round gains one favor token (p. 6).

The Spy's token: at the end of the round, a player who is the only player still in the round to have played or discarded a Spy gains one favor token (p. 13). It does not count as winning the round: the winner still gains their own token, even when it is the same player (p. 13). A player who has played or discarded two Spies, or one of each, still gains one token only (p. 13).

The game ends when a player has enough favor tokens to win, the number depending on the number of players (p. 7). Several players can win the game at the same time (p. 7).

| Players | Favor tokens to win |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | 6 |
| 3 | 5 |
| 4 | 4 |
| 5 | 3 |
| 6 | 3 |

If no player has enough favor tokens, a new round is played (p. 7). The players who have enough favor tokens win the game, and every other player loses; the booklet gives no places (p. 7). See the open point on players who reach the number with different counts.

## Who sees what

- Hands: a player sees their own hand only, and the hands of the others are hidden, as the Countess, the Priest and the Baron show (pp. 9, 12), except through the effects below.
- Priest: it shows the chosen player's hand to the player of the Priest only (p. 12).
- Baron: its comparison is seen by its two players only, and the player who loses discards their hand faceup, which shows it to everyone (pp. 5, 12).
- Guard: a hit makes the chosen player discard their hand faceup, which shows it to everyone; a miss tells everyone only that the named character is not in that hand (pp. 5, 12).
- King: after the trade, each of the two players holds, and so sees, the other's former card (p. 9).
- Prince: the target discards their hand faceup, shown to everyone, and draws a new card that only they see (p. 10).
- Chancellor: the player draws two cards and places two facedown under the deck, and nobody else sees them (p. 10).
- Countess: it is played without showing the other card (p. 9).
- Cards in view: the cards in the play areas and the cards discarded are always visible to everyone (pp. 4–5), and so are the favor tokens each player holds.
- Hidden cards: the deck is hidden, and so is the card set aside facedown at the start of the round, which only the player who draws it with a Prince on an empty deck sees (p. 10). At two players the three cards set aside faceup are visible to everyone (p. 3).
- End of a round: when the deck is empty after a turn, all the players still in the round reveal their hands (p. 6). The booklet asks for no reveal when the round ends with one player left (p. 6).
- Players out or protected: whether a player is out of the round, shown by the broken seal (p. 5), and whether a Handmaid protects them, the Handmaid being played faceup (pp. 4, 11), are known to everyone.

## Not in this spec

- The classic version, for 2 to 4 players: before setup, 1 Guard, 2 Chancellors and 2 Spies go back to the bag, which leaves 16 cards, and all other rules are unchanged (pp. 8, 14).
- The booklet names no other variant, no solo mode, no optional rule and no expansion.

## Open points

### Who takes the first turn of the first round

**Question.** The first turn of the first round goes to the player who most recently hand-wrote a letter (pp. 3, 16): how does the table pick that player?

**Reading.** A player is drawn at random among the seats when the game starts. Later rounds follow the booklet: the winner of the previous round, or one of the tied winners drawn at random.

**Basis.** The criterion cannot be known on a screen, and the booklet itself draws at random for the same choice among tied winners (p. 7); the usual rule of such games.

**Status.** validated 2026-11-18

### A Spy of a player who is out of the round

**Question.** The card says that the Spy earns a favor token if no one else plays or discards a Spy (p. 2), and the full rule says that it does for the only player still in the round who played or discarded one (p. 13): does a Spy that belongs to a player who is out of the round stop the others from earning the token?

**Reading.** No. Only the players still in the round at its end are looked at: a player who has played or discarded a Spy earns the token when no other player still in the round has. A Spy of a player who is out counts for nothing, for them and for the others.

**Basis.** The booklet says that the text of a card is a brief summary and that pp. 8–13 give the complete rules (p. 8), and p. 13 limits the count to the players still in the round.

**Status.** validated 2026-11-18

### The cards that the Chancellor puts under the deck

**Question.** The Princess knocks out a player who plays or discards her for any reason (p. 8), and a Spy counts when it is played or discarded (p. 13): is a card that the Chancellor places on the bottom of the deck, or returns when only one card is left in the deck, discarded? And where does the single card returned go?

**Reading.** No. The card is neither played nor discarded: a Princess put under the deck does not knock her owner out, and a Spy put there does not count for the Spy's token. When only one card is left in the deck, the card returned goes facedown into the deck, hidden from everyone, like the two cards of the normal effect.

**Basis.** The Chancellor's text says to place and to return and never says to discard (p. 10); the usual reading of such games.

**Status.** validated 2026-11-18

### The Chancellor with one card left in the deck

**Question.** The round ends when the deck is empty after a turn (p. 6). A player who plays the Chancellor with one card left in the deck draws it and returns one card (p. 10): is the deck empty after that turn?

**Reading.** No. The returned card is in the deck, so the deck holds one card after the turn and the round goes on; the next player draws it, and the round ends after that turn. With no card left in the deck, the Chancellor has no effect and the round ends after the turn.

**Basis.** A literal reading of the rule of the empty deck (p. 6) and of the Chancellor (p. 10), which do not mention each other.

**Status.** validated 2026-11-18

### What a new round keeps

**Question.** A new round repeats steps 2 to 4 of the setup (p. 7): what does it keep and what does it reset, the favor tokens apart? In particular, does the protection of a Handmaid played at the end of a round last into the next one?

**Reading.** Only the favor tokens carry over. All 21 character cards are gathered and shuffled again, so every hand, play area and set-aside card is reset, every player is back in the round, and no player is protected by a Handmaid.

**Basis.** The booklet says to shuffle the character cards and deal again (p. 7), says that a player is out of the round until the next round (p. 5), and gives the Handmaid's protection no end but its owner's next turn (p. 11); the usual rule of such games.

**Status.** validated 2026-11-18

### Several players reach the number of tokens together

**Question.** The game ends when a player has enough favor tokens, and several players can win it at once (p. 7). The tokens of a round come together at its end (pp. 6, 13), so two players can pass the number in the same round with different counts, for example when a tie gives a token to two players and one of them also earns the Spy's token: do they both win?

**Reading.** All the tokens of the round are gained first, then the game is checked once: every player who has at least the number of tokens wins and shares the win, whatever their count above it, and every other player loses.

**Basis.** The booklet says that several players can win at once and gives no tie-break (p. 7); the other reading, that only the players with the most tokens win, is not in it.

**Status.** validated 2026-11-18
