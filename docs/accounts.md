# Accounts

Sign-in with a password, or with Google, Discord and Twitch, through Supabase Auth, the pages of a signed-in visitor and the friends' panel (PLT-16, PLT-17, PLT-23). What exists, what is still a placeholder, and what has to be set up in Supabase before a visitor can sign in.

## What exists
- `@pbgo/accounts` (`packages/accounts`): `.` the pure part (`Account`, `accountOf`, `safeNext`, `PROVIDERS`, the figures of a profile: `statsOf`, `perGame`, `ordinal`, and the visitor's language: `PlatformLanguage`, `matchPlatformLanguage`, `resolveLanguage`, `planLanguage`), `./config` (the project: `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`, the two variables the games already read; without them the hub has no sign-in and says so), `./client` (`useAccount`, `signInWith`, `saveLanguage`, `useLanguageSync`), `./server` (`createAuthClient`, `getAccount`, `getHeldAccount`, `requireAccount`, `enabledProviders`), `./proxy` (`updateSession`), `./language` (the pure language part, alone) and `./set-language` (the setter, with no Supabase code in it), which the games' pages load too through core, and `./demo`. The app and core import it; it never imports core or a game
- An account on hold, waiting to be deleted (`Account.deletionAt`), is nobody to `getAccount`, `useAccount` and the proxy ("Deleting an account", below). An account is what the provider says of the visitor (`accountOf`): Google gives `full_name` and `avatar_url`, Discord `custom_claims.global_name` (the name they chose, not their login) and `avatar_url`, and Twitch its login in `full_name` (its display name is `nickname`, which is not read) and `avatar_url`; the nickname falls back to the start of the e-mail, then to "Player". Nothing is stored beside Supabase Auth's own `auth.users`: no `profiles` table and no nickname tag (PLT-13, PLT-39); the settings an account keeps are in its `user_metadata`, under keys of ours: the visitor's language ("The visitor's language", below), and the nickname and the preferences of the member's settings ("Settings", below)
- Session: Supabase Auth's cookies (`@supabase/ssr`), the same in the browser and on the server. A page that is for a signed-in visitor asks the Auth server (`getUser()`, never the cookie alone: anyone can write a cookie); `apps/web/src/proxy.ts` (Next 16's `proxy`, the former middleware) renews an expired session before the page renders, and only for `/me/*`, `/login` and `/signup`, so every public page and every game stays static. A visitor who is not signed in and asks for `/me/*` goes to `/login?next=<the page>` (the proxy, then `requireAccount` as a second check)
- Routes (`apps/web`): `/login` (`(hub)/login`: a username and a password (`signInWithPassword`: the first field takes an e-mail address, `emailOf`, since Supabase Auth knows a visitor by their address alone and a username has nowhere to be looked up until the profiles exist, PLT-39; a username is told to use the address), then, under "or continue with", the providers that the project has switched on (`enabledProviders`, below) as small buttons side by side, one column each, each with nothing on it but its mark (`ProviderButton`, `@pbgo/ui`), on the hub's backdrop; `?next=` is where to go back to, `?error=signin` a sign-in that did not work; a visitor already signed in is sent on), `/auth/callback` (the provider's way back: it exchanges the one-time `code` for a session and redirects to `next`), `/logout` (POST only: it ends the session and redirects to `/`; a form posts to it, never a link), `/auth/demo` (development only, below), and the pages of an account `/me` (the profile: figures, the games one by one, the last games), `/me/games` (in progress, then history), `/me/friends` (add a friend, requests, and the list where the side panel has no room) and `/me/settings/**` (the settings: "Settings", below). They are `noindex`, disallowed in `robots.txt`, and not in the sitemap
- `safeNext` is the one way back from a sign-in: a value anyone can write into a link is a path of this site (one slash, no backslash, no control character, not the sign-in's own way) or it is `/me`
- The navbar (`SiteHeader`, a client component on static pages) reads the session in the browser (`useAccount`): the server renders every page as if nobody were signed in, so the first paint is the same everywhere. Until the browser has read its session the two account buttons keep their place but are not drawn (`invisible`), so a signed-in visitor never sees them flash; then Sign in (to `/login?next=<this page>`) and Let's play (to the sign-up, `/signup?next=/games`: a visitor who is not signed in has no account yet, and the new account lands on the games), or `SiteSocial` (`apps/web`): one container on the right, the dock (`SocialDock`), that is a bar while it is closed and a real side panel while it is open, the same element growing and shrinking between the two. The bar has the games the visitor's friends are in (`FriendsBar`, three pills at most), then, behind a thin line, the visitor's picture, with a count of what waits for them on its corner (`AvatarCount`: messages not read, requests to answer), which opens the menu of the account (`AccountMenu`: their picture big, with the dot of their presence, their name and the word that says it, then Profile, My games, Friends, Settings, Sign out; under the bar while the dock is closed, and beside the open panel, on its left, as the Riot Client's does), and the button of the panel (`SocialPanelToggle`: `PanelRightOpenIcon`, then `PanelRightCloseIcon` once it is open). Open, the bar is the header of the panel, the way the Riot Client has it: the games fold away, the picture glides to the start of the bar and says who they are beside it (`SocialDockIdentity`: their name and their status), the button stays where it was, and the panel (`SocialPanel`, 20rem wide, down to the bottom of the window) is under it, in three tabs: the friends game by game (each game with its picture, its name and how many of them are in it, the ones in its lobby and the ones in a game mixed), the conversations, and the way to add a friend with the requests. The tile of the current tab glides to the one that is pressed and its pane comes in from that side. In the conversations what is new is white and what was read is grey. A press on a friend or a conversation opens the chat with them (`ChatWindow`, next to the dock, sliding in from the bottom, with a `···` menu: join or invite them, remove them, block them), where a message sent is kept for as long as the page is open
- The home of a signed-in member (PLT-119, `docs/routing.md`): at `/` the proxy (`apps/web/src/proxy.ts`) looks for a Supabase session cookie (`hasSessionCookie`); a visitor with none goes straight to the static public home, and one with a session has it renewed and is redirected to `/home` (a 307 that carries the renewed cookies and is never cached; a rewrite that showed the member home at `/` made the development server's hot reload reload the page for ever, so the address is `/home`, which the navbar marks as the home). `/home` is read per request, is `noindex` and `Disallow`ed, and sends a visitor who is not signed in to `/`. Its zones, from the top: the announcements (`announcement` of the platform's Sanity project, active between `startsAt` and `endsAt`, two at most, a message bar each; one that can be dismissed is closed for the page only, since nothing is kept on the device), the banners (the three newest `changelogEntry` that have an image, each leading to the page of the first game it is about that the catalog lists, else to `/about#whats-new`), the play zone, in two cards on one row, Find a game on two thirds of it, on a surface a step lighter (`--card-bar`, what is read first), and Create a table on the third beside it (three fifths and two fifths between `lg` and `xl`, where the third is too narrow for the settings of a table; stacked below `lg`, Find a game first), and the news (the four newest entries, with or without a picture): the announcements, the banners and the news are drawn only when the Sanity project holds something, and the others are always there: the play zone, the tables open on the member's games and their tournaments, each with its empty state (`home-empty-zones.tsx`), so that the shape of the home is there to see, as the public home's tables and tournaments are, and, between them, the member's games (`home-your-games.tsx`, PLT-13): two blocks side by side, Recent games, with the member's record over every game they finished ("14 wins in 24 games · 58% win rate") and their last three (`GameRecord`, the rows of `/me/games`), and Your ranks, a row for each game they rated with the game's picture, the points they have toward the next division and their rank (`RankBadge`), the highest first. They are the games that were recorded (`memberGames` of `apps/web/src/lib/member-games.ts`, from `@pbgo/core/server/standings` and `@pbgo/core/lib/member-games`), and a block with nothing to list keeps its empty state: "No game yet" until the member has finished a game, "No rank yet" until they have rated one. The development account has made-up games and two ranks. **Find a game** (`home-find-game.tsx`) is a tile for each game that can be played (`GamePick`: its picture, a tick when it is picked; all are picked to begin with), then the kind of table to look for, Ranked ("Counts for your rank. No helpers.") or Normal, two options to choose between, and a small button with a play icon, "Play ranked" or "Play normal" (`QueueButton`, `lg`), that starts the search: the platform's one queue (`lib/matchmaking.ts`: its games, its kind and since when, kept in session storage and shown in the social panel and on a game's "Play ranked" button when it is a ranked search that has that game), which counts on the button with a cross that stops it, and locks the card until it is stopped; nothing is found for the visitor yet. **Create a table** (`home-create-table.tsx`) has, in this order, a select of the games that can be played, the speed, the number of players (empty and disabled until a game is chosen) and, once a game is chosen, that game's expansions and helpers, which are the settings of the lobby on the game's page (`LobbySettings`, `LobbyPlayers`); its button opens a lobby with the game's own route, sends the options the engine keeps (`engineOptionsOf`) and takes the member to its page `/game/<slug>/<code>`, where they share the link; the number of players and the speed stay in the page until PLT-13's tables, as they do on the game's page. `getEditorial` (`@pbgo/catalog/server`) reads them, cached ten minutes like the catalog, and gives none when the project does not answer. The demo account of development gets made-up ones (`apps/web/src/lib/demo-editorial.ts`, the pictures those the games already have in Sanity) so that the page can be seen whole; a real account sees what the Studio holds, which is nothing today. The matching of players behind Find a game's button, the list of open tables and the tournaments behind their empty states are the next phases (PLT-21)
- The frame of the pages of an account (`AccountShell`): the page in a column that does not stretch with the window, between the navbar and the footer. The friends are not in it: they are the navbar's, on every page, and `/me/friends` lists them in full (`FriendsList`, the `FriendsPanel` of `@pbgo/ui`, with its search). The design docs draw the friends' bar, the social panel with the dock that holds it and the chat window (`docs/design/03-components/05-social/`)

## What is a placeholder
- A real account has no friends, no conversations and no requests: the pages and the panel say so with an empty state. The friends are PLT-23's; until they exist `apps/web/src/lib/account-data.ts` (the pages) and `apps/web/src/lib/use-social.ts` (the panel, in the browser) return nothing for a real account. Its games are not a placeholder since PLT-13: `/me` (the figures, the games one by one and the three latest), `/me/games` (History: the fifty latest, how each ended, the place, the rank points it gave or took and the others at the table; a game that is over has no table code, since its result outlives the table) and the home's Your games read the games that were recorded (`apps/web/src/lib/member-games.ts`: the figures are the totals of every game the member finished, `player_stats`, and not only of the games listed), and say so with an empty state until the member has finished one, or when the database cannot be read (the cause is in the log). The tables a member is at ("In progress" of `/me/games`) are still empty for a real account: a table is the browser's player cookie and not the account's until PLT-120. The buttons that need them (add a friend, invite, join, accept) are not drawn for a real account, and do nothing for the demo account
- The home's figures under the title are counts, not placeholders (`LiveStats` keeps `Stats` alive: the page is static and brings the figures it was built with, and the browser asks `GET /api/hub/figures` for fresh ones when it opens and every half minute while it is in view). **The players online** are, by the owner's definition (2026-11-07), the members who are connected and the guests who sit at a table (`online_player_count()`, migration `0005_online_players.sql`; kept half a minute). A member is connected for two minutes after their last beat (`PresenceBeat`, mounted once in the hub layout, tells `POST /api/hub/presence` about every 45 s while a tab is in view and the visitor is signed in: `member_presence`, `touch_member`) or while a table saw their seat in the last two minutes (`games.seen`; a seat knows its account through `games.member_ids`, which `seat_member` writes after the response when a member opens or joins a table: `resolveSeat`, `seatMember`), and is counted once however many tabs and tables they have. A guest is counted only at a table, once per game (a browser that plays two games has two ids that nothing links); a guest who only looks at the site is not counted. Tables that are over are left out. **The members** (`member_count()`: the users of Supabase Auth that are neither deleted nor anonymous; kept a minute) are of migration `0003_hub_figures.sql`. Both are read by `getHubFigures` of `apps/web/src/lib/hub-stats.ts` (`docs/supabase-and-env.md`). Where a figure cannot be counted (no Supabase, or the migration not applied yet: `pnpm setup-env` applies it) it is left out of the page and never made up; only local development, which has no Supabase, shows a made-up 128 and 12,480 so that the hero can be seen. The demo account of development beats nothing: it is in no database. The figure for the games is the size of the catalog, coming-soon games included.
- What works is what needs no friend to exist: the panel's tabs and search, "Invite a friend" (it copies the address of the sign-in), and the chat window, whose messages stay for as long as the page is open: messages have no backend yet (PLT-23). Joining, inviting, sending and answering a request do nothing but for the demo account, and are not drawn for a real one
- Sitting down at a table (PLT-106): the hub's page of a game opens, joins and starts tables with the game's routes, and a signed-in visitor sits down as their account. The create and join routes ask Supabase Auth who is signed in (`getAccount`, `resolveSeat` in core) and take the account's name (20 characters at most, "Player" when it has none) and its picture on the server, never from the request; the picture is kept with the player (`players[].avatarUrl` of the `games` row), drawn by the lobby's seats and by the seat labels of the board, and never given to the engine. The visitor is still the per-game player cookie, not the account: the same account on two devices is two players, and nothing yet links a table to an account (PLT-13's `table_players.user_id`). A guest types a nickname once per game (`<slug>:profile` in the browser's storage, the key the games' own screens used), and sits down under it.
- Not there yet: the picture's upload and the sound settings (PLT-16), the nickname with a tag (PLT-39), a guest's past games attached on sign-up (PLT-38), the signed-in state in the board of a game (PLT-17), the admin through the account (PLT-41). The Privacy Policy and the Terms of Use (`/legal/privacy`, `/legal/terms`) are drafts for the owner, or a lawyer, to validate (`docs/legal.md`); the three legal pages change with the accounts: the Cookie Policy (`/legal/cookies`) lists the sign-in's cookies (`sb-…`) and must change when PLT-13 replaces the per-game player cookie with a Supabase session, and PLT-13 (profiles, ratings, tables, results) and PLT-23 (friends, conversations) add personal data, so the Privacy Policy changes before they ship

## Setting it up (once for each Supabase project: `pbgo-preview`, then production)
The code needs no new variable. One app per provider is enough: it holds the callback of both projects (`https://ckpbuuxcdsayonyahlkk.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback` for `pbgo-preview`, `https://igxsfcezbhidnlknuudf.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback` for `pbgo`, the production one) and its ID and secret go into both. Do `pbgo-preview` first and try it on `http://localhost:3100/login`, whose `.env.local` points at it. A visitor can only sign in once the providers are enabled in the project (Authentication › Providers) and its redirect URLs allow the hub. The sign-in page draws only the providers that the project it talks to has switched on: `enabledProviders` reads the public settings of the project's Auth (`GET <project>/auth/v1/settings`, the `external` map, with the publishable key), keeps them five minutes in production and not at all in development, and the buttons come in the order of `PROVIDERS`. Switching a provider on in the dashboard is what shows its button, with no change in the code; when the settings cannot be read no provider is drawn and the password form stays. A provider whose app is not open to everybody yet shows its button to everybody all the same, so switch it on in the production project only once it is. The password needs the Email provider (on by default; keep "Confirm email" on): the sign-up page makes the e-mail accounts (item 6 below and "Sign-up with an e-mail address"), and there is no password reset yet. The others:
1. **Google**: in Google Cloud Console, APIs & Services › Credentials, create an OAuth client ID of type Web application; its authorised redirect URI is `https://<project ref>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`. Put its client ID and secret in the Google provider of the project and enable it
2. **Twitch**: at dev.twitch.tv/console (the Twitch account needs two-factor authentication on), register an application: a unique name, as OAuth Redirect URLs the callback `https://<project ref>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback` of each project (the console takes several), a category and the client type Confidential. Under Manage, put its Client ID and a New Secret (shown once) in the Twitch provider of the project and enable it. Supabase asks Twitch for the scope `user:read:email` (`internal/api/provider/twitch.go` of supabase/auth) and fills `full_name` and `name` with the login, `nickname` with the display name and `avatar_url` with the profile picture
3. **Discord**: in the Discord Developer Portal, a new application, OAuth2, add the same redirect `https://<project ref>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`; put its client ID and secret in the Discord provider and enable it
4. **URL configuration** (Authentication › URL Configuration): in production the Site URL `https://playboardgamesonline.app` and the redirect URL `https://playboardgamesonline.app/**`; in `pbgo-preview` the Site URL `http://localhost:3100` and the redirect URLs `http://localhost:3100/**` and `https://*-playboardgamesonline.vercel.app/**`, the pattern of the Vercel preview addresses (`<project>-git-<branch>-<team>.vercel.app`: a `*` does not cross a dot, so the team's slug ends the pattern). They end in `/**` and not in `/auth/callback` because the way back carries a `?next=`, which a pattern with an exact path would not match
5. `pnpm setup-env` fills `apps/web/.env.local` with the preview project's URL and publishable key (they are the same two variables the games use). A checkout without them shows the sign-in page with its buttons off
6. **The sign-up with an e-mail address** (PLT-117; Authentication › Providers › Email, then Emails): "Confirm email" **on** (`mailer_autoconfirm` false: without it nobody is asked for a code and the page's second step has nothing to do); a minimum password length of 8, which is what the form asks (`PASSWORD_MIN`), and no required characters (the form sets no composition rule; "Prevent use of leaked passwords" needs the Pro plan, and the form already tells a `pwned` refusal); "Email OTP Length" 6 (`CODE_LENGTH` is 6: change both together) and an expiry of 3600 seconds or less (the code and the link of one e-mail share it). Then **Emails › Templates › Confirm signup**, the one template this flow uses: write the code and a link that carries the token hash, and never `{{ .ConfirmationURL }}`, which goes through Supabase's own `/auth/v1/verify` with a one-time `code` for the browser that signed up and fails on another device:
   ```html
   <h2>Confirm your e-mail address</h2>
   <p>Your code: <strong>{{ .Token }}</strong></p>
   <p>Or follow this link: <a href="{{ .SiteURL }}/auth/confirm?token_hash={{ .TokenHash }}&type=email&next={{ .RedirectTo }}">confirm my address</a></p>
   <p>If you did not create an account, ignore this e-mail.</p>
   ```
   `&next={{ .RedirectTo }}` is optional (`confirmNext`): it brings the visitor back to the page they came from, because the sign-up gives Supabase Auth that page, whole, as its redirect URL (`emailRedirectTo` is this site's origin and the path, e.g. `https://playboardgamesonline.app/game/courtisans`), which step 4's `/**` allows; without it, or when the address is not allowed and Supabase writes the Site URL instead, the link lands on `/me` or `/`. A `&` in that page's query may cut such a `next` short, if Supabase's template does not escape it: the visitor still lands on a page of the site. **Authentication › Emails › SMTP Settings** (the owner chose Resend on 2026-11-07): until a custom SMTP is set the built-in sender only delivers to the addresses of the members of the Supabase organization (`email_address_not_authorized`, told to the visitor as "We could not send the e-mail") and sends 2 e-mails an hour (`over_email_send_rate_limit`); a custom SMTP lifts that to 30 an hour, which Authentication › Rate Limits can change. Resend's side: add the domain `mail.playboardgamesonline.app` in its dashboard, in the region Ireland (`eu-west-1`; it cannot be changed without deleting the domain and adding it again) and as a subdomain, as it recommends, so that its DNS records stay clear of the root's mail forwarding (the domain's DNS is at Spaceship, not at Vercel), put the records it shows there (at the time of writing a TXT for DKIM at `resend._domainkey.mail` and a CNAME at `send.mail`), wait for "Verified", then make one API key per Supabase project (sending access only, limited to that domain: `supabase-preview` and `supabase-production`; Resend shows a key once). In each project: sender `no-reply@mail.playboardgamesonline.app` named `Play Board Games Online`, host `smtp.resend.com`, port `465`, username `resend`, the project's own key as the password, and the minimum interval between e-mails at its default of 60 seconds. Resend's Free plan allows 3,000 e-mails a month and 100 a day. **Resend is a processor of the confirmation e-mails: it is in `docs/legal.md` (Recipients) and in the Privacy Policy ("Who receives your data" and "Outside the European Union")**. An address gets one confirmation e-mail a minute (the cooldown of the page, `RESEND_SECONDS`, is that minute). No CAPTCHA is set (below). Supabase refuses the addresses of example domains (`email_address_invalid`): try with a real domain

7. **The e-mails of the site's own code** (PLT-36: the link that confirms the deletion of an account): a Resend API key, apart from the one in the SMTP settings of Supabase Auth. In Resend's dashboard, API Keys › Create API key, name it for the app, permission *Sending access*, domain `mail.playboardgamesonline.app` (shown once). Like the Supabase keys there are two, one for production and one for previews and local development (one key alone serves everywhere): put them in `~/.config/pbgo/secrets.env` as `RESEND_API_KEY=re_…` (production) and `RESEND_PREVIEW_API_KEY=re_…` (preview), and run `pnpm setup-env`, which checks their shape, pushes the first to the Vercel production target and the second to the preview and development targets, and writes the second to `apps/web/.env.local` as `RESEND_API_KEY`; it applies from the next deployment. Without it a development server prints the e-mail in its terminal, and a deployment cannot send it: the Delete account row says it is not available. The sender (`MAIL_FROM` of `apps/web/src/site.ts`) is `no-reply@mail.playboardgamesonline.app`, on the domain Resend verified for step 6

Left out on purpose: **Apple** (a US$99 yearly developer account and a secret to renew every six months), **Facebook** (on 2026-11-06 Meta's app review asked for a written reason, a screen recording and API test calls for the `user_age_range` permission, on top of the checklist to go Live: the owner left it out) and **Steam** (it signs in with OpenID 2.0, and Supabase's custom providers take OAuth 2 and OpenID Connect only). Adding one back is an entry in `PROVIDERS` and `PROVIDER_NAMES`, its mark in `brands.tsx`, its tile in `provider-button.tsx`, a colour token when its brand has one (and the lists in `platform.test.ts` and in the colours page of the design docs), and the lines of the Privacy Policy and the Terms that name the providers (`docs/legal.md`).

## Sign-up with an e-mail address (PLT-117)
`/signup` (`(hub)/signup/page.tsx`, `SignupCard` and `SignupCode` of `apps/web/src/components`) makes an account from an e-mail address and a password, in the frame of `/login`: the same card, the same row of providers (`ProviderRow`, since a provider's sign-in makes an account too) and a link each way ("New here? Create an account" on `/login`, "Already have an account? Sign in" on `/signup`, `?next=` kept). A visitor who is signed in already goes on to `next`, like on `/login`. The page is `noindex`, `Disallow: /signup` in `robots.txt`, not in the sitemap, and in the proxy's matcher (it reads the session); `safeNext` never goes back to `/signup`, and `/signup` is a reserved slug.
- **The form** (step 1): an e-mail address, a password (`PasswordInput`: a show / hide button) and a display name that may be left empty, then a line under the button, "By creating an account you accept the Terms of Use and the Privacy Policy. You must be 13 or older." (no checkbox). `checkSignup` (`packages/accounts/src/signup.ts`) judges it before anything leaves, field by field: an address as `emailOf` takes one, a password of 8 characters at least and 72 bytes at most (bcrypt reads no more) with no rule about what it is made of, a display name of 20 characters at most (the length a table keeps). It then calls `signUpWithPassword` (`./client`): `supabase.auth.signUp({ email, password, options: { data, emailRedirectTo, captchaToken } })`. Nobody is signed in by it: Supabase Auth creates the user unconfirmed (`auth.users`) and e-mails the template above.
- **The code** (step 2, `SignupCode`): the card turns to "Check your e-mail" and asks for the six digits (`InputOTP`; the sixth goes by itself, the button is for whoever prefers to press it). `verifySignupCode` calls `verifyOtp({ email, token, type: "email" })` (the type Supabase's documentation gives for "Verify Signup One-Time Password (OTP)", as for the link; `signup` is the older name of the same confirmation); it confirms the address and signs the visitor in, the session lands in the cookies and a whole page load takes them on to `safeNext(next)`. A wrong code is cleared and the field is ready again. "Send a new code" (`resendSignupCode`: `resend({ type: "signup", … })`) waits a minute (`useCountdown`, which counts to a time and not by ticks, so a tab in the background is right when the visitor comes back), starts again when an e-mail went out or when Supabase says to wait, and "Use another e-mail address" goes back to the form with the fields kept. The address printed on the screen is `ph-mask` in recordings.
- **The link** (`app/auth/confirm/route.ts`, `GET`): `/auth/confirm?token_hash=…&type=email&next=…`, which spends the token **on the server**. `confirmRequestOf` takes only `type=email` and `type=signup` (a recovery link, an invitation, a magic link or an address change would sign a visitor in on a page that does not know why) and a value that has the shape of a hashed token; `confirmNext` takes `next` as a path of this site or as the whole address of this site that `{{ .RedirectTo }}` writes (any other address is `/me`); `confirmEmail` calls `verifyOtp({ token_hash, type })` with the cookie-bound server client, which sets the session on the redirect. The answer is always a redirect with `Cache-Control: no-store`: to `next` when it worked, else to `/signup?error=confirm&next=…`, where the card says the link expired or was already used (and offers to sign in or to create the account again for a new code). **The token never reaches a page**: it is not in the address of anything rendered, and nothing of ours logs the request. Two limits: the platform's own request log (Vercel) records the address of any request, the token included, but it is spent by then and expires within the hour; and a mail client or a scanner that opens the link before the visitor does spends it (Supabase's own guide says so), which confirms the address and leaves the visitor with a link that "expired": the card says to sign in, and the code in the same e-mail is the way around it.
- **The nickname**: the display name goes into `user_metadata.full_name` (`DISPLAY_NAME_KEY`), the key Google and Twitch fill and `accountOf` reads for the nickname, so nothing else needs to know how the account was made (`signup.test.ts` keeps the two together); without a name it is the start of the address, as for any account. The account keeps no other setting at sign-up.
- **An address with an account reveals nothing**: a repeated sign-up is answered like a new one (Supabase Auth's obfuscated user, no error, no e-mail; `user_already_exists` is read the same way if the project ever auto-confirms), so the page goes to the code step all the same, and the step says "Nothing yet? Look in your spam folder. If you already have an account, sign in instead." (a sentence everybody reads). A new code is asked the same way and answers the same. What cannot be hidden is that no e-mail arrives for such an address. A password sign-in of an account that was never confirmed says so (`email_not_confirmed`, only to whoever knows the password).
- **The errors, in plain words** (`failureKind`, `signupOutcome`, `codeMessage`, `resendOutcome`; Supabase's own messages are never shown): a weak password or an address Supabase refuses is put on its field; `email_address_not_authorized` (the built-in sender), `unexpected_failure`, a timeout or a 5xx is "We could not send the e-mail. Try again in a few minutes."; `over_email_send_rate_limit` and `over_request_rate_limit` "Too many attempts. Wait a few minutes and try again." (for a new code: "Wait a minute before asking for another code."); `signup_disabled` and `email_provider_disabled` "Creating an account is not available right now."; `captcha_failed`; the network being down; a code that Supabase calls `otp_expired`, which it says of a wrong code too, "That code is wrong or has expired."; anything else "Sign-up did not work. Try again."
- **CAPTCHA, later**: `signUpWithPassword` and `resendSignupCode` already take a `captchaToken`, which goes to Supabase Auth as `options.captchaToken`, and `captcha_failed` has its sentence. Turning CAPTCHA on in the project needs the widget in `SignupCard` (and `SignupCode`) and nothing else. None is drawn.
- **Tested**: the pure parts and the calls to Supabase with a stub client (`signup.test.ts`, `confirm.test.ts`, `client.test.ts`), the two `@pbgo/ui` fields by their markup (`password-input.test.ts`, `input-otp.test.ts`), and, by hand, the pages and the route against a stand-in of Supabase Auth's endpoints that is not in the repo, then, on 2026-11-07, one real sign-up on `pbgo-preview` through Resend, the owner's own: the project's auth logs show the request answered in 1.3 s with no SMTP error, the code verified 12 seconds later (`type: "email"`, `login_method: otp`) and a password sign-in after it. **Not verified**: the link of the e-mail (`/auth/confirm`) on a real e-mail, how the hosted project renders `{{ .RedirectTo }}` in the template, where the e-mail lands in a mailbox other than the owner's (inbox or spam), and a sign-up on the production project, which the owner set on 2026-11-07 (SMTP, template, password length and code length) and has not tried yet.

## Settings (PLT-111)
`/me/settings` (`(hub)/me/settings/layout.tsx`: the sections in a column at the left, `SettingsNav`, a row that scrolls above the page on a phone) is the fourth tab of the account (`AccountNav`) and the "Settings" item of the account menu. It opens on its first section; each section is a page of its own, per request and `noindex` like the rest of `/me`, and a link can lead straight to one.
- **Profile** (`/me/settings/profile`): the nickname (`NicknameRow`, a window: `checkNickname`, 20 characters like a table's), the picture (the provider's, or the initials of the nickname; there is no upload) and the language (`LanguageRow`, moved here from `/me`). The nickname is kept under `user_metadata.pbgo_nickname`, which `accountOf` reads first: a provider writes `full_name` again at every sign-in, so a nickname kept there would not last. The tables a member sits at carry it (`profileOf` takes `Account.nickname`); the ones they already sit at keep the name they were opened with.
- **Account** (`/me/settings/account`): the e-mail address (`EmailRow`: `updateUser({ email })` sends a link to the new address, and to the current one when the project asks both to confirm; the address changes once they are opened, and the link comes back to this page through `/auth/callback`), the password (`PasswordRow`: `updateUser({ password })` with the sign-up's rules, asked twice; a first password for an account that came in with a provider), the linked services (`LinkedServices`: `linkIdentity` sends the member to the provider and back here, `unlinkIdentity` takes it off; the last way in cannot be unlinked, so its button is off), the devices (`DevicesRow`: the browser the member is on, named by `describeDevice`, and `signOut({ scope: "others" })` for the others) and the deletion of the account (`DeleteAccountRow`: a window that e-mails a confirmation link to the address of the account, "Deleting an account", below; an account with no address that mail can reach, or a site where no e-mail can leave, is told to write to the contact address instead). A list of every session, with where and when, needs the project's `auth.sessions`, which the browser cannot read: it waits for a function of the database.
- **Billing** (`/me/settings/billing`): the plan (the free one: every member has it), "Adjust plan" to `/pricing`, the payment method, the invoices and "Cancel plan", drawn with their empty state and disabled where there is nothing to act on. There is no payment provider, and no paid plan can be taken (`PAID_PLANS_NOTICE`, the line of the pricing page, shown at the top).
- **Notifications** (`/me/settings/notifications`): browser notifications only, as PLT-24 decided: whether the browser allows them (`BrowserNotificationsRow`: the Notification API's permission, asked when the member presses Allow; a refusal is undone in the browser's own settings) and two choices, a game that waits for the member and a friend's invitation (`PreferenceRow`). The choices are kept; showing the notifications is PLT-24.
- **Privacy** (`/me/settings/privacy`): hide the game history (kept; what shows it to the other players is the profile pages, PLT-111; it does not stop the games from being written down, `docs/supabase-and-env.md`, Results: the totals and the rank are made of them), the blocked players (an empty list: no block exists yet), the Privacy Policy and a copy of the member's data, asked for by e-mail as the policy says.
- **What is kept, and where**: Supabase Auth's `user_metadata`, under keys of ours that a provider never writes (`NICKNAME_KEY`, `PREFERENCE_KEYS` in `preferences.ts`: `pbgo_nickname`, `pbgo_notify_turn`, `pbgo_notify_invites`, `pbgo_hide_history`), one key each so that `updateUser`'s merge, key by key, never loses another. Each has one writer in `./client` (`saveNickname`, `savePreference`) and one reader (`accountOf`: `Account.nickname`, `Account.preferences`), so that they move to a `profiles` table, with the language, when it exists (PLT-13). `Account.linked` is the user's identities (`user.identities`, which the server's `getUser()` holds), for the page of the account. A value that is not what it should be reads as the default: the member can write their own metadata, so none of it is ever a permission. It is all in the Privacy Policy ("When you sign in") and the inventory (`docs/legal.md`).
- **One change, one outcome**: each change of the account is one call (`change` in `./client`) that answers `done`, `no-session` or `failed` with a sentence, since the project may be unset, nobody may be signed in, and the call may be refused or throw. A refusal is told in the words of `settingsMessage` (`settings.ts`), never Supabase's, and `email_exists` is told like an address that cannot be used, so that a session cannot be used to ask which addresses have an account. The demo account of development holds no session: its changes are shown as done and nothing is asked of anyone (`changing`).
- **To set up in each Supabase project** (`pbgo-preview`, then production): Authentication › Settings › **"Allow manual linking"** on (it covers linking and unlinking; without it the member is told linking is not available). The e-mail templates need no change for the address change: its link goes through Supabase's own verify and `/auth/callback`, which works in the browser that asked, as a provider's sign-in does (a link opened on another device does not sign it in; the sign-up's `token_hash` template is not extended to this). "Secure password change", if the project turns it on, asks for a recent sign-in: the member is told to sign in again.
- **Tested**: the pure parts and the calls to Supabase with a stub client (`settings.test.ts`, `preferences.test.ts`, `account.test.ts`, `client.test.ts`), and the pages, the dialogs and the switches on the demo account in a real browser. **Not verified against a real account**: the e-mail change, the password change, linking, unlinking and signing out the other devices need a test account in `pbgo-preview` made by the owner in the Supabase dashboard (PLT-111); nobody has run them against Supabase Auth yet.

## Deleting an account (PLT-36)
A member deletes their account in `/me/settings/account` (the **Delete account** row), and the proof that it is them is the mailbox of the account: a link in an e-mail, with a button. Nothing else is asked, no password and no second sign-in. The account is then kept `ACCOUNT_DELETION_GRACE_DAYS` (30) days ON HOLD, and the nightly clean-up deletes it. The owner's decisions (2026-11-09): the confirmation is a link by e-mail with a button, an account with no e-mail address cannot make the request, and the data is kept 30 days (the 30 are his first figure, to confirm; they are `ACCOUNT_DELETION_GRACE_DAYS` of `@pbgo/core/lib/retention`, which the Privacy Policy prints). Every change of state is a function of migration `0011_account_deletion.sql` that decides in one transaction and that `supabase/tests/account_deletion.sql` runs end to end; `server/account-deletion.ts` of core only hashes the token, keeps the order of the steps and reads the answers.
- **The way through**: (1) the window of the row ("Send me the link") is `POST /api/hub/account/deletion` with `{ intent: "request" }`: the route takes the address from the session (never from the request), makes a token (32 random bytes, 43 characters of base64url), asks `request_account_deletion` to keep **the hash of the token** and when it stops working (`ACCOUNT_DELETION_LINK_MINUTES`, an hour; the token itself is only in the e-mail) and mails the link. An address is mailed once a minute (`wait`); a new link replaces the old one, which stops working; an e-mail that could not leave takes its link back (`cancel_account_deletion`), so that the next try is not held up. (2) The link, `/auth/delete-account?u=<account>&t=<token>` (`deletionLink`), opens a page (`(hub)/auth/delete-account`) on any device, signed in or not. **The page only looks** (`peek_account_deletion` changes nothing: mail clients and scanners open links, and Supabase's own guide says a scanner spends a link), and asks one more time. (3) Its button is `{ intent: "confirm", user, token }`: `confirm_account_deletion` spends the token (once), puts the account ON HOLD until `ACCOUNT_DELETION_GRACE_DAYS` days from now (the day is fixed here, so a later change of the constant moves no promise already made), writes the mark and deletes every session of the account; the card says the day. (4) Signed in again during the hold, the member finds, instead of the sign-in form, **the offer to keep the account or to delete it now** (`/login`, `DeletionNotice`): *Keep my account* is `{ intent: "keep" }` (`cancel_account_deletion` takes the row and the mark away and the route renews the session, so that the cookies are those of an account that is not on hold), *Delete it now* is `{ intent: "now" }` after one more question (`erase_account`, the feedback first), and *Sign out* is the site's own. (5) When the day has passed, the nightly clean-up (`purgeExpired`, `/api/cron/purge`) deletes it, up to `ERASE_MAX_ACCOUNTS` (20) a night, the oldest first.
- **The three states** of `account_deletions` (a row for each account that asked, deleted with the account): no row, the account is in use; a row with `token_hash` and `token_expires_at`, AWAITING the confirmation of a link; a row with `scheduled_for`, ON HOLD (a check keeps it one or the other). The nightly clean-up deletes the rows whose link stopped working `ACCOUNT_DELETION_LINK_RETENTION_DAYS` day ago (`purge_deletion_links`) and never one that is on hold.
- **The mark**: `confirm_account_deletion` also writes the day into the account's `app_metadata`, `pbgo_deletion_at` (`DELETION_KEY`), and `cancel_account_deletion` clears it, in the same transaction as the row, so the two cannot disagree. `app_metadata` can be written with the secret key alone (a member can write their `user_metadata`, never this), and it comes with the user wherever the user already is: the server's `getUser()`, the browser's session, the claims of the token that the proxy reads. That is why the guard below costs no query.
- **On hold means nobody**: `accountOf` reads the mark into `Account.deletionAt` (`deletionAtOf`: an ISO date, anything else reads as none), and then `getAccount()` returns null for such an account, `useAccount()` says signed out, and the proxy's `signedIn` is false (`isOnHold`). So everything that asks "who is signed in", the seats of a table (`resolveSeat`), the presence beat, the pages of `/me` and the home, treats the account as a visitor: it sits at no new table, is not counted among the players online, keeps no new preference (`saveLanguage` refuses it), and code written later cannot forget the check. One thing the hold does not reach: a game that ends at a table the member already sat at before the hold is still recorded under the account, since the table's `member_ids` holds it; the account goes at the end of the hold with what it holds. Only `getHeldAccount()` returns it, to the sign-in page (which shows the offer, whatever `next` says) and to the deletion route. The claims of a token are as old as the token, so when they say "on hold" the proxy asks the Auth server (`getUser()`, only then): a member who has just kept their account is not sent away until the token is renewed.
- **No address, no request**: `hasMailbox(email)` is true for an address of the right shape whose domain is not `.invalid`, the top-level domain reserved so that it can never receive mail, which is where a provider that gives no address (Steam) puts its stand-in. For such an account, and when `mailAvailable()` is false (production with no `RESEND_API_KEY`), the window says it cannot be done from the site and offers a message to the contact address, as the Privacy Policy says, because the right to have data erased is not ours to refuse.
- **The e-mail**: sent from our servers through Resend's API (`core/server/mail.ts`, `RESEND_API_KEY`; it is the other way in next to the SMTP settings of Supabase Auth, "Setting it up", 7), from `MAIL_FROM`, replies to the contact address. The words are `composeDeletionMail` of core (tested: the link, the numbers of the Privacy Policy, the escaping of a name), the sender and the name are the site's (`apps/web/src/lib/deletion-mail.ts`). It names no one but the member in its greeting. The link points to the site the member is on (a preview deployment's accounts are its own). On a development server with no key the e-mail is **printed in the terminal** (that is how the flow is tried without sending anything); nothing is ever printed in production, where the log holds the status Resend answered or the kind of error, never an address or a word of the message.
- **What the request guards against**: `request`, `keep` and `now` act on the strength of the session's cookies, so they refuse a request whose `Origin` is another site (403 `CROSS_ORIGIN`); `confirm` needs none, the token is the proof. The route is rate limited per IP (`accountDeletion`, ten an hour). The token is a key: 256 bits, kept as its SHA-256, compared by the database, spent by the first confirmation. The address of the page holds it, so the page is `noindex`, has `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, is out of the analytics (`isExcludedPath`: a page view would carry the address to PostHog) and is in the `Disallow` of `/auth/`; the host's request log still records the address of the request (Vercel), but a link works an hour and once.
- **What is deleted** (`erase_account`, in one transaction, after the feedback): the lines of Supabase Auth's audit log about the account (by its id or its address, when the project writes them to the database), then the account itself, which cascades to its identities, sessions, presence, totals, rank points and ratings (with the name and picture copied there), scrubs the tables it sat at (`scrub_member_from_tables`, whatever their status: the seat becomes "Deleted player" with no picture, and its `member_ids` entry goes; it is a trigger on `auth.users`, so a deletion in the dashboard does the same) and erases the name and the picture of its seats in the games played (`erase_member_from_results`, migration 0006). Before that, **the feedback sent with the address of the account** goes with its screenshots (`removeFeedbackOf`: the address alone, whatever its case, wildcards escaped; once the account is gone nothing links that feedback to it, so a failure keeps the account for the next night). A provider's identity (Google, Discord, a Steam number) and the e-mail address are free again. **What stays**: the games played as "Deleted player" (a result names no one); the counts of PostHog, which carry the player id of one game on one device and never the account (`docs/legal.md`, Findings 9: they cannot be found by account, and they go with their retention); Resend's record of the e-mails (30 days); the host's logs.
- **The demo account of development** is in no database: its window is shown the same steps with nothing asked of anyone, and its link (`/auth/delete-account?u=demo&t=demo`) opens a page whose button sends it to the demo account **on hold** (`/auth/demo?held=1`), whose `/login` shows the offer; *Keep* and *Delete it now* send it back to the demo routes. A build answers 404 to all of it.
- **To do after the merge, by the owner**: `pnpm setup-env` (applies migration `0011` to the preview project, then production, and pushes `RESEND_API_KEY` once it is in the secrets file), then look at Vercel › Cron Jobs and call `/api/cron/purge?dry=1`, whose report has `accounts` and `links`. In each Supabase project, Authentication › Audit Logs: "Disable writing auth audit logs to project database" (it is on in `pbgo-preview`, whose audit table is empty; the function deletes the lines about an account all the same).
- **Tested**: the pure parts (`deletion.test.ts`), the route with the database, the mail and the session faked (`routes/api/hub/account/deletion/route.test.ts`), the mail (`mail.test.ts`, `deletion-mail.test.ts`), the server module with the functions it calls checked against the migration's text (`account-deletion.test.ts`), the nightly job against an in-memory database and bucket (`retention.test.ts`: the order feedback then account, a failure keeps the account, the limit, a dry run), and the SQL end to end in a block that rolls back (`supabase/tests/account_deletion.sql`, run on `pbgo-preview` with the migration in the block). **Not verified live** (nobody makes or deletes an account on a hosted service for a test): a real e-mail through Resend, the real Auth server's answers (that a token whose session was deleted is refused, read in `internal/api/auth.go` of supabase/auth, and that a `refreshSession()` after the mark is cleared brings a token without it), and the link's journey in a real mailbox. The owner tries it once on `pbgo-preview` with a throwaway account, after `pnpm setup-env --no-push`.

## The visitor's language (PLT-103)
The language of a visitor is kept on their account, offered first from their browser, and changes when they change it. Two sets of languages, on purpose: a visitor chooses among the platform's **twelve** (`PLATFORM_LANGUAGES` of `@pbgo/studio-kit/constants`, English first and the default and the fallback of every text, then fr, es, de, zh (Simplified Chinese), ja, ru, ar, bn, hi, pt, ko; `PlatformLanguage` in `@pbgo/accounts`), and a site renders the languages it has: the games **four** (`LANGUAGES`, `Locale` of core) until their Studios move to the same system. The four are among the twelve (a type in `packages/core/lib/i18n.ts` stops compiling otherwise), and a visitor whose language a site lacks reads English there: `localeFor` of core, which `getLocale` applies to the cookie. The hub's own texts are English for everyone (PLT-32), so the language changes nothing on the hub yet but the Language row itself; the games and, to come, the catalog follow it.
- **Order**: the account's language, else the cookie `pbgo-locale`, else the browser's, else English (`resolveLanguage`). `matchPlatformLanguage` takes `navigator.languages` or the header `Accept-Language` (weights read, `q=0` and `*` left out) and gives the first language whose first subtag the platform has: `fr-CA` is French, `pt-BR` Portuguese, every `zh-*` (Traditional included: there is no other Chinese yet) Chinese (Simplified), and none is English. The games' own rule without a cookie: the first of their four languages that the browser asks for, else English (`fromAcceptLanguage`, `DEFAULT_LOCALE`: a browser in a language the games lack, and a request with no `Accept-Language` at all, as a crawler sends, get English)
- **Kept**: on the device, the cookie `pbgo-locale` (a year, `SameSite=Lax`, any of the twelve), which is what the server reads, so every page is rendered in the visitor's language at the same address and nothing becomes dynamic; on the account, `user_metadata.pbgo_locale` of Supabase Auth, written with `supabase.auth.updateUser({ data })` by `saveLanguage` and read by `languageOf` (it lands in `Account.language`). Those two functions are the only ones to know the key, so that the language moves to a `profiles.locale` column when the profiles exist (PLT-13); there is no migration. A value in the key that is not one of the twelve (the user can write their own `user_metadata`) reads as none. The key is namespaced because a provider fills keys of its own in the same object (below)
- **One setter**: `setLanguage` (`./set-language`) is used by the games' pickers (`useChooseLocale` of core: the toolbar's menu and `LanguageSelect`) and by the Language row of `/me/settings/profile` (`LanguageRow`, the twelve in their own language). A visitor who holds a session cookie (`sb-…-auth-token`) has the account written first (`./client`, loaded on the spot: a guest never loads the auth client) and the cookie once the account holds it, so the two never disagree; when the account cannot be written nothing changes (`failed`: the cookie would be undone by the account at the next sync). A guest and the demo account keep the cookie alone. The callers draw the page again, set `<html lang>` (games) and report `language_changed` (analytics, now with the twelve)
- **The sync**, account to device: `useLanguageSync`, mounted once by `LanguageSync` in the hub's layout, runs each time the browser reads the session (a page load, a sign-in, the renewal of the token, a change made in another tab). The account wins: its language is written in the cookie and the page is drawn again if it was in another. An account that keeps none yet (its first sign-in, or an account older than this) takes the language in use, the cookie's else the browser's, once; after that a sync never writes the account, only a choice does (`planLanguage`, `applyLanguage`, `syncLanguage`, all tested). The session in the browser holds the account's metadata as of its last renewal, so a change made on another device is looked for once per page load of the hub: `renewIfLanguageChanged` asks the Auth server for the user (one `GET /user`) and, only when its language is not the one the session holds, renews the session, whose new metadata `useAccount` hands to the sync. A device also meets an account at its sign-in, before any page: `/auth/callback` sets the cookie from the account on its redirect and `signInWithPassword` writes it before the page loads, so the first page of a new device, a game's perhaps, is already in it. Nothing for a guest and for the demo account (`isDemoAccount`), and nothing while a change is on its way (`isChangingLanguage`)
- **Limits**: a language chosen on another device reaches the hub at its next full page load (one request to the Auth server, a renewal of the session when the language differs), and only a page of the hub looks: a game's page follows the cookie, so a visitor who goes straight to a game on a device that last held another language reads that one until they open the hub. Apart from that request the sync only reads the session the browser holds and writes a cookie; the server never reads the account for the language
- **Why not the proxy** (looked at, it does not fit): it runs for `/me/*` and `/login` only, and the pages that need the cookie before their first render are the games' (the hub speaks English); matching them would put a function in front of every static page and every game, and a session read in front of all of them, against the rule that public pages and games stay static and untouched by the accounts. Worse, the claims it reads are the account's metadata **as it was when the token was issued**: `updateUser` does not issue a new token (`_updateUser` of `auth-js` replaces the session's user, not its access token, and the server puts `user.UserMetaData` in the JWT at issue, `internal/tokens/service.go`), so for up to an hour the claims hold the old language, and rewriting the cookie from them would undo a choice made a minute earlier. `getUser()` is fresh but a network call to Supabase on every page. Nothing may read the language from the claims
- **What a provider's sign-in does to `user_metadata`** (read in the source of `supabase/auth`, branch `master`, on 2026-11-02, and in the docs; no provider is enabled in either project yet, so none of it was run). Proven by the code: `PUT /user` with `data` (`updateUser`) goes through `UpdateUserMetaData`, which sets each top-level key it is given and deletes only the keys given as null, so the other keys stay; an OAuth sign-in, whether the identity exists, a second provider is linked to the account by its address or the account is new (`createAccountFromExternalIdentity`, `internal/api/external.go`), merges the provider's claims into `raw_user_meta_data` with the same function, so the keys the provider sends are set again each time and every other key is left; the providers build those claims from a fixed struct (`Claims`, with `locale` among its fields) plus the free map `custom_claims`, none of which can be named `pbgo_locale`; a password sign-in and the renewal of a token never touch the metadata (only `admin.go`, `user.go`, `external.go` and `mail.go` call `UpdateUserMetaData`). Google, Twitch and Discord do not fill `locale` at all in that code (LinkedIn does), so the namespace is a safeguard for the day one of them does, not a collision seen today. The docs only say that `user_metadata` is carried in the JWT and that end users can edit it (hence nothing here may be a permission: the value is checked against the twelve). **Not proven**: that the hosted Supabase runs this very code (it is `master`, not the deployed build); any real sign-in with a provider. To check once a provider is enabled on `pbgo-preview`: sign in with it, choose a language in `/me`, sign out, sign in with the same provider again, and look at the user's raw metadata in the dashboard: `pbgo_locale` must still be there. Tested with mocks: `saveLanguage` sends exactly `{ data: { pbgo_locale } }` and nothing else (`client.test.ts`), the sync and the setter (`language-sync.test.ts`, `set-language.test.ts`), the matching and the precedence (`language.test.ts`)
- **English by default** (the owner's decision of 2026-11-02, done in PLT-103): `DEFAULT_LOCALE` is `en`, and a text reads the language asked, then English, then `[missing: <key>]` or nothing, never French (`docs/sanity-and-languages.md`). What the Cookie Policy says of `pbgo-locale` follows this section (`/legal/cookies`)

## Rules
- A page that shows an account is rendered per request (it reads cookies): keep that to `/login`, `/signup` and `/me/*`. The navbar and every other page of the hub ask the browser, never the server
- Never trust what the browser holds: the server asks the Auth server, and a redirect target goes through `safeNext`
- The visitor's language: one setter (`setLanguage`), one writer of the account (`saveLanguage`), one reader (`languageOf`); the cookie is what the server reads and the account never is; the language is never read from the token's claims; a value from a cookie or from `user_metadata` is checked against the twelve (`isPlatformLanguage`) before it is used
- Platform UI only: the pages use `@pbgo/ui` components and tokens, and a new visual component goes into `@pbgo/ui` first, with its section in the docs of the design system (`docs/design/03-components/`)
- What an account stores, what a provider hands over and what a table copies from an account are in the Privacy Policy (`/legal/privacy`, "When you sign in"): change it with the code, and its inventory with it (`docs/legal.md`)
- A sign-up never says which addresses have an account (a repeated sign-up goes through the same steps) and tells what went wrong in its own words, never Supabase's; the token of a confirmation link is spent on the server, is in the address of no page, and nothing of ours logs it (`/auth/confirm`, "Sign-up with an e-mail address")
