# Accounts

Sign-in with a password, or with Google, Discord and Steam (the first two through Supabase Auth, Steam through the hub's own routes; Twitch is a connection: a member can link it to their account from the settings, and it signs nobody in; Steam can be linked from the settings too), the pages of a signed-in visitor and the friends' panel (PLT-16, PLT-17, PLT-23). What exists, what is still a placeholder, and what has to be set up in Supabase before a visitor can sign in.

## What exists
- `@pbgo/accounts` (`packages/accounts`): `.` the pure part (`Account`, `accountOf`, `safeNext`, `PROVIDERS`, `SIGN_IN_PROVIDERS`, `EXTERNAL_PROVIDERS`, the OpenID 2.0 part of the sign-in with Steam in `steam.ts`, the member of a provider that Supabase does not know in `external.ts` (`externalSignIn`), the figures of a profile: `statsOf`, `perGame`, `ordinal`, and the visitor's language: `PlatformLanguage`, `matchPlatformLanguage`, `resolveLanguage`, `planLanguage`), `./config` (the project: `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`, the two variables the games already read; without them the hub has no sign-in and says so), `./client` (`useAccount`, `signInWith`, `saveLanguage`, `useLanguageSync`), `./server` (`createAuthClient`, `getAccount`, `requireAccount`, `clearSessionCookies`, `enabledProviders`, `signInProviders`, `externalProvidersOn`, and `createAdminClient` and `externalDeps` for Steam's sign-in), `./proxy` (`updateSession`), `./language` (the pure language part, alone) and `./set-language` (the setter, with no Supabase code in it), which the games' pages load too through core, `./display` (the site's motion and transparency, as the member chose them: their cookies, the script of the head of every page and the sync, with no Supabase code in it) and `./demo`. The app and core import it; it never imports core or a game
- An account is what the provider says of the visitor (`accountOf`): Google gives `full_name` and `avatar_url`, Discord `custom_claims.global_name` (the name they chose, not their login) and `avatar_url`, and Twitch its login in `full_name` (its display name is `nickname`, which is not read) and `avatar_url`, and Steam, which sends no address, its `personaname` and `avatarfull` through the Web API that the hub asks ("Sign-in with Steam", below), written under the same two keys; the name (`Account.nickname`, which the screens call the username) falls back to the start of the e-mail, then to "Player". Nothing is stored beside Supabase Auth's own `auth.users`: no `profiles` table and no nickname tag (PLT-13, PLT-39); the settings an account keeps are in its `user_metadata`, under keys of ours: the visitor's language ("The visitor's language", below), and the nickname, the preferences and the country of the member's settings ("Settings", below); the date of birth is not among them, the member can write their own `user_metadata`: it is declared once to the server and kept apart ("The age check", below)
- Session: Supabase Auth's cookies (`@supabase/ssr`), the same in the browser and on the server. A page that is for a signed-in visitor asks the Auth server (`getUser()`, never the cookie alone: anyone can write a cookie); `apps/web/src/proxy.ts` (Next 16's `proxy`, the former middleware) renews an expired session before the page renders, and only for `/me/*`, `/login` and `/signup`, so every public page and every game stays static. A visitor who is not signed in and asks for `/me/*` goes to `/login?next=<the page>` (the proxy, then `requireAccount` as a second check). A member who has set up multi-factor authentication is signed in only once their session has passed the second step (`getSessionState`, "Multi-factor authentication", below)
- Routes (`apps/web`): `/login` (`(hub)/login`: a username and a password (`signInWithPassword`: the first field takes an e-mail address, `emailOf`, since Supabase Auth knows a visitor by their address alone and a username has nowhere to be looked up until the profiles exist, PLT-39; a username is told to use the address), then, under "or continue with", the providers that sign in (`signInProviders`, below: Google and Discord when the project has switched them on, Steam when the server is set up for it) as small buttons side by side, one column each, each with nothing on it but its mark (`ProviderButton`, `@pbgo/ui`), in a card at the middle of a page of its own (`AuthShell`: no navbar, the logo alone at the top left, the brand's big letters behind the card, `AuthBackdrop`, where the owner's art will go, and a quiet foot with the legal pages, `AuthFooter`); `?next=` is where to go back to, `?error=signin` a sign-in that did not work; a visitor already signed in is sent on, and one whose account asks for a second step gets the card of the code in its place), `/auth/callback` (the provider's way back: it exchanges the one-time `code` for a session and redirects to `next`), `/auth/steam` and `/auth/steam/callback` (Steam's way there and back, "Sign-in with Steam", below), `/logout` (POST only: it ends the session and redirects to `/`; a form posts to it, never a link), `/auth/demo` (development only, below), and the pages of an account `/me` (the profile: figures, the games one by one, the last games), `/me/games` (in progress, then history), `/me/friends` (add a friend, requests, and the list where the side panel has no room) and `/me/settings/**` (the settings: "Settings", below). They are `noindex`, disallowed in `robots.txt`, and not in the sitemap
- `safeNext` is the one way back from a sign-in: a value anyone can write into a link is a path of this site (one slash, no backslash, no control character, not the sign-in's own way) or it is `/me`
- The navbar (`SiteHeader`, a client component on static pages) reads the session in the browser (`useAccount`): the server renders every page as if nobody were signed in, so the first paint is the same everywhere. Until the browser has read its session the two account buttons keep their place but are not drawn (`invisible`), so a signed-in visitor never sees them flash; then Sign in (to `/login?next=<this page>`) and Let's play (to the sign-up, `/signup?next=/games`: a visitor who is not signed in has no account yet, and the new account lands on the games), or `SiteSocial` (`apps/web`): one container on the right, the dock (`SocialDock`), that is a bar while it is closed and a real side panel while it is open, the same element growing and shrinking between the two. The bar has the games the visitor's friends are in (`FriendsBar`, three pills at most), then, behind a thin line, the visitor's picture, with a count of what waits for them on its corner (`AvatarCount`: messages not read, requests to answer), which opens the menu of the account (`AccountMenu`: their picture big, with the dot of their presence, their name and the word that says it, then Profile, My games, Friends, Settings, Sign out; under the bar while the dock is closed, and beside the open panel, on its left, as the Riot Client's does), and the button of the panel (`SocialPanelToggle`: `PanelRightOpenIcon`, then `PanelRightCloseIcon` once it is open). Open, the bar is the header of the panel, the way the Riot Client has it: the games fold away, the picture glides to the start of the bar and says who they are beside it (`SocialDockIdentity`: their name and their status), the button stays where it was, and the panel (`SocialPanel`, 20rem wide, down to the bottom of the window) is under it, in three tabs: the friends game by game (each game with its picture, its name and how many of them are in it, the ones in its lobby and the ones in a game mixed), the conversations, and the way to add a friend with the requests. The tile of the current tab glides to the one that is pressed and its pane comes in from that side. In the conversations what is new is white and what was read is grey. A press on a friend or a conversation opens the chat with them (`ChatWindow`, next to the dock, sliding in from the bottom, with a `···` menu: join or invite them, remove them, block them), where a message sent is kept for as long as the page is open
- The home of a signed-in member (PLT-119, `docs/routing.md`): at `/` the proxy (`apps/web/src/proxy.ts`) looks for a Supabase session cookie (`hasSessionCookie`); a visitor with none goes straight to the static public home, and one with a session has it renewed and confirmed with the Auth server (`updateSession(request, { confirm: true })`: a session that the server no longer knows has its cookies cleared and sees the public home) and is redirected to `/home` (a 307 that carries the renewed cookies and is never cached; a rewrite that showed the member home at `/` made the development server's hot reload reload the page for ever, so the address is `/home`, which the navbar marks as the home). `/home` is read per request, is `noindex` and `Disallow`ed, and sends a visitor who is not signed in to `/`, and one who has signed in with a first factor and not passed the second step of their account to `/login?next=/home`. Its zones, from the top: the announcements (`announcement` of the platform's Sanity project, active between `startsAt` and `endsAt`, two at most, a message bar each; one that can be dismissed is closed for the page only, since nothing is kept on the device), the banners (the three newest `changelogEntry` that have an image, each leading to the page of the first game it is about that the catalog lists, else to `/about#whats-new`), the play zone, in two cards on one row, Find a game on two thirds of it, on a surface a step lighter (`--card-bar`, what is read first), and Create a table on the third beside it (three fifths and two fifths between `lg` and `xl`, where the third is too narrow for the settings of a table; stacked below `lg`, Find a game first), and the news (the four newest entries, with or without a picture): the announcements, the banners and the news are drawn only when the Sanity project holds something, and the others are always there: the play zone, the tables open on the member's games and their tournaments, each with its empty state (`home-empty-zones.tsx`), so that the shape of the home is there to see, as the public home's tables and tournaments are, and, between them, the member's games (`home-your-games.tsx`, PLT-13): two blocks side by side, Recent games, with the member's record over every game they finished ("14 wins in 24 games · 58% win rate") and their last three (`GameRecord`, the rows of `/me/games`), and Your ranks, a row for each game they rated with the game's picture, the points they have toward the next division and their rank (`RankBadge`), the highest first. They are the games that were recorded (`memberGames` of `apps/web/src/lib/member-games.ts`, from `@pbgo/core/server/standings` and `@pbgo/core/lib/member-games`), and a block with nothing to list keeps its empty state: "No game yet" until the member has finished a game, "No rank yet" until they have rated one. The development account has made-up games and two ranks. **Find a game** (`home-find-game.tsx`) is a tile for each game that can be played (`GamePick`: its picture, a tick when it is picked; all are picked to begin with), then the kind of table to look for, Ranked ("Counts for your rank. No helpers.") or Normal, two options to choose between, and a small button with a play icon, "Play ranked" or "Play normal" (`QueueButton`, `lg`), that starts the search: the platform's one queue (`lib/matchmaking.ts`: its games, its kind and since when, kept in session storage and shown in the social panel and on a game's "Play ranked" button when it is a ranked search that has that game), which counts on the button with a cross that stops it, and locks the card until it is stopped; nothing is found for the visitor yet. **Create a table** (`home-create-table.tsx`) has, in this order, a select of the games that can be played, the speed, the number of players (empty and disabled until a game is chosen) and, once a game is chosen, that game's expansions and helpers, which are the settings of the lobby on the game's page (`LobbySettings`, `LobbyPlayers`); its button opens a lobby with the game's own route, sends the options the engine keeps (`engineOptionsOf`) and takes the member to its page `/game/<slug>/<code>`, where they share the link; the number of players and the speed stay in the page until PLT-13's tables, as they do on the game's page. `getEditorial` (`@pbgo/catalog/server`) reads them, cached ten minutes like the catalog, and gives none when the project does not answer. The demo account of development gets made-up ones (`apps/web/src/lib/demo-editorial.ts`, the pictures those the games already have in Sanity) so that the page can be seen whole; a real account sees what the Studio holds, which is nothing today. The matching of players behind Find a game's button, the list of open tables and the tournaments behind their empty states are the next phases (PLT-21)
- The frame of the pages of an account (`AccountShell`): the page in a column that does not stretch with the window, between the navbar and the footer. The pages of the profile (`/me`, `/me/games`, `/me/friends`: `me/(profile)/layout.tsx`) draw it with the header of the visitor, their picture and their name, and no tabs: the profile is a page, and the settings are a window ("Settings", below). The friends are not in it: they are the navbar's, on every page, and `/me/friends` lists them in full (`FriendsList`, the `FriendsPanel` of `@pbgo/ui`, with its search). The design docs draw the friends' bar, the social panel with the dock that holds it and the chat window (`docs/design/03-components/05-social/`)

## What is a placeholder
- A real account has no friends, no conversations and no requests: the pages and the panel say so with an empty state. The friends are PLT-23's; until they exist `apps/web/src/lib/account-data.ts` (the pages) and `apps/web/src/lib/use-social.ts` (the panel, in the browser) return nothing for a real account. Its games are not a placeholder since PLT-13: `/me` (the figures, the games one by one and the three latest), `/me/games` (History: the fifty latest, how each ended, the place, the rank points it gave or took and the others at the table; a game that is over has no table code, since its result outlives the table) and the home's Your games read the games that were recorded (`apps/web/src/lib/member-games.ts`: the figures are the totals of every game the member finished, `player_stats`, and not only of the games listed), and say so with an empty state until the member has finished one, or when the database cannot be read (the cause is in the log). The tables a member is at ("In progress" of `/me/games`) are still empty for a real account: a table is the browser's player cookie and not the account's until PLT-120. The buttons that need them (add a friend, invite, join, accept) are not drawn for a real account, and do nothing for the demo account
- The home's figures under the title are counts, not placeholders (`LiveStats` keeps `Stats` alive: the page is static and brings the figures it was built with, and the browser asks `GET /api/hub/figures` for fresh ones when it opens and every half minute while it is in view). **The players online** are, by the owner's definition (2026-11-07), the members who are connected and the guests who sit at a table (`online_player_count()`, migration `0005_online_players.sql`; kept half a minute). A member is connected for two minutes after their last beat (`PresenceBeat`, mounted once in the hub layout, tells `POST /api/hub/presence` about every 45 s while a tab is in view and the visitor is signed in: `member_presence`, `touch_member`) or while a table saw their seat in the last two minutes (`games.seen`; a seat knows its account through `games.member_ids`, which `seat_member` writes after the response when a member opens or joins a table: `resolveSeat`, `seatMember`), and is counted once however many tabs and tables they have. A guest is counted only at a table, once per game (a browser that plays two games has two ids that nothing links); a guest who only looks at the site is not counted. Tables that are over are left out. **The members** (`member_count()`: the users of Supabase Auth that are neither deleted nor anonymous; kept a minute) are of migration `0003_hub_figures.sql`. Both are read by `getHubFigures` of `apps/web/src/lib/hub-stats.ts` (`docs/supabase-and-env.md`). Where a figure cannot be counted (no Supabase, or the migration not applied yet: `pnpm setup-env` applies it) it is left out of the page and never made up; only local development, which has no Supabase, shows a made-up 128 and 12,480 so that the hero can be seen. The demo account of development beats nothing: it is in no database. The figure for the games is the size of the catalog, coming-soon games included.
- What works is what needs no friend to exist: the panel's tabs and search, "Invite a friend" (it copies the address of the sign-in), and the chat window, whose messages stay for as long as the page is open: messages have no backend yet (PLT-23). Joining, inviting, sending and answering a request do nothing but for the demo account, and are not drawn for a real one
- Sitting down at a table (PLT-106): the hub's page of a game opens, joins and starts tables with the game's routes, and a signed-in visitor sits down as their account. The create and join routes ask Supabase Auth who is signed in (`getAccount`, `resolveSeat` in core) and take the account's name (20 characters at most, "Player" when it has none) and its picture on the server, never from the request; the picture is kept with the player (`players[].avatarUrl` of the `games` row), drawn by the lobby's seats and by the seat labels of the board, and never given to the engine. The visitor is still the per-game player cookie, not the account: the same account on two devices is two players, and nothing yet links a table to an account (PLT-13's `table_players.user_id`). A guest types a nickname once per game (`<slug>:profile` in the browser's storage, the key the games' own screens used), and sits down under it.
- Not there yet: the picture's upload and the sound settings (PLT-16), the nickname with a tag (PLT-39), a guest's past games attached on sign-up (PLT-38), the signed-in state in the board of a game (PLT-17), the admin through the account (PLT-41). The Privacy Policy and the Terms of Use (`/legal/privacy`, `/legal/terms`) are drafts for the owner, or a lawyer, to validate (`docs/legal.md`); the three legal pages change with the accounts: the Cookie Policy (`/legal/cookies`) lists the sign-in's cookies (`sb-…`) and must change when PLT-13 replaces the per-game player cookie with a Supabase session, and PLT-13 (profiles, ratings, tables, results) and PLT-23 (friends, conversations) add personal data, so the Privacy Policy changes before they ship

## Setting it up (once for each Supabase project: `pbgo-preview`, then production)
The code needs no new variable for Google and Discord (Steam has one, in "Sign-in with Steam", below). One app per provider is enough: it holds the callback of both projects (`https://ckpbuuxcdsayonyahlkk.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback` for `pbgo-preview`, `https://igxsfcezbhidnlknuudf.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback` for `pbgo`, the production one) and its ID and secret go into both. Do `pbgo-preview` first and try it on `http://localhost:3100/login`, whose `.env.local` points at it. A visitor can only sign in once the providers are enabled in the project (Authentication › Providers) and its redirect URLs allow the hub. The sign-in page draws only the providers that the project it talks to has switched on: `enabledProviders` reads the public settings of the project's Auth (`GET <project>/auth/v1/settings`, the `external` map, with the publishable key), keeps them five minutes in production and not at all in development, and `signInProviders` keeps of them the ones that sign in (`SIGN_IN_PROVIDERS`, in the order of the buttons: Google, Discord; Twitch is switched on in the project so that a member can link it, and has no button, the owner's choice of 2026-11-09), then adds Steam when the server is set up for it. Switching a provider on in the dashboard is what shows its button, with no change in the code; when the settings cannot be read the project's providers are not drawn (Steam, which is the server's own, is) and the password form stays. A provider whose app is not open to everybody yet shows its button to everybody all the same, so switch it on in the production project only once it is. The password needs the Email provider (on by default; keep "Confirm email" on): the sign-up page makes the e-mail accounts (item 6 below and "Sign-up with an e-mail address"), and there is no password reset yet. The others:
1. **Google**: in Google Cloud Console, APIs & Services › Credentials, create an OAuth client ID of type Web application; its authorised redirect URI is `https://<project ref>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`. Put its client ID and secret in the Google provider of the project and enable it
2. **Twitch** (a connection, not a way in: it signs nobody in since 2026-11-09, so the sign-in page has no Twitch button, and the settings list it under Connections, apart from the sign-in methods, where a member can link it to their account for the public profile to come): at dev.twitch.tv/console (the Twitch account needs two-factor authentication on), register an application: a unique name, as OAuth Redirect URLs the callback `https://<project ref>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback` of each project (the console takes several), a category and the client type Confidential. Under Manage, put its Client ID and a New Secret (shown once) in the Twitch provider of the project and enable it. Supabase asks Twitch for the scope `user:read:email` (`internal/api/provider/twitch.go` of supabase/auth) and fills `full_name` and `name` with the login, `nickname` with the display name and `avatar_url` with the profile picture
3. **Discord**: in the Discord Developer Portal, a new application, OAuth2, add the same redirect `https://<project ref>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`; put its client ID and secret in the Discord provider and enable it
4. **URL configuration** (Authentication › URL Configuration): in production the Site URL `https://playboardgamesonline.app` and the redirect URL `https://playboardgamesonline.app/**`; in `pbgo-preview` the Site URL `http://localhost:3100` and the redirect URLs `http://localhost:3100/**` and `https://*-playboardgamesonline.vercel.app/**`, the pattern of the Vercel preview addresses (`<project>-git-<branch>-<team>.vercel.app`: a `*` does not cross a dot, so the team's slug ends the pattern). They end in `/**` and not in `/auth/callback` because the way back carries a `?next=`, which a pattern with an exact path would not match
5. `pnpm setup-env` fills `apps/web/.env.local` with the preview project's URL and publishable key (they are the same two variables the games use). A checkout without them shows the sign-in page with its buttons off
6. **The sign-up with an e-mail address** (PLT-117; Authentication › Providers › Email, then Emails): "Confirm email" **on** (`mailer_autoconfirm` false: without it nobody is asked for a code and the page's second step has nothing to do); a minimum password length of 8, which is what the form asks (`PASSWORD_MIN`), and no required characters (the form sets no composition rule; "Prevent use of leaked passwords" needs the Pro plan, and the form already tells a `pwned` refusal); "Email OTP Length" 6 (`CODE_LENGTH` is 6: change both together) and an expiry of 3600 seconds or less (the code and the link of one e-mail share it). Then **Emails › Templates › Confirm signup**, the one template this flow uses: write the code and a link that carries the token hash, and never `{{ .ConfirmationURL }}`, which goes through Supabase's own `/auth/v1/verify` with a one-time `code` for the browser that signed up and fails on another device:
   ```html
   <h2>Confirm your e-mail address</h2>
   <p>Your code: <strong>{{ .Token }}</strong></p>
   <p>Or follow this link: <a href="{{ .SiteURL }}/auth/confirm?token_hash={{ .TokenHash }}&type=email&next={{ .RedirectTo }}">confirm my address</a></p>
   <p>If you did not create an account, ignore this e-mail.</p>
   ```
   `&next={{ .RedirectTo }}` is optional (`confirmNext`): it brings the visitor back to the page they came from, because the sign-up gives Supabase Auth that page, whole, as its redirect URL (`emailRedirectTo` is this site's origin and the path, e.g. `https://playboardgamesonline.app/game/courtisans`), which step 4's `/**` allows; without it, or when the address is not allowed and Supabase writes the Site URL instead, the link lands on `/me` or `/`. A `&` in that page's query may cut such a `next` short, if Supabase's template does not escape it: the visitor still lands on a page of the site. **Authentication › Emails › SMTP Settings** (the owner chose Resend on 2026-11-07): until a custom SMTP is set the built-in sender only delivers to the addresses of the members of the Supabase organization (`email_address_not_authorized`, told to the visitor as "We could not send the e-mail") and sends 2 e-mails an hour (`over_email_send_rate_limit`); a custom SMTP lifts that to 30 an hour, which Authentication › Rate Limits can change. Resend's side: add the domain `mail.playboardgamesonline.app` in its dashboard, in the region Ireland (`eu-west-1`; it cannot be changed without deleting the domain and adding it again) and as a subdomain, as it recommends, so that its DNS records stay clear of the root's mail forwarding (the domain's DNS is at Spaceship, not at Vercel), put the records it shows there (at the time of writing a TXT for DKIM at `resend._domainkey.mail` and a CNAME at `send.mail`), wait for "Verified", then make one API key per Supabase project (sending access only, limited to that domain: `supabase-preview` and `supabase-production`; Resend shows a key once). In each project: sender `no-reply@mail.playboardgamesonline.app` named `Play Board Games Online`, host `smtp.resend.com`, port `465`, username `resend`, the project's own key as the password, and the minimum interval between e-mails at its default of 60 seconds. Resend's Free plan allows 3,000 e-mails a month and 100 a day. **Resend is a processor of the confirmation e-mails: it is in `docs/legal.md` (Recipients) and in the Privacy Policy ("Who receives your data" and "Outside the European Union")**. An address gets one confirmation e-mail a minute (the cooldown of the page, `RESEND_SECONDS`, is that minute). No CAPTCHA is set (below). Supabase refuses the addresses of example domains (`email_address_invalid`): try with a real domain
7. **Steam** is not a Supabase provider: nothing to switch on in the dashboard. It needs a Web API key and migration `0010` ("Sign-in with Steam", below)
8. **Multi-factor authentication** (PLT-129): app authenticators (TOTP) on for enrolment and for verification (Authentication › Sign In / Providers, the multi-factor part: Supabase's default). Never switch verification off while members hold a factor. "Multi-factor authentication", below
9. **The e-mails of the site's own code** (PLT-36: the link that reopens an account its member deleted): a Resend API key, apart from the one in the SMTP settings of Supabase Auth. In Resend's dashboard, API Keys › Create API key, name it for the app, permission *Sending access*, domain `mail.playboardgamesonline.app` (shown once). Like the Supabase keys there are two, one for production and one for previews and local development (one key alone serves everywhere): put them in `~/.config/pbgo/secrets.env` as `RESEND_API_KEY=re_…` (production) and `RESEND_PREVIEW_API_KEY=re_…` (preview), and run `pnpm setup-env`, which checks their shape, pushes the first to the Vercel production target and the second to the preview and development targets, and writes the second to `apps/web/.env.local` as `RESEND_API_KEY`; it applies from the next deployment. Without it a development server prints the e-mail in its terminal, and a deployment cannot send it: the Delete account row says it is not available. The sender (`MAIL_FROM` of `apps/web/src/site.ts`) is `no-reply@mail.playboardgamesonline.app`, on the domain Resend verified for step 6

Left out on purpose: **Apple** (a US$99 yearly developer account and a secret to renew every six months), **Facebook** (on 2026-11-06 Meta's app review asked for a written reason, a screen recording and API test calls for the `user_age_range` permission, on top of the checklist to go Live: the owner left it out) and **Riot Games** (Riot Sign On is open only to the products that Riot has approved, not to any site that wants a sign-in; asked on 2026-11-09, not possible). Epic Games is planned and not built ("Sign-in with Steam", below, for the backend it will reuse). Adding a provider that Supabase handles is an entry in `PROVIDERS`, `SIGN_IN_PROVIDERS` and `PROVIDER_NAMES`, its mark in `brands.tsx`, its tile in `provider-button.tsx`, a colour token when its brand has one (and the lists in `platform.test.ts` and in the colours page of the design docs), and the lines of the Privacy Policy and the Terms that name the providers (`docs/legal.md`).

## Sign-in with Steam
Steam speaks OpenID 2.0, not OAuth and not OpenID Connect, which is all that Supabase's custom providers take, so the hub does the dance itself and Supabase only holds the member. Steam gives a number (the SteamID64, 17 digits) and no e-mail address. The backend is made to serve Epic Games next (`ExternalProvider`; the table allows `epic`, nothing else is built for it).
- **The way there** (`app/auth/steam/route.ts`, `GET`): the Steam button (`signInWith("steam", next)` in `./client`) does not call Supabase, it navigates to `/auth/steam?next=…`. The route refuses (back to `/login?error=signin`) when the server is not set up for Steam, makes a **state** (128 random bits, `newState`), puts it in the cookie `pbgo-steam-state` (`httpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax` so that the way back from Steam carries it, `secure` on https, path `/auth/steam`, ten minutes) and sends the visitor to `https://steamcommunity.com/openid/login` (`checkid_setup`, `identifier_select`: Steam asks which account) with `openid.return_to` = `<origin>/auth/steam/callback?next=…&state=…` and `openid.realm` = the site's origin. The state is in the cookie **and** in the address that Steam signs.
- **The way back** (`app/auth/steam/callback/route.ts`, `GET`) believes nothing it is given until each of these has passed, in this order: the rate limit (`externalSignIn`, 20 a minute per IP); the state of the address is the cookie's (`sameSecret`, a constant-time comparison), which is what stops a link that someone else started from signing the visitor in as them (login CSRF); `checkAssertion` (`packages/accounts/src/steam.ts`): the namespace is OpenID 2.0, the mode `id_res`, the endpoint Steam's, `openid.return_to` is exactly this callback with this `next` and this state, the claimed identifier has the one shape `https://steamcommunity.com/openid/id/<17 digits>` and is the identity, the signature (`openid.sig`) covers `op_endpoint`, `claimed_id`, `identity`, `return_to`, `response_nonce` and `assoc_handle`, and the response nonce is at most five minutes from now; then `steamConfirms`: Steam itself is asked (`check_authentication`, the same fields posted back to its endpoint, five seconds, no redirect followed) whether the signature is its own, and only an answer in OpenID's key-value form with the namespace and one `is_valid:true` counts (Steam answers a forged assertion with `ns:http://specs.openid.net/auth/2.0` and `is_valid:false`: seen on 2026-11-09). Only then is the SteamID64 taken for who the visitor is.
- **The profile**: the name and the picture are asked of Steam's Web API (`ISteamUser/GetPlayerSummaries/v0002`, `fetchSteamProfile`, with `STEAM_WEB_API_KEY`; the address holds the key and is never logged, the answer is not cached). The name is cleaned (control characters and the bidirectional overrides are taken out: a NUL would be refused by the database, an override would turn the names around it at a table) and cut at 40 characters; the picture is kept only if it is an `https` address on `steamstatic.com` or `akamaihd.net`. When Steam does not answer, the sign-in goes on: a new member gets the name "Steam player" and no picture, and a member who exists is left exactly as they are (a failed call is not a profile, and Supabase Auth deletes a metadata key that is set to null, so a picture is never taken away). The board's canvas loads a player's picture with `crossOrigin`: Steam's picture CDN answers `access-control-allow-origin: *` (checked on 2026-11-09 on the default avatar, `avatars.steamstatic.com` and `avatars.akamai.steamstatic.com`).
- **The member** (`externalSignIn`, `packages/accounts/src/external.ts`, the calls to Supabase given in so that the tests read the decisions): Supabase Auth has no member without an address, so the member of an identity that is new is made through the admin API (`createUser`, `email_confirm: true`) with a **stand-in address**, `steam-<random uuid>@external.pbgo.invalid`: `.invalid` is a reserved top-level domain that can never receive mail, and the random part means nobody can have registered it beforehand. `app_metadata` says how they came in a key of ours (`pbgo_origin: "steam"`, `EXTERNAL_ORIGIN_KEY`) and which Steam account they hold (`external: { steam: { subject, name } }`, merged key by key so that another provider's entry is never lost). It is not `provider` and `providers`: Supabase Auth rewrites those from the identities of the user every time a one-time token is spent (`UpdateAppMetaDataProviders` in `verify.go`), and this member holds the e-mail identity of the stand-in address only, so they read `email` from the first sign-in on (found by the independent review of 2026-11-09), `user_metadata` holds `full_name` and `avatar_url` as for any account. A member who exists gets their name and picture refreshed at each sign-in **only when Steam is the way their account was made** (`pbgo_origin`): someone who linked Steam to an account of their own keeps their own. Two first sign-ins at the same moment end with one member (the loser is refused by the table's unique key, deletes the user it just made and signs in as the winner).
- **The table** `public.external_identities` (migration `0010_external_identities.sql`): the key is `(provider, subject)`, so one Steam account is one member, and `(provider, user_id)` is unique, so a member holds one account per provider; `subject` is checked as 17 digits for Steam; `user_id` references `auth.users` `on delete cascade`. Row level security is on with no policy and the public roles have no grant: only the server (the secret key) reads and writes it, like the tables of the games. `supabase/tests/external_identities.sql` checks all of that in one `DO` block that ends with `RAISE EXCEPTION 'RESULT …'` (it rolls everything back; never on production).
- **The session**: `admin.generateLink({ type: "magiclink", email })` gives a one-time `hashed_token` without any e-mail sent, and the route spends it at once with `verifyOtp({ token_hash, type: "email" })` on the cookie-bound server client (the call `/auth/confirm` makes), so the session's cookies are set on the redirect and the token is in the address of nothing. The language cookie is set from the account as after any sign-in. The answer is always a redirect with `Cache-Control: no-store`; the state cookie is cleared whatever happened.
- **What the visitor sees when it fails**: `/login?error=signin&next=…`, the same sentence as any provider's failure, and the log has one line, `[auth/steam] sign-in failed: <reason>` (the step: `state`, `assertion: <problem>`, `steam did not confirm`, `member: <step>`, `session`, `not set up`, `rate limited`), never what Steam or Supabase answered. A visitor who gave up at Steam (`openid.mode=cancel`) goes back to the sign-in as they left it, with no error.
- **What an account made this way looks like** (`accountOf`): `Account.email` is `null` (the stand-in is never shown, and the e-mail identity of the stand-in is not in the linked services); `Account.provider` is `steam` (read from `pbgo_origin`); the navbar's menu says "Signed in with Steam"; the Sign-in methods list Steam as a row with Unlink, off while it is their only way in; the password row is not drawn and the e-mail row has its button off, because the project's "Secure email change" would also ask the stand-in address to confirm. A Steam member who links Google or Discord from the settings can unlink them again, since Steam remains a way in (the count of the ways in reads `app_metadata.external`).
- **Setting it up**: (1) a Web API key at `https://steamcommunity.com/dev/apikey`, for the domain `playboardgamesonline.app` (the Steam account must not be limited); (2) `STEAM_WEB_API_KEY=…` in `~/.config/pbgo/secrets.env`, then `pnpm setup-env`, which checks its shape (32 hexadecimal characters), writes it to `apps/web/.env.local` and pushes it to every target of the Vercel project `pbgo`; it is the switch: `externalProvidersOn` keeps `steam` only when it, `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` and a secret key (`SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY`) are all set, and the button is drawn only then; (3) migration `0010` applied to `pbgo-preview` and then to production (the same `pnpm setup-env`, from an up-to-date `main`, applies it before it pushes the key: run it before the key is set anywhere, or the first sign-in fails at its first step); (4) the Email provider is on in each project, as the session is minted with an `email` one-time token. Nothing to do in Supabase's Providers list. On a Vercel preview the realm and the way back are the address that was asked for, so a preview signs in on its own address.
- **Linking and unlinking Steam from the settings** (PLT-133): the Account settings list Steam among the sign-in methods with Link or Unlink (`SignInMethods`; the row is drawn when the server has the Web API key, or when the account holds Steam). **Link** is `GET /auth/steam?link=1`, for a member who is signed in and is not the demo account (anybody else goes to `/login`): the same state cookie, and `link=1` inside the `return_to` that Steam signs (`steamReturnTo`), so a way back cannot be turned from a sign-in into a link, or back, on the way; it always comes back to `ACCOUNT_SETTINGS_PATH`. The callback makes the same checks as for a sign-in, then asks the Auth server who is signed in (`getAccount`, before Steam is asked anything) and links the number to **that** member (`externalLink`), never to one that the visitor names: an account that another member holds is refused (`taken`), so is a second Steam account for the same member (`has-another`: the table has `unique (provider, user_id)`), nothing of the member's own name or picture is changed (only the name Steam gives is kept, in `app_metadata.external`), and a link that wrote the table and not the metadata is finished by making it again. The way back adds `?steam=linked|taken|has-another|failed` to the settings (`linkNoticeOf`: an alert for a refusal, a toast for a success, told once and the address cleaned; any other value says nothing), and signing in with that Steam account then opens the same member. **Unlink** is `POST /api/hub/unlink-external` (`{ "provider": "steam" }`): the `Origin` must be this site's (403 `FORBIDDEN`), 401 `NOT_SIGNED_IN`, 400 `INVALID_REQUEST`, 429 from the rule `externalUnlink` (10 in ten minutes per IP), 409 `LAST_WAY_IN` when no other way in is left (counted on the server by `externalUnlink` from the identities of Supabase Auth and the other external accounts, the connections not counted: `canUnlink`), 200 `{ "unlinked": true }`; the row of the table goes first, then `app_metadata.external.steam`, and `pbgo_origin` too when the account was made with Steam. The demo account is told as done and nothing is written. Two unlinks sent at the same moment can both pass the check: that is not guarded against.
- **Not built (v1)**: signing in with Steam never links to an existing account by itself (a person with a Google account who signs in with Steam gets a second member, unless they linked Steam from their settings first, above); adding a real address to a Steam member (the hub would have to verify it with a code of its own and set it with the admin API, `updateUserById({ email, email_confirm: true })`, since `updateUser({ email })` waits for the stand-in); Epic Games.
- **Limits**: each Steam sign-in spends a one-time token through Supabase Auth's `/verify`, which the project limits per IP (30 verifications in 5 minutes by default, Authentication › Rate Limits), and the server's address is the same for every sign-in and every `/auth/confirm` link, so a burst above that fails the sign-ins until it clears. A well-formed assertion with a made-up signature costs one call to Steam (five seconds at most) before it is refused: the callback's limit (`externalSignIn`, 20 a minute per IP) counts per server instance, so a flood from many addresses could get the server's address throttled by Steam, and Steam sign-ins would fail until it clears (nothing else would).
- **Tested**: the pure parts (`steam.test.ts`: every way an assertion can be wrong, with a fake `fetch` for Steam's confirmation and the Web API; `external.test.ts`: the member with in-memory fakes of the admin API and the table, the race, a returning member, a member who linked Steam to another account, every failing step; `providers.test.ts`, `account.test.ts`) and the table (`supabase/tests/external_identities.sql`, run on a throwaway Postgres: 14 checks). **Read in the source of `supabase/auth` (branch `master`, 2026-11-09) and not run**: the admin API (`createUser`) and `generateLink` check an address's format only (`validateEmail`, `checkmail.ValidateFormat`); the check that refuses `.invalid` and the hosts that do not resolve (`invalidHostSuffixes`, `internal/mailer/validateclient`) wraps the sending of an e-mail, and none is sent here; `generateLink` of a magic link writes its token in `recovery_token` and sends nothing, and `verifyOtp` with `type: "email"` looks the token hash up in `confirmation_token` and `recovery_token` (`verifyTokenHash`). **Not verified live**: a sign-in with a real Steam account (the first one is the check: read `raw_app_meta_data` of the member afterwards, it must hold `pbgo_origin` and `external`); that `openid.return_to` comes back exactly as it was sent (it is compared as a string: if Steam re-encoded it, every sign-in would fail closed, at `assertion: return-to` in the log); that the hosted build is this code; how `app_metadata` of `updateUserById` merges on it (read as a top-level merge); and that Steam serves avatars only from the two hosts above.

## Sign-up with an e-mail address (PLT-117)
`/signup` (`(hub)/signup/page.tsx`, `SignupCard` and `SignupCode` of `apps/web/src/components`) makes an account from an e-mail address and a password, in the page of `/login` (`AuthShell`, no navbar): the same card, the same row of providers (`ProviderRow`, since a provider's sign-in makes an account too) and a link each way ("New here? Create an account" on `/login`, "Already have an account? Sign in" on `/signup`, `?next=` kept). A visitor who is signed in already goes on to `next`, like on `/login`. The page is `noindex`, `Disallow: /signup` in `robots.txt`, not in the sitemap, and in the proxy's matcher (it reads the session); `safeNext` never goes back to `/signup`, and `/signup` is a reserved slug.
- **The form** (step 1): an e-mail address, a password (`PasswordInput`: a show / hide button) and a display name that may be left empty, then a line under the button, "By creating an account you accept the Terms of Use and the Privacy Policy. You must be 13 or older." (no checkbox). `checkSignup` (`packages/accounts/src/signup.ts`) judges it before anything leaves, field by field: an address as `emailOf` takes one, a password of 8 characters at least and 72 bytes at most (bcrypt reads no more) with no rule about what it is made of, a display name of 20 characters at most (the length a table keeps). It then calls `signUpWithPassword` (`./client`): `supabase.auth.signUp({ email, password, options: { data, emailRedirectTo, captchaToken } })`. Nobody is signed in by it: Supabase Auth creates the user unconfirmed (`auth.users`) and e-mails the template above.
- **The code** (step 2, `SignupCode`): the card turns to "Check your e-mail" and asks for the six digits (`InputOTP`; the sixth goes by itself, the button is for whoever prefers to press it). `verifySignupCode` calls `verifyOtp({ email, token, type: "email" })` (the type Supabase's documentation gives for "Verify Signup One-Time Password (OTP)", as for the link; `signup` is the older name of the same confirmation); it confirms the address and signs the visitor in, the session lands in the cookies and a whole page load takes them on to `safeNext(next)`. A wrong code is cleared and the field is ready again. "Send a new code" (`resendSignupCode`: `resend({ type: "signup", … })`) waits a minute (`useCountdown`, which counts to a time and not by ticks, so a tab in the background is right when the visitor comes back), starts again when an e-mail went out or when Supabase says to wait, and "Use another e-mail address" goes back to the form with the fields kept. The address printed on the screen is `ph-mask` in recordings.
- **The link** (`app/auth/confirm/route.ts`, `GET`): `/auth/confirm?token_hash=…&type=email&next=…`, which spends the token **on the server**. `confirmRequestOf` takes only `type=email` and `type=signup` (a recovery link, an invitation, a magic link or an address change would sign a visitor in on a page that does not know why) and a value that has the shape of a hashed token; `confirmNext` takes `next` as a path of this site or as the whole address of this site that `{{ .RedirectTo }}` writes (any other address is `/me`); `confirmEmail` calls `verifyOtp({ token_hash, type })` with the cookie-bound server client, which sets the session on the redirect. The answer is always a redirect with `Cache-Control: no-store`: to `next` when it worked, else to `/signup?error=confirm&next=…`, where the card says the link expired or was already used (and offers to sign in or to create the account again for a new code). **The token never reaches a page**: it is not in the address of anything rendered, and nothing of ours logs the request. Two limits: the platform's own request log (Vercel) records the address of any request, the token included, but it is spent by then and expires within the hour; and a mail client or a scanner that opens the link before the visitor does spends it (Supabase's own guide says so), which confirms the address and leaves the visitor with a link that "expired": the card says to sign in, and the code in the same e-mail is the way around it.
- **The nickname**: the display name goes into `user_metadata.full_name` (`DISPLAY_NAME_KEY`), the key Google and Twitch fill and `accountOf` reads for the name, so nothing else needs to know how the account was made (`signup.test.ts` keeps the two together); without a name it is the start of the address, as for any account. The account keeps no other setting at sign-up.
- **An address with an account reveals nothing**: a repeated sign-up is answered like a new one (Supabase Auth's obfuscated user, no error, no e-mail; `user_already_exists` is read the same way if the project ever auto-confirms), so the page goes to the code step all the same, and the step says "Nothing yet? Look in your spam folder. If you already have an account, sign in instead." (a sentence everybody reads). A new code is asked the same way and answers the same. What cannot be hidden is that no e-mail arrives for such an address. A password sign-in of an account that was never confirmed says so (`email_not_confirmed`, only to whoever knows the password).
- **The errors, in plain words** (`failureKind`, `signupOutcome`, `codeMessage`, `resendOutcome`; Supabase's own messages are never shown): a weak password or an address Supabase refuses is put on its field; `email_address_not_authorized` (the built-in sender), `unexpected_failure`, a timeout or a 5xx is "We could not send the e-mail. Try again in a few minutes."; `over_email_send_rate_limit` and `over_request_rate_limit` "Too many attempts. Wait a few minutes and try again." (for a new code: "Wait a minute before asking for another code."); `signup_disabled` and `email_provider_disabled` "Creating an account is not available right now."; `captcha_failed`; the network being down; a code that Supabase calls `otp_expired`, which it says of a wrong code too, "That code is wrong or has expired."; anything else "Sign-up did not work. Try again."
- **CAPTCHA, later**: `signUpWithPassword` and `resendSignupCode` already take a `captchaToken`, which goes to Supabase Auth as `options.captchaToken`, and `captcha_failed` has its sentence. Turning CAPTCHA on in the project needs the widget in `SignupCard` (and `SignupCode`) and, very likely, in `/login`: `signInWithPassword` passes no token and Supabase's CAPTCHA probably applies to the password grant too (to check on `pbgo-preview` first). None is drawn; `docs/bots-and-abuse.md` says why the sign-up is the one door that Vercel does not see and when to add one.
- **Tested**: the pure parts and the calls to Supabase with a stub client (`signup.test.ts`, `confirm.test.ts`, `client.test.ts`), the two `@pbgo/ui` fields by their markup (`password-input.test.ts`, `input-otp.test.ts`), and, by hand, the pages and the route against a stand-in of Supabase Auth's endpoints that is not in the repo, then, on 2026-11-07, one real sign-up on `pbgo-preview` through Resend, the owner's own: the project's auth logs show the request answered in 1.3 s with no SMTP error, the code verified 12 seconds later (`type: "email"`, `login_method: otp`) and a password sign-in after it. **Not verified**: the link of the e-mail (`/auth/confirm`) on a real e-mail, how the hosted project renders `{{ .RedirectTo }}` in the template, where the e-mail lands in a mailbox other than the owner's (inbox or spam), and a sign-up on the production project, which the owner set on 2026-11-07 (SMTP, template, password length and code length) and has not tried yet.

## Multi-factor authentication (PLT-129)
A member can ask for a second step at every sign-in: the six-digit code of an authenticator app (a TOTP factor of Supabase Auth, which keeps the secret and judges the code). It is optional, and set up and turned off in the Account section of the settings, "Password & security".
- **The rule, in one place**: a session reaches a member's pages only once it has passed the second step, when the account has one. `getSessionState` (`packages/accounts/src/server.ts`) asks the Auth server for the user (its `factors` are fresh) and reads the assurance level of the session from its access token (`aalOf`: the `aal` claim, read only after `getUser()` has accepted that very token, so nobody else can have written it): a user with a verified TOTP factor whose session is at `aal1` is no account (`getAccount` answers null, so every page, route and seat at a table treats the session as a guest) and is `secondStep`. Whatever the way in: a password, Google, Discord, Steam, the link of an e-mail, a password reset when there is one. An unverified factor (a set-up that was abandoned) counts for nothing, and only TOTP counts: nothing else is offered, and a factor of another kind could not be asked for at sign-in. A level that cannot be read counts as not passed.
- **The sign-in** (`/login`, `MfaChallengeCard`): the page reads `getSessionState`; a visitor with `secondStep` gets the card of the code ("Enter your code": six digits in `InputOTP`, the sixth goes by itself) instead of the sign-in card, and a whole page load takes them on to `next` once `verifySecondStep` is through (`mfa.challengeAndVerify` on the member's first authenticator app, and the next one while the answer is "wrong code"). The browser makes those calls: the limit on codes is the Auth server's, per address, and it must see the visitor's own. After a password, `LoginCard` asks `secondStepNeeded` and loads `/login?next=` again instead of going to `next`. After a provider's way back (`/auth/callback`), the link of an e-mail (`/auth/confirm`) or Steam (`/auth/steam/callback`), the route asks `secondStepPendingAfterSignIn` (the user asked of the Auth server with the token it has just been given) and redirects to `/login?next=` itself: `next` can be a page that anybody sees (the navbar's Sign in and the age gate send the page they are on), where the member would be left as a guest with no code asked. A session that reaches a page of the member by any other road is sent to `/login?next=` by `requireAccount`, and `/home`, which would send it to `/`, sends it there too, since the proxy sees a session at `/` and would send it straight back. Two devices cannot both wait at the card: every successful verification ends the member's other sessions that have not passed it, so the second one is told that its sign-in has ended and offered a Sign in button. The card says "I lost my device" (below) and signs out (a form that posts to `/logout`). `useAccount` reads such a session as signed out, so the navbar offers Sign in, not the dock.
- **Setting it up** (`MultiFactorRow`, the window of its Set up button): `beginSecondStepSetup` takes away a set-up that was abandoned (the Auth server keeps an unverified factor for a few minutes, and a factor's name is unique) and makes a factor (`mfa.enroll`, named "Authenticator app", with the site's name as the issuer the app lists the account under); the window draws its QR code (an `<img>` of the SVG that Supabase Auth gives: the client library puts the markup after the comma as it is, and a `#` in it would cut the address, so `qrImageOf` encodes it), the key in groups of four with a copy button, the sentence that the key is to be kept in a password manager, and the field of the first code; `finishSecondStepSetup` proves it (`challengeAndVerify`). The factor is then the member's second step and **Supabase Auth ends every other session of the member that has not passed it**: the window says so. Closing the window takes an unproven factor away. The QR code and the key are `ph-no-capture`, and the digits of any code field are `ph-mask` (`docs/analytics.md`). The row is drawn for an account with an e-mail address only: a member who came in with Steam has none to be checked by if they lose their device.
- **Turning it off**: `removeSecondStep` asks a code first (the session has passed the second step already, so the Auth server would not ask: the hub does, so that a window left open is not enough), then removes every authenticator app of the member (`mfa.unenroll`, for which Supabase asks `aal2`) and renews the session.
- **What Supabase already refuses** (read in the source of `supabase/auth`, branch `master`, on 2026-11-09, not run on the hosted build): `updateUser` with a new password, e-mail or phone from a session that has not passed the second step, for a member who has one (`insufficient_aal`, which `settingsMessage` tells as "sign in again"), unenrolling a verified factor, and enrolling a further one. So a password or an e-mail change asks nothing more than being signed in, and being signed in means having passed the second step. Every successful verification of a TOTP factor, a sign-in's as well as the first, ends the member's other sessions that have not passed the second step.
- **A lost device**: the member keeps the setup key (the window says so: typing it into a new app gives the codes back); a member who has not writes to the contact address from the e-mail address of their account, and the owner turns the second step off after checking the message: Supabase dashboard, Authentication › Users, the member, "Remove MFA factors" (or `auth.admin.mfa.deleteFactor`). There are no codes of our own: Supabase's recovery codes exist in its source and in supabase-js behind `experimental.recoveryCodes` and are not documented as supported; when they are, they replace this.
- **The errors** (`mfaProblem`, `mfaMessage`; Supabase's own words are never shown): a wrong code and an expired one are one answer (`mfa_verification_failed`); a project that has app authenticators off (`mfa_totp_enroll_not_enabled`, `mfa_totp_verify_not_enabled`) is told "not available"; a session that has ended (`AuthSessionMissingError`, `session_not_found`, `bad_jwt`: the member signed out their other devices, or another device passed its second step) is told "Your sign-in has ended"; the limits (`over_request_rate_limit`), a connection that changed between the two halves (`mfa_ip_address_mismatch`) and the network each have a sentence. A text that is not six digits is told wrong without asking: each try counts against the Auth server's limit.
- **The other devices**: the sessions of a member who turns the second step on are ended on the server, but their access token is good for up to an hour, and the proxy checks it against the project's public key and not against the Auth server. A device that holds one reads as signed in at `/`, which sent it to `/home`, which sent it back (`getUser()` fails, and a page cannot always clear the cookies: **`/` and `/home` bounced between them until the token expired**, reproduced with the real client against a stand-in of the Auth server, it happens after "Sign out other devices" too). The proxy now asks the Auth server on the front page alone (`updateSession(request, { confirm: true })`: one `getUser()`, for a visitor who holds a session cookie) and clears the cookies of a session it does not know, in the response, where it always can; an answer that is not "no such session" leaves them.
- **To set up** (each Supabase project, `pbgo-preview` then `pbgo`): nothing in the code or the environment, and no migration. Authentication › Sign In / Providers, the multi-factor part: app authenticators (TOTP) on for enrolment **and for verification** (Supabase's default for every project; the label is the dashboard's). **Never switch verification off while members hold a factor**: none of them could sign in. Optional: the security e-mails "a verification method was added / removed" (Authentication › Emails), which go out through Resend.
- **Limits**: a session at `aal1` can still read the member's profile from Supabase Auth (`GET /user`: identities, the e-mails of linked providers, the country), which the hub cannot withhold; it cannot change the password, the e-mail or the factor. The first factor can be set up from such a session, so someone who knows a password and moves first can lock the owner out (the contact address is the way back, and the security e-mail tells the member). Wrong codes are limited by Supabase per IP address only (15 a minute for challenge and verify, bursts of 30): a limit per account needs its "MFA verification attempt" Auth hook. Whether Supabase encrypts the secret at rest was not checked. The Privacy Policy and the inventory say all of it (`docs/legal.md`, Findings 17).
- **Tested**: the pure parts (`mfa.test.ts`: the level of a token, the factors that count, the words), the calls to Supabase with a stub client (`client-second-step.test.ts`), the one gate (`session-state.test.ts`) and the proxy (`proxy.test.ts`); and, by hand, in headless Chrome with the real pages and the real supabase-js client against a stand-in of Supabase Auth's endpoints that is not in the repo (the set-up, a wrong code, the other device, the code at sign-in, the routes of a half-signed-in visitor, turning it off, a project with enrolment off, a phone at 375px: 32 checks). **Not verified against a real account**: the real round trip (turn it on in `pbgo-preview` with an account of the owner's own, sign out, sign in with the code, turn it off) and that the hosted Supabase answers as its source says; no account is made on a hosted project.

## Deleting an account (PLT-36)
A member deletes their account in `/me/settings/account` (the **Delete account** row) and it is **closed at once**, the way Spotify's page for closing an account does it: nobody can sign in to it and every session of it ends. An e-mail holds a link that **reopens** it for `ACCOUNT_DELETION_GRACE_DAYS` (7) days at least; when they are over the link is dead and the nightly clean-up deletes the account for good. Nothing else is asked of the member (no password, no second sign-in, no link to open first): the e-mail is the way back, not a gate. The owner's decisions (2026-11-09): the link goes to the address of the account (an account with no e-mail address cannot do it from the site), the account is closed at once and reopens for 7 days (first said 30 days kept on hold behind a confirmation link, then changed on his word), and no sentence about a subscription until there is one to cancel. The 7 are `ACCOUNT_DELETION_GRACE_DAYS` of `@pbgo/core/lib/retention`, which the row, the e-mail, the Privacy Policy and the Terms print. Every change of state is a function of migration `0011_account_deletion.sql` that decides in one transaction and that `supabase/tests/account_deletion.sql` runs end to end; `server/account-deletion.ts` of core only hashes the token, keeps the order of the steps and reads the answers.
- **The way through**: (1) the window of the row ("Delete my account") is `POST /api/hub/account/deletion` with `{ intent: "close" }`: the route takes the address from the session (never from the request), makes a token (32 random bytes, 43 characters of base64url) and the day (`accountDeletionDate`: the last millisecond of the UTC day that is `ACCOUNT_DELETION_GRACE_DAYS` days away, so that "until 16 November" is a whole day), **sends the e-mail first** and only then calls `close_account`, which keeps **the hash of the token** (the token itself is only in the e-mail) and the day, bans the account and deletes its sessions. An e-mail that could not leave leaves the account open and says so: an account is never closed with no way back. The route then takes the cookies of the browser off (`clearSessionCookies`: the proxy reads the cookie, so one that outlived its session would send the member to the member home and from there back to the sign-in) and the page is left for the home at once (a whole page load). The window before the button already says the closing, the e-mail and the days, so there is no second window to say it was sent. (2) The link, `/auth/reopen-account?u=<account>&t=<token>` (`reopenLink`), opens a page (`(hub)/auth/reopen-account`) on any device, since a closed account has no session. **The page only looks** (`peek_reopen_link` changes nothing: mail clients and scanners open links) and offers two buttons. *Reopen my account* is `{ intent: "reopen", user, token }`: `reopen_account` deletes the row and puts back the ban the account had before (none, as a rule). *Delete it now*, after one more question, is `{ intent: "now", user, token }`: the route looks at the link first (`erase_account` itself takes anyone's id), then deletes the account, the feedback first. (3) When the day has passed the link is dead (`peek_reopen_link` and `reopen_account` both check the day, so an account is not reopened between the day and the night's run) and the nightly clean-up (`purgeExpired`, `/api/cron/purge`) deletes the account, up to `ERASE_MAX_ACCOUNTS` (20) a night, the oldest first.
- **Closed is Supabase Auth's own ban**: `close_account` sets `auth.users.banned_until` a hundred years ahead, keeps the ban the account had before in `account_deletions.previous_ban` (reopening puts it back) and deletes the account's rows of `auth.sessions` (the refresh tokens go with them). For a banned user the Auth server refuses a password, a provider's sign-in, a one-time code and a refresh token, and every call that carries a token already issued, with `user_banned` (read in `internal/api` of supabase/auth, branch `master`, on 2026-11-09). So no other part of the site has to know about it: `getAccount()` asks the Auth server and gets an error, so the seats of a table (`resolveSeat`), the presence beat and the pages of `/me` and the home see a visitor. The ban is a hundred years so that it holds even if the clean-up is late; the row's day is what the link and the clean-up go by. The row of `account_deletions` is the only state of ours (`user_id`, `token_hash`, `closed_at`, `scheduled_for`, `previous_ban`; row level security on with no policy; a check keeps the day after the closing) and it goes with the account, or when the account is reopened. One thing the closing does not reach at once: the proxy's `getClaims()` reads a token locally, so a device that was not the one that closed the account is taken for signed in for up to the hour its token lives; the front page asks the Auth server (`updateSession` with `confirm`, which answers `user_banned` with a 403) and takes the cookies off, and a page that needs an account asks it too (`getAccount`) and sends the visitor to `/login`.
- **What a closed account is told when it signs in**: with a password, the Auth server says `user_banned` **before it looks at the password**, so it would tell anyone who knows the address that the account is closed; `signInWithPassword` answers it like a wrong password ("That e-mail and that password do not go together.") and gives nothing away. The member who closed it has the e-mail. With a provider (Google, Discord) only the owner of that provider's account gets as far: the Auth server sends the visitor back with `error_code=user_banned`, `/auth/callback` sends them to `/login?error=closed` and the card says "This account is closed. Use the link we e-mailed you to reopen it." Steam's way back reaches the same card: its one-time token is refused with `user_banned` when it is spent for a session (`/auth/steam/callback`; read in the Auth server's source, `verify.go`, not tried with a real Steam account).
- **No address, no request**: `hasMailbox(email)` is true for an address of the right shape whose domain is not `.invalid`, the top-level domain reserved so that it can never receive mail, which is where a provider that gives no address (Steam) puts its stand-in. For such an account, and when `mailAvailable()` is false (production with no `RESEND_API_KEY`), the window says it cannot be done from the site, since there would be no link to reopen it with, and offers a message to the contact address, as the Privacy Policy says, because the right to have data erased is not ours to refuse.
- **The e-mail**: sent from our servers through Resend's API (`core/server/mail.ts`, `RESEND_API_KEY`; it is the other way in next to the SMTP settings of Supabase Auth, "Setting it up", 9), from `MAIL_FROM`, replies to the contact address. The words are `composeDeletionMail` of core (tested: the link, the day, the 7 days, what is deleted and what stays, the escaping of a name), the sender and the name are the site's (`apps/web/src/lib/deletion-mail.ts`). It names no one but the member in its greeting. The link points to the site the member is on (a preview deployment's accounts are its own). On a development server with no key the e-mail is **printed in the terminal** (that is how the flow is tried without sending anything); nothing is ever printed in production, where the log holds the status Resend answered or the kind of error, never an address or a word of the message.
- **What the request guards against**: `close` acts on the strength of the session's cookies, so it refuses a request whose `Origin` is another site (403 `CROSS_ORIGIN`); `reopen` and `now` need none, the token is the proof. The route is rate limited per IP (`accountDeletion`, ten an hour). The token is a key: 256 bits, kept as its SHA-256, compared by the database. The address of the page holds it, so the page is `noindex`, has `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, is out of the analytics (`isExcludedPath`: a page view would carry the address to PostHog) and is in the `Disallow` of `/auth/`; the host's request log still records the address of the request (Vercel), and the link works for the days of the period. Someone who holds a member's session and not their mailbox can close the account, which is a nuisance, and cannot delete it before the end of the period nor reopen it: only the link can.
- **What is deleted** (`erase_account`, in one transaction, after the feedback): the lines of Supabase Auth's audit log about the account (by its id or its address, when the project writes them to the database), then the account itself, which cascades to its identities, sessions, presence, totals, rank points and ratings (with the name and picture copied there), scrubs the tables it sat at (`scrub_member_from_tables`, whatever their status: the seat becomes "Deleted player" with no picture, and its `member_ids` entry goes; it is a trigger on `auth.users`, so a deletion in the dashboard does the same) and erases the name and the picture of its seats in the games played (`erase_member_from_results`, migration 0006). Before that, **the feedback sent with the address of the account** goes with its screenshots (`removeFeedbackOf`: the address alone, whatever its case, wildcards escaped; once the account is gone nothing links that feedback to it, so a failure keeps the account for the next night). A provider's identity (Google, Discord, a Steam number) and the e-mail address are free again. **What stays**: the games played as "Deleted player" (a result names no one); the counts of PostHog, which carry the player id of one game on one device and never the account (`docs/legal.md`, Findings 9: they cannot be found by account, and they go with their retention); Resend's record of the e-mails (30 days); the host's logs.
- **The demo account of development** is in no database: its window is the same, but nothing is closed and no e-mail leaves: its button goes straight to the page its link would open (`/auth/reopen-account?u=demo&t=demo`), whose two buttons show the screens that follow and change nothing. A build answers 404 to the demo's routes.

## The visitor's language (PLT-103)
The language of a visitor is kept on their account, offered first from their browser, and changes when they change it. Two sets of languages, on purpose: a visitor chooses among the platform's **twelve** (`PLATFORM_LANGUAGES` of `@pbgo/studio-kit/constants`, English first and the default and the fallback of every text, then fr, es, de, zh (Simplified Chinese), ja, ru, ar, bn, hi, pt, ko; `PlatformLanguage` in `@pbgo/accounts`), and a site renders the languages it has: the games **four** (`LANGUAGES`, `Locale` of core) until their Studios move to the same system. The four are among the twelve (a type in `packages/core/lib/i18n.ts` stops compiling otherwise), and a visitor whose language a site lacks reads English there: `localeFor` of core, which `getLocale` applies to the cookie. The hub's own texts are English for everyone (PLT-32), so the language changes nothing on the hub yet but the language picker itself; the games and, to come, the catalog follow it.
- **Order**: the account's language, else the cookie `pbgo-locale`, else the browser's, else English (`resolveLanguage`). `matchPlatformLanguage` takes `navigator.languages` or the header `Accept-Language` (weights read, `q=0` and `*` left out) and gives the first language whose first subtag the platform has: `fr-CA` is French, `pt-BR` Portuguese, every `zh-*` (Traditional included: there is no other Chinese yet) Chinese (Simplified), and none is English. The games' own rule without a cookie: the first of their four languages that the browser asks for, else English (`fromAcceptLanguage`, `DEFAULT_LOCALE`: a browser in a language the games lack, and a request with no `Accept-Language` at all, as a crawler sends, get English)
- **Kept**: on the device, the cookie `pbgo-locale` (a year, `SameSite=Lax`, any of the twelve), which is what the server reads, so every page is rendered in the visitor's language at the same address and nothing becomes dynamic; on the account, `user_metadata.pbgo_locale` of Supabase Auth, written with `supabase.auth.updateUser({ data })` by `saveLanguage` and read by `languageOf` (it lands in `Account.language`). Those two functions are the only ones to know the key, so that the language moves to a `profiles.locale` column when the profiles exist (PLT-13); there is no migration. A value in the key that is not one of the twelve (the user can write their own `user_metadata`) reads as none. The key is namespaced because a provider fills keys of its own in the same object (below)
- **One setter**: `setLanguage` (`./set-language`) is used by the games' pickers (`useChooseLocale` of core: the toolbar's menu and `LanguageSelect`) and by the language picker of `/me/settings/language` (`LanguagePicker`, the twelve, each in its own language with its English name beside it). A visitor who holds a session cookie (`sb-…-auth-token`) has the account written first (`./client`, loaded on the spot: a guest never loads the auth client) and the cookie once the account holds it, so the two never disagree; when the account cannot be written nothing changes (`failed`: the cookie would be undone by the account at the next sync). A guest and the demo account keep the cookie alone. The callers draw the page again, set `<html lang>` (games) and report `language_changed` (analytics, now with the twelve)
- **The sync**, account to device: `useLanguageSync`, mounted once by `LanguageSync` in the hub's layout, runs each time the browser reads the session (a page load, a sign-in, the renewal of the token, a change made in another tab). The account wins: its language is written in the cookie and the page is drawn again if it was in another. An account that keeps none yet (its first sign-in, or an account older than this) takes the language in use, the cookie's else the browser's, once; after that a sync never writes the account, only a choice does (`planLanguage`, `applyLanguage`, `syncLanguage`, all tested). The session in the browser holds the account's metadata as of its last renewal, so a change made on another device is looked for once per page load of the hub: `renewIfLanguageChanged` asks the Auth server for the user (one `GET /user`) and, only when its language is not the one the session holds, renews the session, whose new metadata `useAccount` hands to the sync. A device also meets an account at its sign-in, before any page: `/auth/callback` sets the cookie from the account on its redirect and `signInWithPassword` writes it before the page loads, so the first page of a new device, a game's perhaps, is already in it. Nothing for a guest and for the demo account (`isDemoAccount`), and nothing while a change is on its way (`isChangingLanguage`)
- **Limits**: a language chosen on another device reaches the hub at its next full page load (one request to the Auth server, a renewal of the session when the language differs), and only a page of the hub looks: a game's page follows the cookie, so a visitor who goes straight to a game on a device that last held another language reads that one until they open the hub. Apart from that request the sync only reads the session the browser holds and writes a cookie; the server never reads the account for the language
- **Why not the proxy** (looked at, it does not fit): it runs for `/me/*` and `/login` only, and the pages that need the cookie before their first render are the games' (the hub speaks English); matching them would put a function in front of every static page and every game, and a session read in front of all of them, against the rule that public pages and games stay static and untouched by the accounts. Worse, the claims it reads are the account's metadata **as it was when the token was issued**: `updateUser` does not issue a new token (`_updateUser` of `auth-js` replaces the session's user, not its access token, and the server puts `user.UserMetaData` in the JWT at issue, `internal/tokens/service.go`), so for up to an hour the claims hold the old language, and rewriting the cookie from them would undo a choice made a minute earlier. `getUser()` is fresh but a network call to Supabase on every page. Nothing may read the language from the claims
- **What a provider's sign-in does to `user_metadata`** (read in the source of `supabase/auth`, branch `master`, on 2026-11-02, and in the docs; no provider is enabled in either project yet, so none of it was run). Proven by the code: `PUT /user` with `data` (`updateUser`) goes through `UpdateUserMetaData`, which sets each top-level key it is given and deletes only the keys given as null, so the other keys stay; an OAuth sign-in, whether the identity exists, a second provider is linked to the account by its address or the account is new (`createAccountFromExternalIdentity`, `internal/api/external.go`), merges the provider's claims into `raw_user_meta_data` with the same function, so the keys the provider sends are set again each time and every other key is left; the providers build those claims from a fixed struct (`Claims`, with `locale` among its fields) plus the free map `custom_claims`, none of which can be named `pbgo_locale`; a password sign-in and the renewal of a token never touch the metadata (only `admin.go`, `user.go`, `external.go` and `mail.go` call `UpdateUserMetaData`). Google, Twitch and Discord do not fill `locale` at all in that code (LinkedIn does), so the namespace is a safeguard for the day one of them does, not a collision seen today. The docs only say that `user_metadata` is carried in the JWT and that end users can edit it (hence nothing here may be a permission: the value is checked against the twelve). **Not proven**: that the hosted Supabase runs this very code (it is `master`, not the deployed build); any real sign-in with a provider. To check once a provider is enabled on `pbgo-preview`: sign in with it, choose a language in `/me`, sign out, sign in with the same provider again, and look at the user's raw metadata in the dashboard: `pbgo_locale` must still be there. Tested with mocks: `saveLanguage` sends exactly `{ data: { pbgo_locale } }` and nothing else (`client.test.ts`), the sync and the setter (`language-sync.test.ts`, `set-language.test.ts`), the matching and the precedence (`language.test.ts`)
- **English by default** (the owner's decision of 2026-11-02, done in PLT-103): `DEFAULT_LOCALE` is `en`, and a text reads the language asked, then English, then `[missing: <key>]` or nothing, never French (`docs/sanity-and-languages.md`). What the Cookie Policy says of `pbgo-locale` follows this section (`/legal/cookies`)

## The age check (PLT-125)
A game, or an expansion, for adults is played only by members who are old enough. The age is the member's **declared** date of birth: a declaration, never a proof (a real check would be a provider of identity or age checks, another budget and another processor, and nothing asks for it yet). What the code does is keep the declaration where the member cannot rewrite it and judge it on the server.
- **Where the ages come from**: the Studio's "Minimum age" of a `game` and of an `expansion` in the platform's Sanity project (an expansion that says none is played at its game's), read through the catalog (`ageRulesOf` of `@pbgo/catalog/server`, as fresh as the catalog: ten minutes). The platform enforces an age from `RESTRICTED_FROM` (16) up, and the number of the game is the age a member must have (16 asks for 16, 18 for 18); below it the age is advice that the page shows ("Ages 8+"), because the site's own minimum is 13 and the age on a box is a guess at what a child understands: a game for 14 must not shut out a member of 13, nor every guest. On 2026-11-09, 32 of the 81 games of the Studio said an age (6, 8, 10 and 14), so the check refuses nobody until the first game, or expansion, for 16 or 18 is set in the Studio.
- **What a table asks**: the highest enforced age among its game and the expansions its options switch on (`tableAge` of `@pbgo/core/lib/age-rules`; an expansion is switched on by the engine option whose key is its slug, `engineOptionsOf`). An expansion for adults played with a game for everybody makes its table a table for adults.
- **The date**: `member_birthdates` (migration `0012`, `docs/supabase-and-env.md`), one row per member, written and read by the server only and deleted with the account. It is not in `user_metadata`, which the member can rewrite from their browser. It is declared once: the database never replaces a date (`set_member_birthdate`), so a refusal cannot be got round by declaring another one; a mistake is corrected by the owner, who edits the row after a message to the contact address. A date that makes the member younger than 13 (the Terms), is in the future or is more than 120 years back is refused and not kept (`birthdateProblem`). The route is `POST /api/hub/birthdate` (`docs/routing.md`).
- **The age**: whole years on the UTC calendar (`ageInYears` of `@pbgo/accounts`: a birthday counts on its day, a 29 February on the 1st of March in a year that has none), from the date the database holds and the instant of the request. Never from the browser.
- **Where it is enforced**, on the server and before anything is written (`ageGateFor` of `@pbgo/core/server/age-gate`; the app gives the routes the rules because core cannot read the catalog, which depends on core): `POST /api/<slug>/games` (a table opens with the game's defaults, and one of them may switch an expansion for adults on), `…/join` (judged on the table as it is when the write lands, so a host who switches an expansion on while somebody is on the way refuses them), `…/options` (switching on an expansion for adults, or for a higher age: the host must have the age and so must everybody seated, found by the accounts the table recorded for its seats, `games.member_ids`, and their dates; switching off, or changing anything else, asks nothing) and `…/start` (the last look: nobody is seated who is not old enough when the game begins). The routes are made by factories (`postGame`, `postJoin`, `postOptions`, `postStart`) that take the rules, so a route made without them does not compile. For a game that has an age to ask, the seat of a member is written to `games.member_ids` before the response and not after it, so that the next check finds it (a host who acts in the few milliseconds between a seat and its account is refused and asks again).
- **What it refuses with** (worded in `CORE_ERROR_MESSAGES`): `AGE_SIGN_IN_REQUIRED` (401: a guest has no date, so a guest does not sit at a table that asks for an age), `AGE_DATE_REQUIRED` (403: a member who declared none), `AGE_TOO_YOUNG` (403) and, when somebody seated does not qualify, `AGE_TABLE_NOT_ALLOWED` (403, which names nobody). A seat that sat down since the table was read throws `CONFLICT`, and the host asks again.
- **What the pages say** (`components/age-gate.tsx`, `lib/age-state.ts`): a page tells the visitor what is needed before they ask, from the same rules (`ageStatesFor`, pure), and the server decides all the same. An expansion for adults that the visitor cannot choose has a lock on its card with the reason ("Sign in to play it", "Add your date of birth to play it", "Not available at your age": `ExpansionOption.locked` of `LobbySettings`, set by `lockedExpansions`), and a press on it is handed to the page (`onLockedPick`). `useAgeGate()` is what a component that calls a game's routes uses, with the `dialog` it renders: `guard(call)` runs the call and, when the server says the date of birth is missing (`AGE_DATE_REQUIRED`), asks for it in a window (three fields judged by `birthdateProblem`, saved with `POST /api/hub/birthdate`, with the line that says it cannot be changed afterwards) and runs the call again; `demand(refusal)` is for what the page knows already (the "Play ranked" queue and the home's Find a game, which have no server to refuse: they ask first); `fail(error)` tells a refusal in a toast, with a way to sign in for a guest. Opening a table, joining, setting the options and starting go through them (`TableCreator`, `HomeCreateTable`, `OpenTables`, `OwnLobby`, `StartGameButton`). The home offers a member no game they are too young for. The settings of a lobby start again from what the table holds when the server refuses one (an expansion for adults with somebody seated who is not old enough), since they draw a choice before the server has answered it.
- **A game with no enforced age costs no lookup**: neither the person's date nor the table's seats are read for it. A game that has one, in its own right or through an expansion, reads the date of the person who acts; the seats are read only when the age of a table goes up or the table starts.
- **The demo account of development** counts as an adult: it is in no database, and `POST /api/hub/birthdate` answers `saved` for it without writing.
- **If something cannot be read**: the database cannot say who is old enough (the table is missing, a column is) → the request fails with `SERVER_ERROR` and nobody is let through. The platform's Sanity project does not answer while the catalog is cold → no game is known to ask for an age, so tables open without the check until it answers (one line in the logs), rather than every game shutting.
- **Not covered**: a real verification (a false date passes); a table that was open when the age of a game changed; a spectator (whoever has the link of a table can look at its board); the "Play ranked" queue, which lives in the browser (`lib/matchmaking.ts`) with nothing behind it: when the matchmaking of PLT-21 seats people it must build its tables through `ageGateFor`, as the routes do (`assertMay(tableAge(options))` for each person it seats).
- **Tested**: the pure rule (`age.test.ts`: leap days, UTC, the boundaries), the tables' ages (`age-rules.test.ts`), the store with a fake client (`birthdate.test.ts`), the gate (`age-gate.test.ts`), the real routes against an in-memory table (`age-check.test.ts`: who is refused with which code, that a refusal writes nothing, the expansion for adults, the race of a host and a joiner, a database that cannot be read), the route of the date (`routes/api/hub/birthdate/route.test.ts`), `ageRulesFor` (`catalog.test.ts`) and the SQL (`supabase/tests/member_birthdates.sql`). Not verified against a real account: no account is made on a hosted project; the real round trip (declare the date, be refused, be let in) needs a test account in `pbgo-preview` made by the owner and the migration applied (`pnpm setup-env`).

## Rules
- A page that shows an account is rendered per request (it reads cookies): keep that to `/login`, `/signup` and `/me/*`. The navbar and every other page of the hub ask the browser, never the server
- Never trust what the browser holds: the server asks the Auth server, and a redirect target goes through `safeNext`
- The visitor's language: one setter (`setLanguage`), one writer of the account (`saveLanguage`), one reader (`languageOf`); the cookie is what the server reads and the account never is; the language is never read from the token's claims; a value from a cookie or from `user_metadata` is checked against the twelve (`isPlatformLanguage`) before it is used
- Platform UI only: the pages use `@pbgo/ui` components and tokens, and a new visual component goes into `@pbgo/ui` first, with its section in the docs of the design system (`docs/design/03-components/`)
- What an account stores, what a provider hands over and what a table copies from an account are in the Privacy Policy (`/legal/privacy`, "When you sign in"): change it with the code, and its inventory with it (`docs/legal.md`)
- A sign-up never says which addresses have an account (a repeated sign-up goes through the same steps) and tells what went wrong in its own words, never Supabase's; the token of a confirmation link is spent on the server, is in the address of no page, and nothing of ours logs it (`/auth/confirm`, "Sign-up with an e-mail address")
- Who is signed in is decided in one place, `getSessionState`: a session that has not passed the second step of its account is no account anywhere, so a reader of the session goes through `getAccount` and never reads the Auth user alone ("Multi-factor authentication")
- A Steam sign-in believes nothing that the browser or Steam's redirect says until the state, the assertion and Steam's own confirmation have been checked, in that order; the stand-in address of a member who came in with it is never shown, never e-mailed and used for nothing but Supabase's own key ("Sign-in with Steam")
