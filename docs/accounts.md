# Accounts

Sign-in with a password, or with Facebook, Google, Apple and Discord, through Supabase Auth, the pages of a signed-in visitor and the friends' panel (PLT-16, PLT-17, PLT-23). What exists, what is still a placeholder, and what has to be set up in Supabase before a visitor can sign in.

## What exists
- `@pbgo/accounts` (`packages/accounts`): `.` the pure part (`Account`, `accountOf`, `safeNext`, `PROVIDERS`, the figures of a profile: `statsOf`, `perGame`, `ordinal`, and the visitor's language: `PlatformLanguage`, `matchPlatformLanguage`, `resolveLanguage`, `planLanguage`), `./config` (the project: `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`, the two variables the games already read; without them the hub has no sign-in and says so), `./client` (`useAccount`, `signInWith`, `saveLanguage`, `useLanguageSync`), `./server` (`createAuthClient`, `getAccount`, `requireAccount`), `./proxy` (`updateSession`), `./language` (the pure language part, alone) and `./set-language` (the setter, with no Supabase code in it), which the games' pages load too through core, and `./demo`. The app and core import it; it never imports core or a game
- An account is what the provider says of the visitor (`accountOf`): Google and Facebook give `full_name` and `avatar_url`, Discord `custom_claims.global_name` (the name they chose, not their login) and `avatar_url`, and Apple's sign-in on the web gives neither a name nor a picture; the nickname falls back to the start of the e-mail (never an Apple relay address, `…@privaterelay.appleid.com`, which says nothing of the person), then to "Player". Nothing is stored beside Supabase Auth's own `auth.users`: no `profiles` table and no nickname tag (PLT-13, PLT-39); the one setting an account keeps is the visitor's language, in its `user_metadata` ("The visitor's language", below)
- Session: Supabase Auth's cookies (`@supabase/ssr`), the same in the browser and on the server. A page that is for a signed-in visitor asks the Auth server (`getUser()`, never the cookie alone: anyone can write a cookie); `apps/web/src/proxy.ts` (Next 16's `proxy`, the former middleware) renews an expired session before the page renders, and only for `/me/*` and `/login`, so every public page and every game stays static. A visitor who is not signed in and asks for `/me/*` goes to `/login?next=<the page>` (the proxy, then `requireAccount` as a second check)
- Routes (`apps/web`): `/login` (`(hub)/login`: a username and a password (`signInWithPassword`: the first field takes an e-mail address, `emailOf`, since Supabase Auth knows a visitor by their address alone and a username has nowhere to be looked up until the profiles exist, PLT-39; a username is told to use the address), then, under "or continue with", the four providers as four small buttons side by side, each with nothing on it but its mark (`ProviderButton`, `@pbgo/ui`), on the hub's backdrop; `?next=` is where to go back to, `?error=signin` a sign-in that did not work; a visitor already signed in is sent on), `/auth/callback` (the provider's way back: it exchanges the one-time `code` for a session and redirects to `next`), `/logout` (POST only: it ends the session and redirects to `/`; a form posts to it, never a link), `/auth/demo` (development only, below), and the pages of an account `/me` (the profile: figures, the games one by one, the last games, then the Settings with the Language row), `/me/games` (in progress, then history) and `/me/friends` (add a friend, requests, and the list where the side panel has no room). They are `noindex`, disallowed in `robots.txt`, and not in the sitemap
- `safeNext` is the one way back from a sign-in: a value anyone can write into a link is a path of this site (one slash, no backslash, no control character, not the sign-in's own way) or it is `/me`
- The navbar (`SiteHeader`, a client component on static pages) reads the session in the browser (`useAccount`): the server renders every page as if nobody were signed in, so the first paint is the same everywhere. Until the browser has read its session the two account buttons keep their place but are not drawn (`invisible`), so a signed-in visitor never sees them flash; then Sign in (to `/login?next=<this page>`) and Let's play, or `SiteSocial` (`apps/web`): one container on the right, the dock (`SocialDock`), that is a bar while it is closed and a real side panel while it is open, the same element growing and shrinking between the two. The bar has the games the visitor's friends are in (`FriendsBar`, three pills at most), then, behind a thin line, the visitor's picture, with a count of what waits for them on its corner (`AvatarCount`: messages not read, requests to answer), which opens the menu of the account (`AccountMenu`: their picture big, with the dot of their presence, their name and the word that says it, then Profile, My games, Friends, Sign out; under the bar while the dock is closed, and beside the open panel, on its left, as the Riot Client's does), and the button of the panel (`SocialPanelToggle`: `PanelRightOpenIcon`, then `PanelRightCloseIcon` once it is open). Open, the bar is the header of the panel, the way the Riot Client has it: the games fold away, the picture glides to the start of the bar and says who they are beside it (`SocialDockIdentity`: their name and their status), the button stays where it was, and the panel (`SocialPanel`, 20rem wide, down to the bottom of the window) is under it, in three tabs: the friends game by game (each game with its picture, its name and how many of them are in it, the ones in its lobby and the ones in a game mixed), the conversations, and the way to add a friend with the requests. The tile of the current tab glides to the one that is pressed and its pane comes in from that side. In the conversations what is new is white and what was read is grey. A press on a friend or a conversation opens the chat with them (`ChatWindow`, next to the dock, sliding in from the bottom, with a `···` menu: join or invite them, remove them, block them), where a message sent is kept for as long as the page is open
- The frame of the pages of an account (`AccountShell`): the page in a column that does not stretch with the window, between the navbar and the footer. The friends are not in it: they are the navbar's, on every page, and `/me/friends` lists them in full (`FriendsList`, the `FriendsPanel` of `@pbgo/ui`, with its search). The design docs draw the friends' bar, the social panel with the dock that holds it and the chat window (`docs/design/03-components/05-social.md`)

## What is a placeholder
- A real account has no friends, no conversations, no requests and no games: the pages and the panel say so with an empty state. The games (the tables, the results) and the friends are in the platform schema of PLT-13 (`tables`, `game_results`, `players`) and PLT-23; until they exist `apps/web/src/lib/account-data.ts` (the pages) and `apps/web/src/lib/use-social.ts` (the panel, in the browser) return nothing for a real account. The buttons that need them (add a friend, invite, join, accept) are not drawn for a real account, and do nothing for the demo account
- The home's figures under the title are counts, not placeholders: the players connected now (`online_player_count()`: the player ids that a table saw in the last two minutes, `games.seen`, counted once per game, tables that are over left out; kept a minute) and the members (`member_count()`: the users of Supabase Auth that are neither deleted nor anonymous; kept ten minutes), both of migration `0003_hub_figures.sql` (`docs/supabase-and-env.md`), read by `getHubFigures` of `apps/web/src/lib/hub-stats.ts`. A guest who played is not a member, and a browser that plays two games is two players online (nothing links the two ids). Where a figure cannot be counted (no Supabase, or the migration not applied yet: `pnpm setup-env` applies it) it is left out of the page and never made up; only local development, which has no Supabase, shows a made-up 128 and 12,480 so that the hero can be seen. The figure for the games is the size of the catalog, coming-soon games included.
- What works is what needs no friend to exist: the panel's tabs and search, "Invite a friend" (it copies the address of the sign-in), and the chat window, whose messages stay for as long as the page is open: messages have no backend yet (PLT-23). Joining, inviting, sending and answering a request do nothing but for the demo account, and are not drawn for a real one
- Not there yet: the rest of the settings page (nickname, picture, sound, hide my history: PLT-16; the language is its first row), the nickname with a tag (PLT-39), a guest's past games attached on sign-up (PLT-38), the signed-in state in a game (PLT-17), account deletion (PLT-36), the admin through the account (PLT-41). The Privacy Policy and the Terms of Use (`/legal/privacy`, `/legal/terms`) are stubs, for the owner to write; the Cookie Policy (`/legal/cookies`) lists the sign-in's cookies (`sb-…`) and must change with them (PLT-13 replaces the per-game player cookie with a Supabase session)

## Setting it up (once for each Supabase project: `pbgo-preview`, then production)
The code needs no new variable. One app per provider is enough: it holds the callback of both projects (`https://ckpbuuxcdsayonyahlkk.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback` for `pbgo-preview`, `https://igxsfcezbhidnlknuudf.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback` for `pbgo`, the production one) and its ID and secret go into both. Do `pbgo-preview` first and try it on `http://localhost:3100/login`, whose `.env.local` points at it. A visitor can only sign in once the providers are enabled in the project (Authentication › Providers) and its redirect URLs allow the hub. The password needs the Email provider (on by default; keep "Confirm email" on): there is no sign-up page or password reset yet, so an e-mail account can only be made by hand in the dashboard (Authentication › Users) until they exist. The others:
1. **Facebook**: at developers.facebook.com, create an app (the use case "Authenticate and request data from users with Facebook Login") and, in the settings of Facebook Login, put `https://<project ref>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback` under Valid OAuth Redirect URIs. Under Use Cases › Authentication and Account Creation check that `public_profile` and `email` are listed (add `email` if it is not: Supabase needs the address). Put the App ID and the App Secret (Settings › Basic) in the Facebook provider of the project and enable it. Only people with a role on the app can sign in while it is in Development: to open it to everybody fill in its icon, the privacy policy URL (`https://playboardgamesonline.app/legal/privacy`, still a stub: PLT-36) and the terms URL, then switch it to Live (Meta may ask to verify the `email` permission). A Facebook account with no confirmed e-mail cannot sign in
2. **Google**: in Google Cloud Console, APIs & Services › Credentials, create an OAuth client ID of type Web application; its authorised redirect URI is `https://<project ref>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`. Put its client ID and secret in the Google provider of the project and enable it
3. **Apple**: it needs the Apple Developer Program (about US$99 a year). In Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles create an App ID with Sign in with Apple, a Services ID (its identifier is the client ID) with Sign in with Apple configured for the domain `<project ref>.supabase.co` and the return URL `https://<project ref>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`, and a key with Sign in with Apple (download the `.p8` once, note its Key ID and the Team ID). The Apple provider of the project takes the Services ID and a secret, which is a JWT signed with that key. **Apple lets that secret live six months at most: generate a new one and paste it in every six months, or Apple sign-in stops working** (put a reminder in the calendar). On the web Apple gives no name and no picture, so an Apple account is "Player" with its initials until the settings page exists (PLT-16), and a visitor may hide their address (a `privaterelay.appleid.com` one)
4. **Discord**: in the Discord Developer Portal, a new application, OAuth2, add the same redirect `https://<project ref>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`; put its client ID and secret in the Discord provider and enable it
5. **URL configuration** (Authentication › URL Configuration): in production the Site URL `https://playboardgamesonline.app` and the redirect URL `https://playboardgamesonline.app/**`; in `pbgo-preview` the Site URL `http://localhost:3100` and the redirect URLs `http://localhost:3100/**` and `https://*-playboardgamesonline.vercel.app/**`, the pattern of the Vercel preview addresses (`<project>-git-<branch>-<team>.vercel.app`: a `*` does not cross a dot, so the team's slug ends the pattern). They end in `/**` and not in `/auth/callback` because the way back carries a `?next=`, which a pattern with an exact path would not match
6. `pnpm setup-env` fills `apps/web/.env.local` with the preview project's URL and publishable key (they are the same two variables the games use). A checkout without them shows the sign-in page with its buttons off

## The demo account (development only)
`/auth/demo?next=/me` signs a made-up account in (a cookie, `pbgo_demo_account`; `/auth/demo?off=1` signs it out) to see the pages of a signed-in visitor on a machine with no provider set up. It has a picture, friends, requests, conversations and games of its own (`account-data.ts`, `demo-data.ts`; the pictures look like the avatars of random people on Discord: DiceBear avatars (illustrated faces, cartoons, pixel art, abstract gradients; CC0 styles only) drawn once and kept as data URIs, the account's own small one in `demo.ts` and the friends' and requests' in `demo-avatars.ts`, which only the docs of the design system and the demo data import, so that nothing is a file, nothing is fetched and it works offline). A build answers 404 to the route and ignores the cookie (`demoAllowed()` reads `NODE_ENV`, which the build inlines), and nothing about it is stored anywhere

## The visitor's language (PLT-103)
The language of a visitor is kept on their account, offered first from their browser, and changes when they change it. Two sets of languages, on purpose: a visitor chooses among the platform's **twelve** (`PLATFORM_LANGUAGES` of `@pbgo/studio-kit/constants`, English first and the default and the fallback of every text, then fr, es, de, zh (Simplified Chinese), ja, ru, ar, bn, hi, pt, ko; `PlatformLanguage` in `@pbgo/accounts`), and a site renders the languages it has: the games **four** (`LANGUAGES`, `Locale` of core) until their Studios move to the same system. The four are among the twelve (a type in `packages/core/lib/i18n.ts` stops compiling otherwise), and a visitor whose language a site lacks reads English there: `localeFor` of core, which `getLocale` applies to the cookie. The hub's own texts are English for everyone (PLT-32), so the language changes nothing on the hub yet but the Language row itself; the games and, to come, the catalog follow it.
- **Order**: the account's language, else the cookie `pbgo-locale`, else the browser's, else English (`resolveLanguage`). `matchPlatformLanguage` takes `navigator.languages` or the header `Accept-Language` (weights read, `q=0` and `*` left out) and gives the first language whose first subtag the platform has: `fr-CA` is French, `pt-BR` Portuguese, every `zh-*` (Traditional included: there is no other Chinese yet) Chinese (Simplified), and none is English. The games' own rule without a cookie: the first of their four languages that the browser asks for, else English (`fromAcceptLanguage`, `DEFAULT_LOCALE`: a browser in a language the games lack, and a request with no `Accept-Language` at all, as a crawler sends, get English)
- **Kept**: on the device, the cookie `pbgo-locale` (a year, `SameSite=Lax`, any of the twelve), which is what the server reads, so every page is rendered in the visitor's language at the same address and nothing becomes dynamic; on the account, `user_metadata.pbgo_locale` of Supabase Auth, written with `supabase.auth.updateUser({ data })` by `saveLanguage` and read by `languageOf` (it lands in `Account.language`). Those two functions are the only ones to know the key, so that the language moves to a `profiles.locale` column when the profiles exist (PLT-13); there is no migration. A value in the key that is not one of the twelve (the user can write their own `user_metadata`) reads as none. The key is namespaced because a provider fills keys of its own in the same object (below)
- **One setter**: `setLanguage` (`./set-language`) is used by the games' pickers (`useChooseLocale` of core: the toolbar's menu and `LanguageSelect`) and by the Language row of `/me` (`LanguageRow`, the twelve in their own language). A visitor who holds a session cookie (`sb-…-auth-token`) has the account written first (`./client`, loaded on the spot: a guest never loads the auth client) and the cookie once the account holds it, so the two never disagree; when the account cannot be written nothing changes (`failed`: the cookie would be undone by the account at the next sync). A guest and the demo account keep the cookie alone. The callers draw the page again, set `<html lang>` (games) and report `language_changed` (analytics, now with the twelve)
- **The sync**, account to device: `useLanguageSync`, mounted once by `LanguageSync` in the hub's layout, runs each time the browser reads the session (a page load, a sign-in, the renewal of the token, a change made in another tab). The account wins: its language is written in the cookie and the page is drawn again if it was in another. An account that keeps none yet (its first sign-in, or an account older than this) takes the language in use, the cookie's else the browser's, once; after that a sync never writes the account, only a choice does (`planLanguage`, `applyLanguage`, `syncLanguage`, all tested). The session in the browser holds the account's metadata as of its last renewal, so a change made on another device is looked for once per page load of the hub: `renewIfLanguageChanged` asks the Auth server for the user (one `GET /user`) and, only when its language is not the one the session holds, renews the session, whose new metadata `useAccount` hands to the sync. A device also meets an account at its sign-in, before any page: `/auth/callback` sets the cookie from the account on its redirect and `signInWithPassword` writes it before the page loads, so the first page of a new device, a game's perhaps, is already in it. Nothing for a guest and for the demo account (`isDemoAccount`), and nothing while a change is on its way (`isChangingLanguage`)
- **Limits**: a language chosen on another device reaches the hub at its next full page load (one request to the Auth server, a renewal of the session when the language differs), and only a page of the hub looks: a game's page follows the cookie, so a visitor who goes straight to a game on a device that last held another language reads that one until they open the hub. Apart from that request the sync only reads the session the browser holds and writes a cookie; the server never reads the account for the language
- **Why not the proxy** (looked at, it does not fit): it runs for `/me/*` and `/login` only, and the pages that need the cookie before their first render are the games' (the hub speaks English); matching them would put a function in front of every static page and every game, and a session read in front of all of them, against the rule that public pages and games stay static and untouched by the accounts. Worse, the claims it reads are the account's metadata **as it was when the token was issued**: `updateUser` does not issue a new token (`_updateUser` of `auth-js` replaces the session's user, not its access token, and the server puts `user.UserMetaData` in the JWT at issue, `internal/tokens/service.go`), so for up to an hour the claims hold the old language, and rewriting the cookie from them would undo a choice made a minute earlier. `getUser()` is fresh but a network call to Supabase on every page. Nothing may read the language from the claims
- **What a provider's sign-in does to `user_metadata`** (read in the source of `supabase/auth`, branch `master`, on 2026-11-02, and in the docs; no provider is enabled in either project yet, so none of it was run). Proven by the code: `PUT /user` with `data` (`updateUser`) goes through `UpdateUserMetaData`, which sets each top-level key it is given and deletes only the keys given as null, so the other keys stay; an OAuth sign-in, whether the identity exists, a second provider is linked to the account by its address or the account is new (`createAccountFromExternalIdentity`, `internal/api/external.go`), merges the provider's claims into `raw_user_meta_data` with the same function, so the keys the provider sends are set again each time and every other key is left; the providers build those claims from a fixed struct (`Claims`, with `locale` among its fields) plus the free map `custom_claims`, none of which can be named `pbgo_locale`; a password sign-in and the renewal of a token never touch the metadata (only `admin.go`, `user.go`, `external.go` and `mail.go` call `UpdateUserMetaData`). Facebook, Google, Apple and Discord do not fill `locale` at all in that code (LinkedIn does), so the namespace is a safeguard for the day one of them does, not a collision seen today. The docs only say that `user_metadata` is carried in the JWT and that end users can edit it (hence nothing here may be a permission: the value is checked against the twelve). **Not proven**: that the hosted Supabase runs this very code (it is `master`, not the deployed build); any real sign-in with a provider; Apple's flow. To check once a provider is enabled on `pbgo-preview`: sign in with it, choose a language in `/me`, sign out, sign in with the same provider again, and look at the user's raw metadata in the dashboard: `pbgo_locale` must still be there. Tested with mocks: `saveLanguage` sends exactly `{ data: { pbgo_locale } }` and nothing else (`client.test.ts`), the sync and the setter (`language-sync.test.ts`, `set-language.test.ts`), the matching and the precedence (`language.test.ts`)
- **English by default** (the owner's decision of 2026-11-02, done in PLT-103): `DEFAULT_LOCALE` is `en`, and a text reads the language asked, then English, then `[missing: <key>]` or nothing, never French (`docs/sanity-and-languages.md`). What the Cookie Policy says of `pbgo-locale` follows this section (`/legal/cookies`)

## Rules
- A page that shows an account is rendered per request (it reads cookies): keep that to `/login` and `/me/*`. The navbar and every other page of the hub ask the browser, never the server
- Never trust what the browser holds: the server asks the Auth server, and a redirect target goes through `safeNext`
- The visitor's language: one setter (`setLanguage`), one writer of the account (`saveLanguage`), one reader (`languageOf`); the cookie is what the server reads and the account never is; the language is never read from the token's claims; a value from a cookie or from `user_metadata` is checked against the twelve (`isPlatformLanguage`) before it is used
- Platform UI only: the pages use `@pbgo/ui` components and tokens, and a new visual component goes into `@pbgo/ui` first, with its section in the docs of the design system (`docs/design/03-components/`)
