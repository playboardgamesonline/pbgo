# The platform's Sanity project

`playboardgamesonline` (project `mhgqs6fe`, dataset `production`, public): the content of the hub itself, apart from the games' own projects. It holds the **catalog** (every game the hub lists and the expansions played with them, with the people, publishers and categories they point at), the **editorial** documents (articles, changelog, announcements) and, one day, the **shop**. The hub reads the catalog from it (PLT-90), in place of the Notion Games database it read before: this project was first filled by a seed from the Notion rows, and the Studio is where it is edited now. Notion stays the internal tracker (what is being built, in which order). Part of PLT-19 and PLT-90.

It is not a game's Sanity project: a game's project holds what is played (cards, board, sounds) and its texts in four languages, this one holds what the hub says about games.

## Where it lives

| What | Where |
| --- | --- |

| The seed | `platform/content`, package `@pbgo/platform`: `content:seed` (Notion → drafts), `content:art` (Notion pictures → a folder) and `content:migrate-expansions` (the expansions made as games → the `expansion` type, PLT-102) |
| The model | `platform/apps/studio/schemaTypes/`, the lists it shares with the seed in `constants.ts` (the creator roles are `CREATOR_ROLES` of `@pbgo/studio-kit/constants`, which the hub words its credits from too) |
| The hub's reader | `packages/catalog/src/sanity.ts` (`readSanityGames`), the public API with no token; what the hub makes of it is `docs/catalog.md` |
| Tests | `pnpm --filter platform-studio test` (the model, the rules, the sidebar and the tabs, the queries of the Games and Expansions tabs) and `pnpm --filter @pbgo/platform test` (the seed against the Studio's schema, the art step, the migration of the expansions) |

## The model

The Studio's sidebar has three blocks: **Catalog**, **Editorial** and **Shop**. The shop block is empty on purpose: there is no document type for it, no skin, until we have looked at how Discord builds its shop.

### Catalog

| Type | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| `game` | **Identity**: `name`, `slug`, `bggId`, `kind` (game · reimplementation), `baseGame` (→ game, for a reimplementation), `order`. **Facts**: `playersMin`, `playersMax` (empty: or more), `duration` (minutes), `minAge`, `complexity` (1 to 5, BoardGameGeek's weight), `releaseDate`, `categories` (→ category). **Credits**: `creators` (a list: one creator with one or several roles per line), `publishers` (→ publisher). **Texts**: `tagline`, `description`, in English. **Rules**: `rulesFr`, `rulesEn`, `rulesEs`, `rulesDe` (links). **Visuals**: `cover`, `logo`, `icon`, `background`, `boxFront` (the box, front). **Related**: `related` (→ game) |
| `expansion` | **Identity**: `name`, `slug`, `baseGame` (→ game, required, a strong reference), `bggId`. **Facts**: `playersMin`, `playersMax` (empty: or more), `duration` (minutes), `minAge`, `releaseDate`. **Credits**: `creators`, `publishers`, as a game's. **Texts**: `description`, in English. **Rules**: `rulesFr`, `rulesEn`, `rulesEs`, `rulesDe` (links). **Visuals**: `cover`, `boxFront` (the box, front). In tabs like a game's, without Related; no kind, order, categories, complexity, tagline or related. The game it is played with has a read-only **Expansions** tab |
| `creator` | `name`, `photo`, `website`, `bggId`, and a read-only **Games** tab |
| `publisher` | `name`, `website`, `logo`, `bggId`, and a read-only **Games** tab |
| `category` | `name`, `icon`, `color` (#rrggbb), `description` |

### Editorial

| Type | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| `article` | `title`, `slug`, `excerpt`, `body` (rich text), `cover`, `publishedAt`, `author`, `games` |
| `changelogEntry` | `title`, `date`, `kind` (new game · feature · improvement · fix), `body` (rich text), `image`, `games` |
| `announcement` | `title`, `message`, `tone` (info · success · alert), `startsAt`, `endsAt`, `link` (address and label), `dismissible`, `game` |

### What was decided, and why

- **English for now.** Every text is in English; the translation system of Sanity (field-level or document-level) comes later and will need a migration of the fields. The one multilingual thing is the link to the rules, one field per language of the platform (`LANGUAGES` of `@pbgo/studio-kit`). The French, Spanish and German descriptions of the Notion rows are not imported: since PLT-90 the hub shows English until the translations are plugged in.
- **The roles are on the line of the game, not on the person.** A person is a designer of one game, an artist of another, both on a third: `game.creators` holds one line per person with all their roles on that game, and `creator` has no role. The roles are BoardGameGeek's credits, in its words: Designer, Solo designer, Developer, Artist, Graphic designer, Editor, Sculptor, Writer, Insert designer (`CREATOR_ROLES`). Its "Editor" is who edits the text, the rules or the cards, not the publisher, which is a `publisher` apart: that is why the list is `creators` and not `credits`, which would include the publishers. Left out: "Accessory designer" (it credits accessories, not games) and "Honored designs" (awards). A person has one `bggId`, the number in the address of their BoardGameGeek page.
- **The link is stored on the game only.** A creator and a publisher do not list their games: the other side is computed, by the **Games** tab of their form in the Studio (a query on the games, with the roles of a creator on each) and by a reverse query for the hub, `*[_type == "game" && $id in creators[].creator._ref]`. Two stored sides would one day disagree.
- **A document for what is shared.** Creators, publishers and categories are documents of their own, referenced by the games, not names written on each game: a name is written once, a person has a photo and a website, a category an icon and a color.
- **References are strong once published, except `related`.** A strong reference needs the document it points at to be published first, and keeps it from being deleted. The dataset refuses a strong reference to a document that is not published, a draft's included, so the seed writes each reference as the Studio does while the target is a draft: weak (`_weak`) and marked `_strengthenOnPublish`, which makes the Studio turn it strong when the draft is published. That is why the order is: creators, publishers and categories, then the games that have no `baseGame`, then the games that have one, then the expansions, whose `baseGame` is a strong reference too. `related` is the exception: two games are related both ways, so strong references would each wait for the other to be published; it is weak for good (`weak: true` in the schema, and `weak` in the mark the seed writes). The editorial documents point at games strongly.
- **An expansion is a document of its own (PLT-102).** It used to be a `game` with `kind: "expansion"` and a hidden `baseGame`, kept off the hub by a filter on `kind`: one missed filter and it showed up as a game, and its form carried fields it has no use for (categories, complexity, order, related). It is now an `expansion`, which the hub never reads as a game because the hub never reads it at all (`_type == "game"` only). Its `baseGame` is required and a strong reference, so a game cannot be deleted from under its expansions. Its pictures are a `cover` and a `boxFront`, and no logo, icon or background (the owner's ruling: those are what the hub draws of a game, and an expansion has no page there). An expansion that plays without its base game is not one: it is a `reimplementation` of it, a game (Flip 7 Vengeance). Hub pages for expansions are out of scope; whoever lists the expansions of a game does it by name or by release date, there is no `order`.
- **No `status` and no `live` flag.** Whether a game is playable and indexable stays in the registry (`packages/games/src/registry.ts`, PLT-83): one switch, in the code that serves the game. `order` is a number that lists the games, lowest first; the seed numbers them 10, 20, 30… in the order the hub lists them now, so that one can be slipped between two.
- **`cover` and `boxFront` are two pictures.** The cover is the game's picture on the hub, the box front the front of its box (the games' own Studios call it `boxFront` too). Notion has neither.
- **A rich body, later.** `body` is a list of blocks that holds text only for now; pictures and the like are more kinds of block in that list, added with no migration.

## The seed (`platform/content`)

It imports the Notion Games rows as the hub listed them before PLT-90 (`platform/content/notion/` is its own copy of the reader, `readNotionGames`, then `hubOrder` puts the games in the order the hub used to list them in, the live games of the registry first), so the first numbers of `order` are the hub's old list. Like the games' seeds (`docs/content-pipeline.md`, which holds the guarantees) it writes **drafts only**, in one transaction, fills what is empty and keeps what was edited in the Studio, reads the dataset back to check that running it again does nothing, and never publishes: the owner publishes in the Studio.

| Document | Made from |
| --- | --- |
| `game-<slug>` | each game of the catalog: the name, the slug, the figures, `bggId`, `releaseDate`, the categories, the publishers, the credits, the English description, the links to the rules, `related` (both ways: the row's own links first, then the games that name it). A standalone expansion (Flip 7 Vengeance) is a `reimplementation` of its base game; every other game is a `game` |
| `expansion-<slug>` | each expansion of the source (`NotionExpansion`, `buildExpansions`): the figures, the credits, the English description and the links to the rules as a game's (`sharedFields`), and its `baseGame`, written like the other references and left out when the base game is not in the catalog. **None is seeded yet**: the Notion reader only counts the 25 rows that have a `Parent item` (and are not standalone expansions), so `SeedSource.expansions` is empty in a real run; the builder, its ids and its tests are there for the day the reader gives them. An expansion has no art slot (Notion holds no cover or box front); its people and publishers get their documents like a game's |
| `creator-<name>` | each person in a `Designers` or `Artists` cell: the people of `Designers` get the role Designer, those of `Artists` the role Artist, and a person in both cells of one game has both roles on one line. The other seven roles have no source in Notion: they are filled in the Studio |
| `publisher-<name>` | each publisher a game lists, with its website |
| `category-<name>` | each tag a game lists |

- The id of a person, a publisher or a category is made of its name, accents and capitals gone (`creator-emilie-menard`); two names that make one id stop the seed, to be settled in Notion. The expansions of the Notion rows (25 today) are not imported, and neither are `Relation kind`, the fonts, the Creators' `Photo` and `Website` (empty today), the Editors' `Country`, the descriptions in the other languages, and a game's cover and box, which Notion has not.
- Only the documents of these types are the seed's (the four above, and `expansion` once the source gives some): a game made by hand in the Studio is reported as "in the dataset but not in the seed" and left alone, and the editorial documents are written in the Studio.
- **Token and run**, from the repo root: `SANITY_API_WRITE_TOKEN_PLATFORM` (an Editor token of the project, in `apps/web/.env.local` only: it is not pushed to Vercel, since the hub reads the public dataset with no token) and `NOTION_API_KEY` (a read-only Notion integration connected to the Games, Editors, Creators and Games Development databases, its secret starts with `ntn_`; `pnpm setup-env` no longer writes it, so it goes in `apps/web/.env.local` by hand, or in the environment). `pnpm --filter @pbgo/platform content:seed --dry-run` says what would change, `--apply` writes the drafts. The options are the ones of the pipeline (`--only`, `--overwrite`, `--verbose`).

### The pictures: a second step

Notion hosts the pictures (the games' logo, icon and background, a publisher's logo) behind links that expire after about an hour, so they are copied in two commands, not one:

1. `pnpm --filter @pbgo/platform content:art --out <folder>` reads the links fresh and downloads the pictures the rows hold into a folder **outside the repo** (say `~/Companies/PBGO/assets/platform`), named after the slot each fills: `game-<slug>-logo.png`, `game-<slug>-icon`, `game-<slug>-background`, `publisher-<name>-logo`. A dry run by default, `--apply` downloads; a file that is already there is left as it is. A picture of a kind the art step does not read (a GIF, an icon file) is reported: convert it and put it in the folder by hand. A cell that holds something that is not a picture (a video, say) is reported as "not a picture" and left out.
2. `pnpm --filter @pbgo/platform content:seed --art-dir <folder>` normalizes the pictures (WebP, an SVG is kept), uploads those the dataset does not hold and places them; `--apply` to write. A picture never carries a credit: the sources are unknown.

## Publishing

The seed never publishes. In the Studio, publish the creators, publishers and categories first, then the games, then the expansions (see the references above): the Studio's Publish makes the references strong. A publication made another way (a script, the Sanity MCP) has to do what the Studio does, or the references stay weak, which the hub reads just as well but which keeps nothing from being deleted: drop `_strengthenOnPublish` from each reference, and `_weak` with it unless the mark says the field is weak for good (`related`), create the published document and delete the draft, all in one transaction. That is how the 170 documents of the first seed were published (2026-10-30, on the owner's word), after a dry run that checks that no strong reference points at a document that would not be published. Once published, a document is public (a public dataset: its published documents can be read with no token). A game that is published shows on the hub within about eleven minutes (Sanity's CDN keeps an answer a minute, the hub's cache ten), and `pnpm catalog:list` says what the project answers right now; a draft is never on the hub, and neither is an expansion: the hub reads `game` documents only.

## Moving the expansions made as games (PLT-102)

Before the `expansion` type an expansion was a `game` of kind `expansion`. The one that was made that way, the draft `drafts.game-courtisans-diplomats-confidants` (2026-11-02), is moved by `pnpm --filter @pbgo/platform content:migrate-expansions` (`platform/content/migrate-expansions.ts`; the plan is `migration/expansions.ts`, the dataset side `migration/http-store.ts`, a plain `fetch` of the project's API with the write token):

- For each draft `game` of kind `expansion` it creates the draft `expansion-<slug>` (for this one `expansion-courtisans-diplomats-confidants`, the id the ticket names) and deletes the game draft, **in one transaction**. The fields the two types share are copied as they stand; the references are written again by the rules above (weak, marked to be made strong on publication); the kind is the only thing left behind. It publishes nothing.
- It is a dry run unless `--apply` is given. The dry run prints exactly what is created, deleted, moved and left behind, with the new document in full; `--apply` writes, then reads the dataset back and says if it is not what the plan said.
- It moves drafts only, and stops without writing anything on what it could not move without losing or guessing: a published game of that kind (or one in a release), a field an expansion has no place for (an `order`, a `logo`… with a value), a draft with no base game, a credit or a publisher that points at nothing, a reference to a document that is not there or of another type, an expansion that exists already, two drafts of one slug. It lists every problem at once.
- Run it when the owner says so, after the Studio with the `expansion` type is deployed (`deploy:studio`, also the owner's word), with the game draft closed in the Studio. Then the owner publishes the expansion in the Studio: its base game, its creators and its publisher are published already. It needs `SANITY_API_WRITE_TOKEN_PLATFORM` like the seed. Neither the merge of PLT-102 nor the seed does any of this.

## What reads it

The hub (PLT-90): `readSanityGames` of `@pbgo/catalog` reads the published documents with the public API and one GROQ query that follows the references (`categories[]->name`, `creators[]{ "name": creator->name, roles }`, `publishers[]->{ name, "url": website }`, `related[]->slug.current`), and `docs/catalog.md` says what the hub makes of the answer: the games in `order` (the live games of the registry first), the credits one line per role, pictures from the image CDN, English descriptions. A field of a game maps to the hub's `CatalogGame` one by one: `playersMin` and `playersMax` are `players`, `minAge` is `age`, `description` is `descriptions.en`, `creators` keep their roles. An expansion is not a `game`, so that query never returns one; a `reimplementation` is a game and is listed. (The reader still counts and leaves out a `game` whose `kind` is the former `expansion`: none is left once the migration above has run.) Whatever shows the expansions of a game reads `_type == "expansion"` and follows `baseGame->slug.current`; their pictures are `cover` and `boxFront`, and they have no `order`. What the hub does not read yet: the tagline, the cover and the box front, the people's photos, websites and BGG ids, the publishers' logos and BGG ids, the categories' icons, colors and descriptions, and the editorial documents.
