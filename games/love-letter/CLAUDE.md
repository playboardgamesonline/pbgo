# Love Letter · Play Board Games Online

Love Letter, served by `apps/web` at `/love-letter`. The binding exports the engine (`GAME`), so outside production a table can be opened and started, and its board draws the table (the plates, the cards, the deck, the tokens) from the view and a player can play their turn on it (a card, a player, a character, a Chancellor's keep, the deal); what the others do is told (the cards come in one after the other, a Priest's look, a Baron's comparison, a King's trade and a Guard's guess are shown, the history says it), and the edges are handled (a player who is out, the next round dealt by another player, the end of the game). The board is written and the game is live and indexable (`live: true` and `indexable: true` in the registry, set on 2026-11-23: the `game-go-live` stage, GAM-57), so the hub lists it and `/love-letter` shows its page. 2 to 6 players, the 2025 edition (Z-Man Games). Left: the Spanish and German texts (English and French are published; the board reads the English where another language has none), the 80-character description of its own `settings` (the Launch list of the gamemaster flags it; the hub reads the catalog's), and the owner's reading of the French card texts, which an agent drafted. The rules spec is `docs/games/love-letter/rules.md`.

## Engine (GAM-55)
Written from the validated spec in `packages/engine/src`, with the files and the order of `docs/engine/04-build-a-game/03-writing-the-engine.md`. Skyjo's engine was the model for a game of rounds and turns, Exploding Kittens' for the private entries of the log. What follows is the plan the owner approved, brought up to what the code does. Time: none. Options: none. Aids: none. 2 to 6 players. No case came up while coding that the booklet and the nine readings above leave open.

### Rules as coded (booklet pages as printed)
- **The box** (p. 2): 21 character cards of ten characters valued 0 to 9: Spy 0 ×2, Guard 1 ×6, Priest 2 ×2, Baron 3 ×2, Handmaid 4 ×2, Prince 5 ×2, Chancellor 6 ×2, King 7, Countess 8, Princess 9. The keys are the Studio's ten, as they are, append only. The value decides every comparison, the Spy being the lowest (pp. 2, 6, 12)
- **A round** (p. 3, again p. 7): the 21 cards are shuffled, one is set aside face down, three more face up at two players, and each player gets one. The deck then holds 15, 17, 16, 15 and 14 cards at 2 to 6 players
- **A turn** (p. 4): draw the top card, play one of the two and resolve its effect. The card played stays face up in the player's play area, the other stays in hand. Clockwise, a player out of the round is skipped (p. 5). Nothing is played at the same time
- **Choosing a player** (pp. 5, 11): a card that asks for another player can pick anyone in the round but its own player and a player a Handmaid protects; the Prince can pick any player, its own included. When every other player in the round is protected, the Guard, Priest, Baron and King are played with no effect and the Prince must pick its own player. A card played with no effect is still played
- **Out of the round** (pp. 5, 8, 10, 12): by playing or discarding the Princess, by a Guard that names the card held, by the lower card of a Baron's comparison. The hand is discarded face up, no effect is resolved, the player cannot be chosen and their turn is skipped
- **The characters** (pp. 8–13): Princess, out when played or discarded for any reason. Countess, no effect; she must be played when the other card of the turn is the King or a Prince, and the other card is not shown (the three cards of a Chancellor are free). King, trade hands with another player. Chancellor, draw two cards (one if the deck holds one, none: played with no effect), keep one of the cards in hand and put the others under the deck face down, in an order the player picks, shown to nobody. Prince, any player discards their hand face up with no effect and draws a new card, the set-aside card when the deck is empty; a Princess discarded that way is out and draws nothing. Handmaid, nobody else can choose the player until the start of their next turn. Baron, compare hands with another player, the lower value is out and a tie spares both. Priest, look at another player's hand. Guard, name a character other than the Guard: another player who holds it is out. Spy, no effect
- **End of a round** (p. 6): at once when one player is left (they win, nothing is revealed), else after any turn that leaves the deck empty: the players still in reveal their hands, the highest value wins and a tie gives every tied player the win. A winner gains one favor token
- **The Spy** (p. 13): at the end of a round, the only player still in the round who played or discarded a Spy gains one more token, never more than one, and it is not a win. A Spy still in a hand was neither played nor discarded
- **End of the game** (p. 7): when a player holds 6, 5, 4, 3 or 3 tokens at 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6 players; several can win together. Else a new round is dealt and the winner of the last one takes the first turn
- The two rule mistakes of p. 13 cannot happen: the engine answers a Guard truthfully and refuses a play that breaks the Countess's rule

### What the booklet leaves open
Validated with the spec on 2026-11-18, coded as read:
1. First turn of the first round: a seat drawn from the seed (the booklet says the player who last wrote a letter, pp. 3, 16). Later rounds: the winner of the last round, one of the tied winners drawn when there are several (p. 7)
2. A Spy of a player who is out counts for nothing, for them and for the others: only the players still in the round at its end are looked at (p. 13)
3. The cards a Chancellor puts under the deck are neither played nor discarded: a Princess there knocks nobody out, a Spy there does not count. With one card left in the deck the card returned also goes into the deck, hidden (p. 10)
4. The Chancellor with one card left in the deck: the returned card is in the deck, so the round goes on and ends after the next turn. With none left, no effect, and the round ends after the turn (pp. 6, 10)
5. A new round keeps only the favor tokens: all 21 cards are shuffled again, every player is back in the round, no Handmaid protects anybody (pp. 5, 7, 11)
6. Several players reaching the number together: all the tokens of the round are gained first, then every player who holds at least the number wins and shares the win, whatever their count above it. Every other player loses, no places (p. 7)

Validated with the plan (2026-11-18, the owner accepted every default; the booklet says nothing about them):
7. The draw is part of the turn: the engine draws for a player the moment the turn comes to them, so a player on turn holds two cards and there is no `draw` action (p. 4 says "draw one card", with no choice in it)
8. Between two rounds the table waits for the player who takes the first turn to press Deal (`deal`), as Flip 7 and Skyjo do, so that the finished round stays on the table: the hands revealed, who won, the Spy's token. An absent player is dealt for
9. The 13 favor tokens of the box are a supply, not a rule: the engine counts tokens with no limit (4 players at 3 tokens each plus one round already make 13, a Spy's token or a tie make more; the booklet is silent)

### Who sees what
- Hands: their owner only, as faces. The others get a count (1 between turns, 2 on the turn, 3 while a Chancellor is read). After a round that ended on a comparison, the hands of the players still in are public
- The deck: its size, never a card nor the order. The card set aside face down: that there is one, never which. At two players the three set aside face up are public
- The play areas, who is out, who is protected, the favor tokens: public
- Guard: the guess, the target and hit or miss are public; a hit lays the target's hand face up for all. Priest: the target's card goes to the Priest's player only. Baron: both cards go to the two players only, and the one who loses lays their hand face up for all (so a tie shows nothing). King: after the trade both players know both cards. Prince: the discard is public, the card drawn goes to its player only. Chancellor: the cards drawn and the cards put back go to their player only. Countess: only her player's view says that she is forced
- How a private reveal travels: `step` returns the entry with `to` (who reads it in full) and `masked` (what everyone else reads: the fact and never the card), the platform keeps it (`game_log`) and each player is sent `viewLog` of it (`docs/engine/01-the-game/10-views.md`, "Per-viewer log"). The state keeps no memory of what was shown, and the view none either: a player reads it in the last 60 lines of their log (see Friction). In a private entry a card that is still in a hidden place after the move (another player's hand, the deck) is named by its key, never by its id, so that no id of a hidden card reaches a log. A card that its reader holds after the move may carry its id (the card drawn, the cards a Chancellor kept), and so may a card the Chancellor put back, whose old id no longer exists once it is reissued; the cards of the player's own hand are in `me.hand`

### State, lean: ids and numbers
```ts
type State = {
  options: OptionValues      // none
  seats: string[]            // player ids, clockwise
  ids: number[]              // opaque ids, aligned with CATALOGUE (21)
  rng: number                // saved stream: the shuffles, the pick of a first player
  idsRng: number             // side stream: the new ids of the cards a Chancellor puts back
  round: number              // the round going on, the last one between two rounds
  turn: number               // the seat that plays now, or that deals between two rounds
  stage: Stage
  deck: number[]             // top first, hidden
  aside: number | null       // the card set aside face down, hidden; null once a Prince's target took it
  faceUp: number[]           // at two players, the three set aside face up
  hands: number[][]          // by seat: 1 card, 2 on the turn, 3 with a Chancellor, 0 once out
  areas: number[][]          // by seat: the cards played or discarded this round, oldest first
  out: boolean[]             // by seat
  shielded: boolean[]        // by seat: a Handmaid protects them until the start of their next turn
  won: number[]              // by seat: rounds won, a token each
  spies: number[]            // by seat: Spy tokens
}
type Stage =
  | { kind: "play" }         // `turn` plays one of their two cards
  | { kind: "keep" }         // `turn` played a Chancellor: keeps one card, puts the others under the deck
  | { kind: "between"; revealed: boolean; winners: number[]; spy: number | null }  // the round is over, `turn` deals the next one (nobody once the game is over)
```
Derived, never stored: the tokens of a seat (`won + spies`), `tokensToWin`, `isOver`, `progress`, `waitingFor`, who can be chosen, what can be played. Every id is in exactly one place (deck, aside, faceUp, a hand or an area); `out` means an empty hand; no id changes except the cards a Chancellor puts back (`reissueIds`: they were seen, then go back into hiding). The shuffle of a round and the pick of a first player come from `rng`, carried from step to step; every order comes from an array

### How a game goes
- Who is waited for: one player at a time. The player of the turn (`play`, then `keep` after a Chancellor), between two rounds the player who takes the first turn, nobody once the game is over, never a player who is out. Nothing is simultaneous
- `setup` seeds the streams, draws the first player and deals round 1 (`setupStep`: the log opens with it). A turn starts by drawing (`startTurn`: the protection of the player ends, the top card joins their hand, a private entry), then waits for `play`
- `play { card, target?, guess? }` is checked in this order: the game is not over, the action is well formed, the game waits for the player (`NOT_YOUR_TURN`), the stage is `play` (`INVALID_PHASE`), the card is in their hand (`UNKNOWN_CARD`), the Countess rule (`MUST_PLAY_COUNTESS`), then the target (`INVALID_TARGET`: it must be one of `targetsOf`, and none may be given when there is none) and, for the Guard with a target, the guess (`INVALID_CHOICE`: one of the nine other keys). The card goes to the area, its effect runs, then the turn ends
- The turn ends (`endTurn`) in this order: one player left in the round, the round ends at once; the deck empty, the round ends on a comparison; else the next player still in draws. The Chancellor stops the turn at the stage `keep` until `keep { card, bottom }`: `bottom` lists the cards that go under the deck, the first one first, so the last of them is the last card of the deck
- A round ends (`endRound`): the hands are revealed on a comparison, the winners and the Spy's token are written, the next first player is picked (the winner, or one of the tied winners by `rng`, which is drawn from only when there are several), the stage becomes `between`. A winner of the game is every player at or above `tokensToWin`: the game is over, nobody is waited for
- `deal` (the player who takes the first turn) starts the next round: all 21 cards shuffled, one aside, three face up at two players, one card each, the first turn drawn. Every turn takes one card from the deck for good (a Chancellor gives back what it took), so a round always ends, and every round gives a token, so a game always ends

### Actions and refusals
`CLIENT_ACTIONS` = `play`, `keep`, `deal` (`satisfies` the `Action` union). The codes are in `LOVE_LETTER_ERROR_CODES`: the shared ones (`INVALID_PLAYERS`, `GAME_OVER`, `NOT_YOUR_TURN`, `NOT_WAITING`, `INVALID_ACTION`, `INVALID_PHASE`, `UNKNOWN_CARD`, `INVALID_TARGET`, `INVALID_CHOICE`) and one of the game's own, `MUST_PLAY_COUNTESS`, which the binding lists in `ERROR_CODES` and whose message is written in Sanity. An extra field of an action is ignored; a missing or mistyped one is `INVALID_ACTION`, or the code of its field

### Events and the view
- Events, plain data (`LoveLetterEvent`; the exact union is written with the code): `round` (number, first player, the faces set aside), `draw` (private: the card), `play`, `guess` (target, character named, hit), `look` (Priest, private: the card), `compare` (Baron, private to the two: both cards), `protect`, `discard` (a Prince's target, public), `out` (the card laid face up and the cause), `swap` (King, private to the two: both cards), `chancellor` and `bottom` (private: the cards drawn, the cards put back), `fizzled` (a card played with no effect: nobody to pick, or a Chancellor with the deck empty), `reveal` (the hands at the end of a round), `end` (how, who won, the Spy's token), `over`. Each private entry has a `masked` version with the fact and never the card
- `PlayerView`: `me` (`null` for a spectator; else the hand as faces, the card drawn this turn last, and `playable`: the ids that may be played now, so the Countess rule lives in the engine only), `players` (by seat: `cards` in hand, `hand` shown after a comparison, `area`, `out`, `shielded`, `tokens`), `standings` (tokens by seat), `deck` (size), `aside` (0 or 1), `faceUp`, `round`, `target` (tokens to win), `phase` (`turn` with `targets`, the other players a card can choose now, in seat order, the same for every viewer; `keep`; `between` with `next`, `winners`, `spy` and `revealed`; or `over`) and `results`
- `Results`: winners are rank 1, every other player shares the next rank (the booklet gives no places), `total` is the player's tokens, `detail` has three lines (`scoreLines`: `rounds`, `won`, `spy`: the rounds played, the rounds won, the Spy's tokens). No word is written in the engine: the three `score.<key>` texts come in the game's Sanity after the engine

### Absent player
`autoPlayFor` plays a whole turn, never a click: it plays the card of the lowest value that may be played (the Princess last), picks the first player who can be chosen after them clockwise and names the first character that is not the Guard; after a Chancellor it keeps the card of the highest value and puts the others back in the order of the hand; between two rounds it deals. Deterministic, no random number. `NOT_WAITING` when the game is not waiting for them

### Friction (found while planning and coding)
- A private reveal and the contract suite: `secretsFor(state, viewer)` knows the state only, and who was shown what is not in it. A card that the Priest, the Baron or the King showed is still in another hand, so an entry that names its id to the player it is for reads as "the log shows what its viewer must not know" (`checkEntries` of `packages/engine-kit/src/conformance.ts`). The private entries therefore name a card by its key, which a check that looks for numbers can never find: the suite cannot prove that a masked entry hides it, so `cards.test.ts` and `chancellor.test.ts` do by hand. Checked with a deliberately broken version: a Priest whose entry names the card to the whole table (no `to`, no `masked`) passes the contract suite and the simulation and fails those tests alone
- What a viewer was shown is nowhere but the log: the state and the view keep no `known`, so a player who looked at a card reads it in the last 60 lines of their log, and a board that wants a mark by the opponent's hand has to rebuild it from those lines (a card leaves a hand by a play, a swap, a discard). Exploding Kittens keeps a `known` for the top of its pile; here it would be an aid, and none is coded
- The machinery of a step and the helpers around it were written again in each engine that says what happens; they are the kit's now (`openStep`, `closeStep` and `noteEvent`, `seatOf`, `playerAt` and `nextSeat`, `removeFrom`, `keyOfId`, `faceOfId` and `facesOfIds`, and the type `CardFace`, which `Face` of `types.ts` narrows to the ten characters), and so is the rig of the takeover test (`takeoverRig` of core). `open` of `machine.ts` gives the kit's `openStep` this game's types, and `close` is `closeStep`. What stays in the tests is this game's: the rigging of a table from the names of the cards (`layout`), the invariants of a state (`problems`), `refusal`, `seedOf`, `players` and the random player
- The junk that the contract suite sends is a fixed list of field names (`checkJunk` of `conformance.ts`: `card`, `cardId`, `amount`, `position`, `target`, `targetId`, `value`, `choice`, `cards`): an action whose fields are named otherwise (`guess` and `bottom` here) gets none, so `simulation.test.ts` sends its own
- `roundsOutcome` and `ranks` cannot say this game's end: a player at or above the target wins together with every other player at or above it whatever the counts, and the others all share one rank after them. The conformance suite wants every winner at rank 1, so `ranks()` of the tokens is out; the end is written in the game, a few lines
- `cleanChoices` returned a choice in the order of the options, so the Chancellor's order of two cards (the point of the choice) was lost and `keep` checked `bottom` by hand; the kit's rules now have `ordered`, and `keep` takes `bottom` with `cleanChoices(bottom, others, { ordered: true, min })`, refusing with `INVALID_CHOICE` as before
- The pages of the engine docs disagree on face-down cards: "Opaque ids" says a card seen face down is shown under an id, "Cards" says a hand is a count, never a list of ids, and of the engines read here only Courtisans shows a face-down card under an id (its hidden spy), where Flip 7, Skyjo and Exploding Kittens give counts. Love Letter follows the counts, so its ids only name cards that are face up or in their owner's hand, and the only use of `reissueIds` is the two cards a Chancellor puts back

### A turn on the table
- The turn starts by itself: the engine draws, the card flies from the deck to the hand (face up for the viewer, a back for the others) and the crown moves; the shared announcement says "your turn" to the viewer
- The viewer presses a card of their hand. A card that chooses nobody (Spy, Handmaid, Countess, Princess, Chancellor) is played at once. A card that chooses another player (Guard, Priest, Baron, King) is picked: it comes out of the hand and lies in front of the viewer (`selected`), and the cards of each player of `phase.targets` are lit; pressing them plays it, but for the Guard, which goes on to the nine characters other than the Guard as small faces, and pressing one plays it. A Prince is held the same way and lights those cards and the viewer's own. When nobody can be chosen (the others are out or protected) the Guard, Priest, Baron and King are played at once with no effect, and the Prince has the viewer's own cards alone. At every step the ticker says what to do (`promptOf`)
- A card that is picked sends nothing, so a mistake is undone by pressing the card again, or another card, or the empty table (a Prince's own area answers a press, so the empty table is the way back for a Prince); a card that is played at once is played by the press, since an object that is pressed does what it names. The engine decides and the board offers: `me.playable` for the cards, `phase.targets` for the players; a refusal that gets through (`MUST_PLAY_COUNTESS`, `INVALID_TARGET`) is a toast
- A Chancellor: the three cards (two when the deck held one) lie in the hand and the ticker says to keep one; pressing a card keeps it: with two cards the move is sent, with three the card kept lifts and the ticker says to press the card that goes under the deck first, which sends `keep { card, bottom }` with the other card last (the last card of the deck). The card kept is put back by pressing it again
- A visitor with no seat never reaches a started table (the platform's page says the game has started, though its API would give a view with no seat): the board still draws such a view, which the admin's preview serves, with the seats of a table with one more player and nothing to press (`layout.test.ts` holds the layouts from two to five seats; six has no seventh seat for the empty chair)
- Between two rounds the finished round stays on the table until the player who takes the first turn presses Deal (the others read who deals): the hands laid face up when the round ended on a comparison, the winners and the Spy's token said in words, the new tokens added to their stacks. A player who is out reads it in the ticker, and the cards they played are in the middle with the others'. An absent player is dealt for and played for (the platform)
- What the others do is told by the log (`useGameLog`, the last 60 lines as the viewer reads them): a card that is played, discarded or laid down flies from its player's hand to its place in the row of the middle (a Spy, and the Handmaid that protects, stay in front of their player: they fly there), a card drawn flies from the deck to the hand (to the viewer's hand on the screen, or as the last back of another player's), a Priest's look, a Baron's comparison and a King's trade are shown above the columns for the time it takes to read them, as faces to the players the log gives them to and as backs to the others (the history of the ticker says what happened); a Guard's guess shows the character it named, whoever reads it. The view is drawn at once and what the move ends with (the announcements, the end screen, the controls of the viewer) waits for it to play out (`pacing.ts`, with the same shape as Flip 7's); a table that is opened or reloaded plays nothing, but for the deals, which come from the deck, and reduced motion shows nothing at all. The ticker's history has a sentence for each event, the same for the players the entry is for and for the others with the card left out

### The look
- The table is the surface the cards lie on: one flat dark colour, endless, with **no grain, no noise and no picture**, and a pool of light added to it that eases away to nothing: the mat is data, `surface={{ color: surfaceDark, light: { center: surface, radius: 22 } }}` of the kit's `Table`, which lays the colour, the pool and its dither, so the game paints and keeps no texture of its own. The colours are the settings' (`surface` in the middle, `surfaceDark` at the edge and beyond, `background`), replaced by what is saved in `/gamemaster/love-letter`. The defaults of the binding are a wine, the hue of the medallions and of the card back, to be made darker and more saturated and shown to the owner
- The lettering is the owner's two fonts, uploaded to the game's settings: Cabazon Regular (Jim Parkinson, a blackletter) for the display font and Minion Pro Regular (Adobe, an old-style serif) for the body font, both WOFF2. The binding names no font (`SETTINGS_DEFAULTS` holds colours only), so the page asks no font service for anything: no Google Fonts. The display font is the names on the faces and on the plates, the status words (Out, Protected, won the round), the Deal button and the counts the board draws (the cards left in the deck); the body font is the card texts; the count of favor tokens on a plate (`2 / 4`) is the kit's `Counter`, which takes no font, so it stays in the platform's own. The lettering of the table is the kit's (`useLettering`: the display and the body font of the uploaded files, with the kit's own as the fallback, and the bold of the table), which the board gives its plates and its faces and the kit's buttons and counts take by themselves. Both fonts have one weight and the page declares them at 100 to 900 (`fontFaceCss`), so the table's weight 700 draws the regular glyphs and never a false bold. The web licences of Cabazon and Minion Pro are not verified, which is the owner's call; where the files come from is in "The fonts" of `~/Companies/PBGO/assets/love-letter/SOURCES.md`. What the lettering is sized by is measured in the browser, never assumed: the card text is the largest size that fits its plate with its lines made even (`setText` and `balance`: no last line of one word)
- A face is painted on a canvas (`paintFace`): the Sanity picture over the whole rectangle, then the name centred in the banner and the card text centred in its plate, in the language asked, the geometry of "Pictures" (layouts A and B; 12 letters, 130 or 90 characters at most; the text set smaller when it overflows), after `document.fonts` has loaded the two fonts so that a fallback is never frozen into a texture. A face that Sanity lacks is a `standIn` with `[missing: <key>]`
- A card is 1.2 by 1.674 world units (the proportions of the pictures, 38:53), every card of the table; the viewer's hand, held on the screen, is bigger. A card says what it is with its `state` (`interactive`, `selected`, `disabled`), which the table draws, and a player protected by a Handmaid is told in the word under their name
- A favor token is a plain disc `Piece` in the settings' `accent` until the table can build a piece from the model (see Gaps)
- Sounds (`SOUNDS` of the binding, `src/lib/sounds.ts`): the cards play `hover`, `select`, `slide` and `place` by themselves, the announcements `turn` and `victory`, the platform's buttons `click`, all declared from `SHARED_EFFECTS`, and a line of the log makes the one sound no card shows, `eliminate` for a player knocked out of the round (`useLogSounds` of the screen). The files are the owner's, uploaded in Gamemaster › Audio: an effect with no file stays silent
- Skin and in-game theme (`DEFAULT_SKIN`, `UI_THEME`): six player colours that read on the wine table and are none of the gold accent; calm motion (fades and short slides, no confetti: a game of reading faces and deducing), round avatars, the announcements in the display font over the table's own colour, the game over in the game's colours
- The board holds no hex colour: the Deal button takes the `primary` tone of the game's theme and the cards the colours of `UI_THEME.card`. The camera is fitted to the window (`fitFor` in `camera.ts`, which asks `fitCamera`), the scene always in the middle, the controls in the middle band; `PHOTO` is the share photo's camera. The banner of the first visit may cover the lower edge of the viewer's line: the check measures what lies over each control. The camera is given the plates, the play areas and the middle to centre on, and the area of each seat to bring into the window (the hands that stand are in it, with `REACH_MARGIN` more round it, since a card that stands is seen a little higher than the flat model of the fit puts it): `camera.test.ts` checks that the window is filled and the platform's boxes keep off the table

### What is written
- The words of the board are the Board tab of `texts`, as Flip 7's (`createTexts` with a tab, `WORD_FIELDS` of `src/lib/words.ts`, `BOARD_WORDS` of the seed, `board-words.test.ts` in the Studio and in the seed keeping the three lists equal). About thirty-five, English first, French written for the owner to read, Spanish and German left empty as for the card texts (the board shows the English): what the ticker says to do (choose a card, choose a player, choose any player, name a character, you must play the Countess, keep one card, press the card that goes under the deck first), that the viewer is out of the round, the Deal button, the spoken names of a card (Play, Keep) and of a player's area, the status words (Out, Protected, won the round), the round and the tokens that win for the ticker, the announcements that are the game's own, and the sentences of the history, one for each event of the log, a full one and a masked one where the entry is private (look, compare, swap, chancellor, bottom). A sentence says what the player did and leaves out who, since the ticker heads each turn with the name
- With the binding, the seed gets the message of `MUST_PLAY_COUNTESS` and the three score lines (`score.rounds`, `score.won`, `score.spy`), in French and English, flagged for the owner to read. A write to the game's Sanity is a dry run first and then the owner's yes; publishing is the owner's

### What the board asks of Sanity
`loadGameData` (server) gives the board one catalog, read through the public API and cached 60 s: for each character its face (the picture, served as it is), its name and its card text in the language asked (else English, else `[missing: <key>]`), `game.cardBack`, the URL of `favorTokenModel` (read by nothing until a piece can be made from it) and the words. A picture it lacks is `null` and the table draws a stand-in; the admin's preview, which gives no data, reads every word as missing. The colours and fonts are the settings'; the player colours are the skin's.

### Board friction (for the engine lane; nothing is written in Linear)
- A name of twelve capitals in the display font (a W is 0.89 of the height of the lettering) was wider than the room a plate is given, and the plate had no fit, so a long name stuck out of its seat instead of shrinking; the kit's plate now draws a name wider than `SEAT_SETTINGS.nameRoom` smaller (`maxWidth` of `TableText`), and the platform cuts a name at twelve characters
- No glTF `Piece`: the favor token is a plain gold disc meanwhile (the model is in the game's Sanity)
- No mark for a card that only some viewers have seen (a Priest's, a Baron's or a King's reveal): the board tells it as a sequence from the log and keeps no mark by the opponent's hand
- A face lettered over a blank plate (wrap a text into a box, shrink it until it fits, after the fonts have loaded) has no helper: it is written in the game, as Flip 7's `fitPrompt` is
- A `Card` that can be pressed has a keyboard stand-in (`ariaLabel`: Play or Keep and the name of the character): the cards of the hand, the nine faces of a Guard's guess, the players' areas and the Deal button are reachable with Tab
- An ordered choice was missing from the contract (`cleanChoices` lost the order): it is the `ordered` rule now, and the board sends `bottom` as the engine checks it
- A text on the table is 6 to 10 px tall at 1440 by 900 (the face's text is 3 to 8 px): anything that must be read goes through the lens of the page (a canvas drawn again at the size of the page), which the table kit has no block for
- The kit's `Counter` took no font, so a count on the table was drawn in the platform's own font while every other lettering of the table was in the game's: it writes in the table's display font now (`useLettering`) and takes a `font`
- Two presses make a move (a card, then the control that sends it) and `pnpm check:board` expected every press to move the table: a `local` press was added to the tool
- `pnpm check:board` presses the first control the board offers, the moment it is offered: a card that is still coming in from the deck is pressed where it no longer is (the control at its place in the line is another card's), and a card pressed while the page still sends the last move is ignored, which a `local` press never notices. The config of the game holds its own guards (`still`: the controls have not moved since the last look, and a pause before a card is picked, and a line of controls as long as the cards that can be pressed); a tool that waited for the controls to be at rest would serve every game
- Core sets its headings in capitals in the display font (the announcement, the round line of the ticker, the label of the players list, the names under the podium), and blackletter capitals read badly: "IT'S YOUR TURN" shows an I that looks like a Z with a tail. It is core's `uppercase`, not worked around in the game. The figures are fine: Cabazon's are old-style (a 1 has a flag and a foot and cannot be taken for an I, a 3 and a 4 drop below the line, a 0 is a plain oval), in the players list, the ticker and the scores of the end screen alike
- The end screen writes a score as points in places the game cannot reach (`scoreText` of core's `game-over.ts`, "4 pts" under a podium), where the score of this game is favor tokens: the game sets the shared label `pointsShort` of its Copy to tokens (the seed's `ui_game`), which the announcement and the share image read
- The Launch list of the gamemaster flags "Description" for a game whose `settings` has none, and the seed's `settings` holds the credits only (the catalog's description is English only): not a text of the board, left to `game-go-live`

## Making it playable
