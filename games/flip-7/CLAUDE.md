# Flip 7 · Play Board Games Online

Flip 7 (Eric Olsen, The OP Games), served by `apps/web` at `/flip-7`. The engine is written (ruleset 3.1 of the booklet), so is the Sanity content, and the binding exports `GAME` and the board: a player hits by pressing the draw pile, stays and deals with the papers on the table and gives an action card by pressing a player's plate, and the history, the announcements and the game over are drawn (what is still to do is in "Making it playable"). Outside production a table can be opened and played; in production the home page says "coming soon" and the table pages answer 404 until `live` is set in the registry. Listed nowhere until then. 3 to 8 players, about 20 minutes; the web edition is not official.

## Structure
- `package.json` — `@pbgo-game/flip-7`, the binding package that `@pbgo/games` loads on demand
  - `src/lib/site.ts` — `SLUG`, name, keywords, genres, author, contact, `ERROR_CODES` (the game's own refusal, `CANNOT_STAY`; no tagline, no description: a game's texts live in Sanity only)
  - `src/index.ts` — the binding shared by the server and the client: the engine (`GAME`), the site constants, the default colors (`SETTINGS_DEFAULTS`: the indigo of the table and the yellow, replaced by what is set in `/gamemaster/flip-7`), the skin (`DEFAULT_SKIN` of `lib/skin.ts`: the colors of the seats) and the look of the in-game UI (`UI_THEME` of `lib/ui-theme.ts`: every color a `var(--…)` of the settings, lively motion, round seats)
  - `src/lib/words.ts` — `WORD_FIELDS`, the words of the board (the fields of the Board tab of `texts`), and `fill`, which puts a name, a card or a number in the `{placeholders}` of a word · `src/lib/catalog.ts` — `ClientCatalog`, what the board is given (the picture of each card under its key in the engine's catalogue, the name of each action card, the back, the words) and `MISSING_CATALOG`, the same with a `[missing: <key>]` for every text and no picture, for when Sanity has nothing
  - `src/sanity/` — the query of the board (`queries.ts`), its fetch cached 60 s (`catalog.ts`), the catalog built from it in the language asked (`catalog-client.ts`: a text reads the language asked, else English, else the marker, and is logged once) and the types that `pnpm --filter flip-7-studio typegen` writes (`types.ts`, generated, offline)
  - `src/ui.tsx` — `Logo` and `Game`, loaded with `dynamic` so that the hub carries no three.js · `src/runtime.tsx` — client runtime (styles, `BindingProvider`) · `src/server.ts` — server side: `loadGameData`, which gives the board its catalog
  - `src/components/game/game.tsx` — `Game`, the screen around the table: the announcements (the start, a new round, your turn, a Flip 7, the victory or the defeat), the ticker (the round and the score to reach, and the history of the table grouped by turn) and the game over, all the platform's, and the four intents the table sends (`hit`, `stay`, `deal`, `target`); the Phases and Replay folders of the debug panel
  - `src/components/board/` — the table, the only place that imports `@pbgo/table` and three.js (see The board): `scene.tsx`, `layout.ts` (the knobs and where everything lies, with `layout.test.ts`), `camera.ts` (the camera fitted to the window, with `camera.test.ts`), `viewport.ts` (the size of the window), `textures.ts` (the faces and the back read from the catalog, a stand-in for a picture that is missing, and the gradient of the mat), `pacing.ts` (the pace of a move and its timeline, with `pacing.test.ts`) and `glows.ts`
  - `src/lib/cards.ts` — the name of a card, a figure, `+4`, `x2`, or the name an action card has in Sanity · `src/lib/history.ts` — the sentence of each event of the log and who did it (`sentenceOf`, `actorsOf`) · `src/lib/skin.ts` and `src/lib/ui-theme.ts`, which `src/index.ts` exports
  - `src/conformance.test.ts` (the contract of an engine, played the way the server plays it) · `src/takeover.test.ts` (absent players, and the votes to play for them) · `src/error-codes.test.ts` (the codes the engine refuses with, against `ERROR_CODES`)
- `packages/engine` — `@flip-7/engine`, the whole game: pure TypeScript on the engine-kit helpers, tested with Vitest (see Engine)
- `apps/studio` — `flip-7-studio`, Sanity Studio of the project `ky3exlhe` (https://flip-7.sanity.studio). Its own document types (the three card categories `numberCard`, `actionCard` and `modifierCard`, the `game` singleton and `texts`, the shared Copy document with a Board tab that holds the words of the board) are in `schemaTypes/` (`cards.ts` builds the three card types from one factory), the lists they share with the seed (categories, card keys) in `schemaTypes/constants.ts`, and the guards of the Studio (`studio-forms.test.ts` for the forms, `board-words.test.ts` for the words, `pnpm --filter flip-7-studio test`)
- `content/` — the Sanity content as versioned code (the game's data and config) and the seed script, run by the shared `@pbgo/content-kit` (see Content); `vitest.config.ts` runs its tests and those of `src`

## Engine (`packages/engine/src`)
- `types.ts` — `State`, `Frame`, `Status`, `Action` (`CLIENT_ACTIONS`), the views (`PlayerView`, `PublicPhase`, `PublicSeat`), the events (`Flip7Event`), the error codes (`FLIP7_ERROR_CODES`)
- `catalogue.ts` — `CARDS` (the 22 faces), `DECK` (their copies, 94 cards), `CATALOGUE` (the 94 cards, by face) · `scoring.ts` — the scores of the booklet (`lineScore`, `isFlip7`) · `options.ts` — `OPTIONS`
- `machine.ts` — what every rule is written with: `open` / `close` of a step, the cards and the lines, the scores, `waitingFor`, `targetsOf` · `round.ts` — how a round goes: `receive` (a card turned over), `giveCard`, `endRound`, `startRound`, `run`
- `actions.ts` — `hit`, `stay`, `chooseTarget`, `dealRound` and `step(state, action)`, the one entry point · `setup.ts` — `setupGame` · `autoplay.ts` — `autoPlayFor` · `results.ts` · `view.ts` — `playerView` (carries `standings`, what each seat has banked, for the list of the players of the HUD) · `debug.ts` · `game.ts` — `GAME` (with `scoreLines` and `progress`, the best total over the target) · `index.ts` — the exports (`@pbgo/engine-kit` too)
- `test-utils.ts` — helpers of the tests (`layout` builds a rigged table from the names of the cards), not exported

### How a game goes
- `step(state, action)` gives `{ state, events }`; `GAME.apply` keeps the state and `GAME.step` hands the events (`Flip7LogEntry`) to the platform, which keeps them in the log of the table and sends each player `PublicGame.log` (`useGameLog`, PLT-45). `GAME.autoPlayStep` does it for a player played for, and `GAME.setupStep` for the first round, dealt while the game is set up. The state holds no log and the view none either. Nothing is hidden in Flip 7, so no entry has a `to` or a `masked`
- A step is `open` (a private copy of the state, the stream picked up where the last step left it), a few mutations, `close`. Nothing mutates its input, and there is no `Math.random` nor `Date.now`
- Lean state: ids and numbers only. A card is the opaque id it holds (`ids` of the engine-kit); its face comes from the catalogue, never stored. `deck` and `discard` are top first; every order comes from an array (`seats`, `lines`, the piles), never from the keys of an object (`jsonb`). `lines[seat]` are the cards in front of each player, in the order they came; `status[seat]` is `active`, `stayed`, `frozen` or `busted`
- The round is a stack of `Frame`s that the engine plays by itself until it needs a player (`run`): `deal` (the opening deal), `turn` (a player hits or stays), `flip` (a Flip Three on a player), `target` (a player holds an action card and chooses who gets it). A settled state is either an empty stack (between two rounds) or ends with a `turn` or a `target`: `waitingFor` is always one player, nobody once the game is over
- Between two rounds the next dealer deals (`deal`): the board shows the finished round (the lines stay in front of the players) until then. `isOver` and the winners come from the scores of the finished rounds (`rounds[round][seat]`, `sumRounds` of the engine-kit), never stored
- Everything is public but the order of the draw pile: the view gives its size and the faces it still holds (what the players could count themselves), never the id of one of its cards (tests check every field of the view)

### Rules as coded (booklet, ruleset 3.1)
- 94 cards: twelve 12s, eleven 11s … two 2s, one 1, one 0; +2, +4, +6, +8, +10 and x2 once each; three Freeze, three Flip Three, three Second Chance. 3 to 8 players here (the booklet says 3+). First to 200 at the end of a round wins
- Opening deal: one card face up to each player, the player on the left of the dealer first, the dealer last. An action card pauses the deal until it is played; then the first turn is the player on the left of the dealer
- A turn is one hit or one stay. A hit turns the top card over in front of the player; a number they already hold busts them (nothing scored, out of the round) unless they hold a Second Chance. A stay banks the line and leaves the round; it needs at least one card in front of the player
- Action cards go to any active player, the holder included; with one possible target there is nothing to choose and the card goes there. Freeze: the target banks and is out. Flip Three: the target flips three cards, one at a time, stopping as soon as they bust or make a Flip 7; every kind of card counts toward the three. Second Chance: kept; a second one is given to another active player who holds none, or discarded when there is nobody
- The round ends when nobody is active any more, or at once on a Flip 7 (seven different numbers, the zero included; modifiers and action cards never count). Score of a line: number cards added, doubled with the x2, then the +N added, then 15 for a Flip 7; busted scores 0
- Next round: the cards of the round go to the discard (not back into the deck), the deck is handed to the left, that player deals. When the deck runs out the discard is shuffled into a new one; cards in front of players stay where they are, busted players' included
- **Where the booklet is silent, the engine decides as follows (to be validated: they come from the usual reading and from third-party rules sites, Dized's rules Q&A and BoardGameArena's help, not from the booklet; the publisher's FAQ, https://theop.games/pages/flip-7-faqs, is the place to settle them). Page numbers are the printed ones:**
  1. The first turn is the player on the left of the dealer (the booklet says "in turn")
  2. An action card dealt in the opening deal is given by the player it was dealt to, to any active player, even one not dealt yet. A player frozen before their deal gets no card; a player given a Flip Three before their deal is still dealt their card afterwards
  3. A Freeze or a Flip Three flipped during a Flip Three waits until the three cards are done, then is played in the order flipped by the flipper, on any active player (they may have frozen themselves in between); if they bust or make a Flip 7 it is not played and stays in front of them. A second Second Chance flipped during a Flip Three is NOT held back (the booklet only holds back Freeze and Flip Three): it is given away at once
  4. A stay is possible with any card in front of the player, a lone Second Chance or x2 included (it banks 0)
  5. A tie for the best total at or above the target plays on: rounds go on until one player is alone at the top (p.12 only says that the player with the most points wins)
  6. When a draw finds no card anywhere, the round ends as if everybody still in had stayed. It takes all 94 cards in front of the players, which a table of 8 cannot hold (7 number cards at most each, 71 cards with every modifier and action card): the rule is there so the game never gets stuck, and `rounds.test.ts` reaches it from a rigged deck
  7. The finished round's lines stay in front of the players until the next deal, which is the new dealer's to do, so the board can show the result
  8. When a Flip 7 ends the round, the players who are still in (neither busted nor stayed) bank their line as if they had stayed, and only a bust scores nothing. The booklet says the round ends "for everyone" (p.1, p.9) and does not say what the others score
  9. A table is 3 to 8 players (owner's ruling, GAM-24): the cover says 3+ and p.3 suggests a second deck above 18, which the engine does not have; the web edition stops at the 8 seats a screen can show, one line of cards each (`MAX_PLAYERS` in `setup.ts`, and `players.max` of the registry). The "party of two or less" challenge (200 points in under 5 rounds, p.12) is not played here; the minimum is `MIN_PLAYERS`

### Absent player
`autoPlayFor` plays the simplest legal step, always the same for the same state (no random number): on a turn the player stays (they bank what they hold), or hits when they have nothing in front of them; a Freeze they hold freezes them; a Flip Three or a Second Chance goes to the next player clockwise who can get it; between two rounds they deal. `NOT_WAITING` when the game is not waiting for them.

### Options (`options.ts`) and words
- `targetScore`: 200 by default (the booklet), 50 to 500 in steps of 50. A lower one makes a shorter game
- The engine names no text. The words of the option (`option.targetScore.label`, `option.targetScore.help`) and of the four lines of the score of a player (`score.rounds`, `score.bestRound`, `score.flip7`, `score.busts`, listed in `scoreLines` of `GAME`) are the `engineTexts` of the game's Sanity `texts`, seeded as drafts by `content/data/texts.ts` (French is the booklet's, English the base, Spanish and German were written by an agent and are for the owner to read); `content/parity/engine.test.ts` keeps the list equal to `engineTextKeys(GAME)`

### Errors
The codes are listed with their meaning in `FLIP7_ERROR_CODES` (`types.ts`). Six of the seven are shared by every game (`SHARED_ERROR_CODES` of the engine-kit) and worded by the platform; `CANNOT_STAY` is the game's own, listed in `ERROR_CODES` of the binding (`error-codes.test.ts` keeps the list and the engine in step). Its message is a draft of the seed (`errorMessages` of `content/data/texts.ts`) for the owner to publish (Gamemaster › Copy › Errors). The board does not offer a stay that the view says is impossible (`canStay`), so a player only meets it through a screen that is out of date. The action route accepts the types of `CLIENT_ACTIONS` only.

### The cards are a contract with the studio
The 22 cards of `CARDS` are filed in the Sanity studio by category, which is the engine's `kind`, one list each: Number (`numberCard`, keys `0` … `12`), Action (`actionCard`: `freeze`, `flip-three`, `second-chance`) and Modifier (`modifierCard`: `plus-2`, `plus-4`, `plus-6`, `plus-8`, `plus-10`, `times-2`). A category lists its cards under the simplest key it can (the figure of a number, not `number-7`), and `engineKeyOf` of `schemaTypes/constants.ts` gives back the key of the catalogue (`number-` and the figure for a number, the key itself for the others). The studio's card (its copies, its visual and, for an action card, its name) is read by that key: a `Face` carries it as its `type`. Append only, never rename. An expansion is more cards, a `DECK` entry each, and a branch in `round.ts` for each new effect; a new `kind` is a new category in the studio (`content/parity/engine.test.ts` fails until it has one).

### Tests
`pnpm --filter @flip-7/engine test` and `typecheck`. One test file per part of the game (catalogue, scoring, setup, deal, turns, cards, rounds, view, autoplay, debug, game), each rule with the page of the booklet it comes from, and `simulation.test.ts` for 72 whole games (3 to 8 players, every size against every kind of table: careful and greedy, with absent players) with the invariants checked after every move: no card lost, the turn goes to the right player, the scores of every round recomputed from the booklet independently, a Flip 7 ends the round, replay from a state stored with its keys in another order, no view shows a card of the pile, junk actions refused, the takeover. The tests were also run against 32 deliberately broken versions of the rules (a modifier added before the doubling, a Second Chance that never saves, a deal that starts with the dealer…): each one fails a test. The contract of an engine, played the way the server plays it with the draw pile as the secret, is run from the binding (`src/conformance.test.ts`, which also holds `ERROR_CODES` against what the engine refuses with)

## Content (Sanity)
25 documents: 22 cards in three categories, one document type each (13 `numberCard` `number-card-<figure>`, 3 `actionCard` `action-card-<key>`, 6 `modifierCard` `modifier-card-<key>`; the category says what the engine calls the `kind`, so a card has no such field), each with, in the order of the Studio form, its English game key (`key`, a dropdown of the keys the engine knows), its place in its category (`order`, so that the Studio lists the cards in the order of the deck), for an action card its name in FR, EN, ES and DE, its copies and last its visual; a card has no Effect or Rule field and no description under a field (what a card does is the engine's, and `schema.test.ts` keeps the form: "the forms of the cards"); the `game` singleton (card back, box, mat: images only, in three tabs, Board, Card backs and Box) and `settings` (credits: Eric Olsen; publisher The OP Games and its link). A number or a modifier is named by the figure printed on it (a 7, a +4, the x2), which its key already says, so it has no name to translate: the Studio lists it under that figure. Only the three action cards, named by words, carry a name in the four languages; the seed gives them their English titles in every language, for the owner to translate in the Studio. There is no `rules` document: the rules are links to PDFs set in `settings` (Gamemaster › Mechanics), and none is set yet, the booklet has no address of its own to link
- The pipeline itself (options, token, plan, art, drafts-only writes, report) is `@pbgo/content-kit` (`packages/content-kit`, shared with the other games); `content/` keeps only the data (`content/data/`: cards, `game`, `settings`, `texts`, the art slots) and the config
- `pnpm --filter @pbgo-game/flip-7 content:seed --dry-run` shows what would change, `--apply` writes the drafts. Nothing is published from code; the owner publishes in the Studio. The token is `SANITY_API_WRITE_TOKEN_FLIP_7`
- Art (27 slots: the 22 cards, number then action then modifier, then the card back, the box front and back, the table mat and the logo) lives outside the repo, in one flat folder (e.g. `~/Companies/PBGO/assets/flip-7`), under the slot's name, which is the key of the engine's catalogue (`number-7.png`, `plus-4.png`, `freeze.png`, `card-back.png`…; the figure printed on the card works too: `7.png`, `+4.png`, `x2.png`), and is placed with an absolute `--art-dir`. A card is one picture for every copy of it. It can also be dropped straight onto the "Visual" field of each card in the Studio
- `pnpm --filter @pbgo-game/flip-7 test` and `typecheck` cover `content/` (no network, nothing written)
- `content/parity/engine.test.ts` checks the seed against the engine, read from `@flip-7/engine` itself (only Vitest loads it here: the seed runs with Node alone and never imports it): the category of each card (the engine's `kind`), the keys and their order within each category, the categories the Studio lists (also at compile time), the copies of each card against `DECK`, the titles the Studio lists the figure cards under against the values of the cards, the default `targetScore`, the words of the engine (the keys `engineTextKeys` asks for, keyed as the Copy page keys them) and the message of each code of `ERROR_CODES`
- The `texts` draft of the seed holds three things: the words of the engine (`engineTexts`), the message of `CANNOT_STAY` (`errorMessages`) and the 25 words of the board (`BOARD_WORDS` of `content/data/texts.ts`, one `localeString` each under the name of its field): the buttons, the status of a player, the two announcements that are the game's own and the sentences of the history (what a player did, with who left out: the ticker of the HUD names the player as the heading of their turn), with `{target}`, `{card}`, `{points}` and `{number}` for the board to fill in (`fill` of `src/lib/words.ts`). English is the base, French follows the glossary, Spanish and German are written by an agent and are for the owner to read. Three lists say the same names: the Board tab of the Studio (`schemaTypes/texts.ts`), `WORD_FIELDS` of the binding and the seed, and `board-words.test.ts` (of the seed and of the Studio) keeps them equal, with the same placeholders in the four languages and named in the title of each field. A change of the Studio's `texts` needs `pnpm --filter flip-7-studio typegen` (its types are committed) and the owner's deploy of the hosted Studio
- The whole process, the options and the art naming: `docs/content-pipeline.md` at the root

## The board
The table is one scene on `@pbgo/table` (`src/components/board/scene.tsx`), a function of the view: the same view gives the same poses on every client. The screen around it (the HUD, the players, the chat, the menu) is core's.

| Zone | Block | A player can press there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The seat of each player, the viewer in front and the others around the far half in turn order, with the total under the name and the word busted, frozen or stayed | `PlayerSeat` | the plate of a player who can get the action card the viewer holds |
| The line in front of a player: every card face up, in the order it came, closer together as the line grows | `Zone` over `row` | nothing |
| The cards set aside during a Flip Three (a Freeze or a Flip Three that waits), small, beyond the line | `Zone` | nothing |
| The draw pile, a count (only its size is known), with its number under it | `Pile` | its top card, on the viewer's turn: Hit |
| The discard, face up | `Zone` over `pile` | nothing |
| Stay, beside the discard, when the view says the viewer can stay | `Paper` | Stay |
| Deal, in the middle, for the dealer between two rounds | `Paper` | Deal |
| The card to give, in the middle for everybody, with the `selection` frame for the one who gives it | `Zone` | nothing: the plates above are what is pressed |

- Nothing is hidden in Flip 7, so every line is face up, and every line reads upright from the viewer's side, whatever the seat: the cards do not take the yaw of their seat
- A table of 3 and a table of 8 cannot share a ring: the knobs of the layout come in pairs (`radiusX3` and `radiusX8`, the margin, the scale of the opponents' cards) and `tableOf(n)` goes from one to the other, so a table is tuned at both ends, in the Layout folder of the debug panel
- How far the camera stands is no knob: it is fitted to the window (`fitCamera` of `camera.ts`). The log of the turns at the top left (text laid on the table, whose room is kept at 320 px) and the menu and the list of the players at the top right are drawn over the board, so the camera stands as near as it can with every plate, line, pile and the button in the window and clear of those boxes (`HUD`, their size in pixels, measured on core's HUD, which publishes none: when the HUD changes these numbers change, and `camera.test.ts` says what must still hold), a little farther for a table that stands more in the middle of the window. The rig of the table backs the camera off below a 16:10 window (`aspectScale`), so the fit works out where the camera really stands. A window too small for the table (8 players need about 1180 by 820, 3 players fit in 1024 by 640, and 900 by 600 fits none) gets the table as small as still reads, with the least of it under the boxes
- The viewer's one button lies in the middle band of the table, never in the bottom one that the cookie banner covers, beside the discard and not in front of the viewer's line: Stay on a turn, and the same paper in the same place for Deal between two rounds
- The draw pile's top card is turned up with the back for its picture, which looks the same, because a card that lies face down shows no halo, outline or shine (see Friction); its glow, on the viewer's turn, is what says that it can be pressed
- A move is paced from its lines in the log (`pacing.ts`, `timelineOf`): the order of the events gives each card that the last move turned over the seconds it waits before it flies in from above the draw pile, so the three cards of a Flip Three come one after the other, after the action card that makes them flip, and the opening deal goes round the table. A card that only changes place (a Freeze or a Second Chance given, the cards of a bust that go to the discard) flies from where it lay: the table remembers a card by its id. The view is drawn at once; what it holds back waits for the move to play out: the announcements (`paused` of `useAnnouncements`) and what a seat says, the word under a name and the total. The pace is the Pace folder of the debug panel (`PACE_SETTINGS`)
- A table that is opened or reloaded plays nothing, the cards are where they lie (`openedAt` of `game.tsx`), but for the opening deal, which is drawn when the start screen goes; a visitor who asks for less motion has no waiting at all. The players' strip and the ticker of the HUD are core's and show the result of a move at once
- The Rig the deck folder of the debug panel puts a Freeze, a Flip Three, a Second Chance, a number the player holds or the seventh number of a Flip 7 on top of the draw pile (the engine's own debug commands), to see each case at once
- The colors are the settings' (`useSiteSettings().theme`) and `skin.playerColors`, the pictures and the words come from the catalog; the mat is a gradient from `surface` to `surfaceDark`, painted as a texture (`matTexture`), since the game has no mat picture. The card back is a stand-in until the owner hands it over

## Making it playable
The engine is done, the binding exports it (`GAME`, `ERROR_CODES` and the three tests of `src`), gives the board its data (`loadGameData`: the 22 faces, the back, the names of the action cards and the 25 words, from Sanity) and draws the table. What is left:
- The edges: the phone (below 900 px the platform covers every board with its desktop-only screen, so nothing was seen on one; laptops and tablets, held either way, are fitted) and what the owner reads on the table once the words are published (the length of the announcements and of the sentences)
- The drafts of the seed, published in the Studio after the owner has deployed the hosted Studio: the words of the engine and of the board and the message of `CANNOT_STAY` (Spanish and German to read), and the `game` and `settings` drafts. Until then every word of the board reads `[missing: <key>]`, which is what every check so far saw
- The card back, handed over by the owner
- Then set `live` (and `indexable` once it is ready for search engines) in `packages/games/src/registry.ts`. See `template/CLAUDE.md` ("Adapting to a new game")

## Friction (pilot of the process)
What got in the way while Flip 7 went from its rulebook toward a game that is live, the pilot of the way a game is built: kept here so that the skills and the checks of that process are changed from it (they are in `.claude/skills/README.md`, "A game from A to Z", and `pnpm check:game flip-7` says how far the game has got). One line each, newest last; a line that was fixed says where.
- Engine brought up to date (the first slice of the pilot): the contract suite had never been run on this engine, and the kit had moved since it was written. It found the words in the engine and, once `progress` was added, that `progress` itself must stay between 0 and 100 (the kit's `progressOf` clamps it, the suite wants the definition's own to be sound).
- `secretsFor` of the contract suite was typed `readonly string[]`, but the opaque ids of the kit are numbers: a string `"123"` is never found in a view that holds `123`, so a leak could not have been caught. The type is `readonly (string | number)[]` now (`packages/engine-kit/src/conformance.ts`, with a test and the line in `docs/engine/04-build-a-game/06-testing.md`).
- `localeText` of the content kit asks for the four languages: a seed of words is written in French, English, Spanish and German at once, which an agent can only do by translating, so the Spanish and the German are for the owner to read. A second game would write the same ten lines of `texts.ts`: a helper in the content kit that turns `[key, fr, en, es, de]` rows into the `engineTexts` document would remove them.
- The dry run of the seed (`content:seed --dry-run`, read-only, the token is never printed) works from any checkout that has `apps/web/.env.local` with the game's token; the first step wrongly said this worktree had none. On 2026-11-11 it read the project `ky3exlhe` and said 24 documents unchanged (the cards, the game, the settings) and `texts` one new draft: the six words of the engine and the message of `CANNOT_STAY` are the whole of it. The `--apply` stays the owner's word to give.
- The binding's `vitest.config.ts` included only `content/**`: tests added to `src` would never have run. Fixed in the binding step (`src/**` and `content/**` now), and `check:game` fails when it is not so.
- The binding step brought what the older games lack, all of it the template's: the three tests of `src` (the contract suite moved here from the engine package, where it ran since the first step and would have run twice, 13 s each), and in the Studio the guard `studio-forms.test.ts` with the `test` script and the dev dependencies it needs.
- `ERROR_CODES`: the notes listed five codes to word in Sanity, written before `SHARED_ERROR_CODES` settled. The kit already shares four of the five, as it does the two the platform translates: only `CANNOT_STAY` is the game's own. `errorVocabularyProblems` tells which, the notes did not.
- `takeover.test.ts` is the template's file, about 200 lines of which `humanMove` and the assertions are the game's; `jsonb`, `stamp`, `takeover` and `finishAlone` are the same in every game. A helper next to `castVotes` in the takeover module of core would leave a game with its two functions.
- A binding that exports `GAME` and has no `Game` is playable outside production up to a table that never loads: `GameClient` has no board to draw, so the players of a started table read "Loading the table": for as long as the page stays open after a debug start, and after a normal start until the ready check (a minute) gives up and takes everybody back to the game's page, since nobody can press "I'm here" without a board (seen on the local server, 2026-11-11). Only previews and local development see it, since `live: false` keeps the page "coming soon" in production; the skill and the checklist said "playable as soon as `GAME` is exported" and did not say it.
- The words of the board (the data step of the board): the template has no model for a game's own words, Courtisans' `catalog.ts` and Studio `texts` were the one (`createTexts` with a Board tab, the field names listed in the binding, the loader that reads the language asked, else English, else `[missing: <key>]`). The names live in three places, the Studio, the binding and the seed, and nothing tied them until `board-words.test.ts`; typegen runs offline and its 826 lines are committed. The text rules of the seed shaped the wording: a prompt is a question (`Who gets {card}?`, no ellipsis at the end) and no sentence agrees in gender with a card, which may be a figure or the name of an action card (`discards {card}`). The first wording had the player's name in every sentence: the ticker (`groupTurns` of core) already heads each turn with it, which only showed when the HUD's API was read, so the sentences were rewritten before anything was committed.
- The seed's engine texts were keyed `option-targetScore-label` where the Copy page of the gamemaster keys the same items `option_targetScore_label`: a seed run after a save in the Copy page would have added every text a second time (the merge pairs the items by `_key`). Fixed to the page's rule, with a test; nothing had been applied.
- The pictures of the cards are read by the browser from the Sanity CDN, into a canvas, so they need the project's CORS: `go-live.sh` lists the production domain, `http://localhost:3100` and the Studio only, and the CDN answers 403 to any other origin. Seen on `p1.localhost:3100`, the host of a second cookie jar, where every card read a stand-in; the origin of a Vercel preview is not listed either. The two seats of a check are therefore the viewer on `localhost` and the others through the API, which `engine-board` says now, and a preview would show stand-ins until `https://*.vercel.app` is among the project's origins.
- The cookie banner floats over the bottom of the table: the rule is in `docs/design/01-guidelines/04-in-game-rules.md` and was not in the skill, so the first Stay paper lay in front of the viewer's line and the pointer pressed the banner. It is in `engine-board` now; the controls lie in the middle band.
- A card that lies face down shows no glow: the halo and the outline are planes behind the card whose front looks at its face, so a card turned over turns them away from the camera, and the shine, the frame and the sparkle lie on the face, under it. Found by turning a glow up to the extreme and seeing nothing, then in `card.tsx`. The draw pile says that it can be pressed by glowing, so its top card is turned up with the back for its picture (`top.front`), which looks the same. A `Card` that draws its glow on whichever side is up would let a pile glow face down; the docs of the glows and of `Pile` say now that a face-down card shows none.
- One ring and one camera do not serve 3 to 8 seats: with one set of knobs the seats of 8 crowded and hid under the HUD while those of 3 floated far apart. The knobs are in pairs, for 3 and for 8, and `tableOf` goes between them; the template has one ring and does not show how. The checks with 3, 5 and 8 seats found it, and `engine-board` now asks for them from the first slice. How far the camera stands is no knob of the ring: see the next lines.
- The phone check of the skill cannot be made: below 900 px the platform covers every game page with its desktop-only screen (`interface.desktopOnly`, true by default, set in the Gamemaster), so the board has no phone to be checked on until the owner decides it. It was tuned for a desktop window, 1280 by 800, at 3, 5 and 8 seats.
- What a player can press was checked with a script written for the purpose, kept in a scratchpad: a headless Chromium driven over the DevTools protocol, a place of the table projected through the camera to a pixel, pressed with the mouse, and the version of the table read through the API to see that the move went through (the draw pile, the Stay and Deal papers and the plate of a player), the other seats played through the API. The next game writes it again; a script of the repo that takes the camera and the places to press would be the way to check a board that nobody can open in the Browser pane, which does not paint 3D out of view.
- The `stagger` of a zone is the place of a card in its list, not its place among the cards that arrive: in a line that grows, a card that comes alone waits for its rank (the seventh card of a line waited 0.6 s at 0.1 s a card), and the three cards of a Flip Three, which come together, flew almost at once. The board gives each card of the last move its own `from` with a `delay` taken from the order of the events (`timelineOf`), and the docs of `Zone` say so now.
- `usePlayLog` is the documented way to animate from the log, and the board does not use it. It had to know, in the very render that receives a move, that the move is still playing, to hold back the announcements and the words under the seats, and the `busy` of the hook turns true one render later, after the effect that gives the lines to its player. The board derives it from the version of the log, the greatest `v`, and a timer; the docs of the log say so. The first reading plays nothing, as the hook does, but for the opening deal, which waits behind the start screen.
- A move is checked in frames, not in a screenshot: a flight lasts under a second and a Flip Three has three. The check records the page with a screencast over the DevTools protocol (`Page.startScreencast`), starts its sheet at the first frame where the text of the page changes (the page learns of a move about 0.4 s after the request) and tiles nine frames into one picture with ffmpeg. The cases are reached with the engine's own debug commands (`freezeOnTop`, `flipThreeOnTop`, `secondChanceOnTop`, `bustOnTop`, `flip7`), which any seated player may send; the Rig the deck folder of the panel has a button for each. The script is in a scratchpad again.
- The boxes that the platform draws over the board hid seats. The log of the turns, a card 320 by 310 px at the top left, hid the leftmost plate of a table of 8 at 1280 by 800 and the one of a table of 3 on a tablet held across (1024 wide), and the list of the players, at the top right, the rightmost. A fixed camera cannot serve every window, so the board fits its camera to the window and to those boxes (`fitCamera`). Core publishes no size for its HUD: they are numbers measured in pixels in `camera.ts`, and the HUD changed under the work (the log became text laid on the table, in a box 512 px wide and 299 high of which the text takes what its longest line needs), so they were measured again (`HUD`: the strip 240 wide, from 84 px down, 47.5 px a player). A hook of core that gave the free zone of the table would replace them, for every game.
- The rig of the table already backs the camera off below a 16:10 window (`aspectScale`, internal to the kit), which the first fit ignored: a tablet held upright showed a table twice too small. The package exports `aspectScale` now and the fit works the real distance out with it.
- A visitor who does not sit at a started table reads "This game has already started": the platform has no spectator, so the third tab of the skill's check cannot be made, and the board's path for a viewer with no seat (`view.me === null`) is covered by the layout tests only.
- A table of 8 starts by itself when the eighth player joins, so the host's `start` then answers `GAME_IN_PROGRESS` and the options can no longer be changed (409): a script that seats 8 and then starts must expect it.
