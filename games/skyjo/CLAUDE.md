# Skyjo · Play Board Games Online

Skyjo (Alexander Bernhardt, Magilano; French edition by Blackrock Games), served by `apps/web` at `/skyjo`. The engine is written from the French booklet (4 pages), was checked on 2026-11-18 against the publisher's own booklet (ten languages) and is tested, the board is drawn on `@pbgo/table`, and the Sanity content is described and seeded as code (see "Status" for what was and was not done). Live and indexable: the switches were set in the registry on 2026-11-18 (GAM-68), so the hub lists it and `/skyjo` shows its page. 2 to 8 players; the web edition is not official.

### How a game goes
- `step(state, action)` gives `{ state, events }` (`SkyjoLogEntry`; the `start` event says `how` the first player was found, `"sum"` or `"ender"`). The state is lean: ids and numbers. `grids[seat]` is 12 cells, `column * 3 + row`, each `{ card, up }` or `null` once its column was thrown away; `stage` is the one thing the round waits on
- Stages: `reveal` (everybody turns two cards over, simultaneous: `waitingFor` lists every player who still has to; the stage carries `starter`, the seat of the player who ended the round before, `null` in the first round, and the view says it as `phase.starter`) → `choose` (the player who begins is the `starter`, in the first round the highest sum; take the discard or draw) → `place` (a card in hand: from the discard it must be placed, from the pile it may be placed or thrown away) → `flip` (after a throw-away, one face-down card turns over) → next player … → `between` (the round is counted, the dealer deals) → `reveal` …
- Hidden: the cards face down (to everybody, their owner too, so a face-down cell is `{ up: false }` and carries no id), the order of the draw pile (the view gives its size) and the card just drawn from it (seen by the holder only; the log entry of the draw has `to` and a `masked` version). The card taken from the discard was seen by all
- The draw pile runs out: the discard, but its top card, is shuffled into a new pile (`reshuffleDiscard` with `keep: 1`); at 8 players that happens in nearly every round

### Rules as coded
Pages: EN is the English text of the publisher's booklet (Magilano), by its printed pages; FR is the French booklet of Blackrock Games, by the page of its PDF (p.1 is the cover; its figures 1 to 3 are numbered in it). The publisher's booklet has the same text in ten languages (DE p.6-7 is the original, then IT p.8-9, FR p.10-11, ES p.12-13, NL, DA, FI, SV, NO), so "the booklet" below is that text. The Blackrock booklet, which the engine was first written from, leaves out one sentence of it (who begins the later rounds). Checked rule by rule against both on 2026-11-18 (GAM-66).
- 150 cards: five -2, ten -1, fifteen 0, ten of each from 1 to 12 (EN p.24, FR p.2). 2 to 8 players (EN p.2)
- A round: each player gets 12 cards face down in 4 columns of 3, one card from the pile starts the discard, each player turns two cards of their own over at will (EN p.4, FR p.2). All 150 cards are shuffled again for every round (EN p.4: "the well-shuffled deck of cards")
- Who begins: in the first round the player whose two cards add up highest (EN p.4, FR p.2); in every later round the player who ended the round before, whatever the sums (EN p.4: "The player who ended the current game round gets to start the next game round"; the Blackrock booklet does not say it). The turn goes clockwise
- A turn: take the top of the discard and swap it with one of your 12 cards, face up or down (the replaced card goes face up on the discard); or draw the top of the pile and either swap it likewise or throw it on the discard and turn one face-down card over (EN p.4, FR p.2-3). A card taken from the discard must be placed (EN p.4 "has to exchange it", FR p.3: `MUST_PLACE`)
- Three equal cards face up in a column go to the discard, after the replaced card (EN p.5 Image II, FR p.3 fig. 2); also when a card is turned over, and at the count when the last cards are turned. The three are equal, so which of them lies on top cannot be told; a placement can only make one column, the count can make several
- The round ends when a player has all their cards face up (columns thrown away leave no card to count): every other player plays one last turn (EN p.4, FR p.3). Then all cards are turned, columns of three equal cards go, each player scores the sum of their cards (a negative total lowers the score: FR p.4), and the ender's points are doubled if they are not strictly the lowest of the round (a tie doubles: "fewer or the same amount", EN p.4), but only when positive (EN p.4, FR p.4). The booklets' example, 10, 24 and 10, gives 20 (EN p.5 Image I, FR p.4 fig. 3)
- The game ends when a player's total reaches 100 or more at the end of a round; the lowest total wins (EN p.4-5, FR p.2 and p.4). No option: the target is 100 (`TARGET_SCORE`); a shorter game is a later option, not coded
- **Where the booklets are silent, the engine decides as follows (the owner validated these readings on 2026-11-18, after the audit):**
  1. A tie for the highest sum of the two opening cards, which only the first round has: one of the tied players, drawn from the seed. It used to be the first of them clockwise from the dealer's left, which favoured the tied player after the longest run of untied seats (two neighbours tied at 8 seats: one of them began 7 times in 8)
  2. The dealer is in neither booklet: the first one is drawn from the seed, after each round the deal passes one seat clockwise, and the dealer's only duty is to press Deal between two rounds (it does not decide who begins)
  3. The two opening cards are turned in any order by all the players at once, final, and everybody sees them as they are turned
  4. A tie for the lowest total at the end: all the tied players win (the booklets say "the player with the lowest score", `roundsOutcome` with `tie: "shared"`; the other reading, another round until the tie breaks, is `tie: "playOn"`)
  5. A player who ends the round by throwing away their last column (no card left) is the ender, with a count of 0
  6. When the draw pile is empty the discard, but its top card, is shuffled into a new pile when somebody draws (neither booklet says so: it is the usual table rule). It is no rare case: 53 cards are left in the pile after the deal at 8 players, and with the absent-player strategy the pile ran out in 32 rounds of 32 at 8 players (1 of 48 at 6). If even that finds no card, drawing is refused (`INVALID_PHASE`) and the discard can still be taken, which cannot happen since the grids hold 96 cards at most

### Absent player
`autoPlayFor` plays a whole turn (not one click): it turns the first cards of the grid (positions 0, 3 then the other column tops), takes the top of the discard when it is 4 or less and it helps (or completes a column), else draws; a card goes where it completes a column, else over a face-up card at least two points higher, else over a face-down card when it is 4 or less, else it is thrown away and the first face-down card is turned over; between two rounds it deals. Deterministic, no random number; every turn turns a card over or lowers a card, so a round always ends. `NOT_WAITING` when the game is not waiting for them.

### Errors
`SKYJO_ERROR_CODES` in `types.ts`. The shared ones are worded by the platform; `INVALID_POSITION`, `CARD_FACE_UP` and `MUST_PLACE` are the game's own.

### Tests
`pnpm --filter @skyjo/engine test` and `typecheck`. A file per part (catalogue, scoring, setup, turns, rounds, view, autoplay, debug, game), each rule with the page of the booklet, `simulation.test.ts` (2 to 8 players, every size twice, one with a third of the moves played for absent players, the count of every round written again from the names of the faces, who begins every round, invariants after every move, junk actions) and `conformance.test.ts` (the contract of the kit).

### Friction
None that blocks. The kit had what the rules need (`reshuffleDiscard`, `roundsOutcome`, the stream carried in the state). One small gap: it has no helper to pick one element at random, so `beginTurns` draws among tied players by hand (`Math.floor(rng() * tied.length)`); worth a helper if a second game needs it.

## The board
One scene on `@pbgo/table` (`scene.tsx`), a function of the view. The screen around it (HUD, players, chat, menu) is core's.

| Zone | Block | A player can press there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The seat of each player, the viewer in front and the others all round the table in turn order, each turned toward the middle, with the total under the name and a word (last turn, ended the round, the points of the round) | `PlayerSeat` | nothing |
| The grid of each player: 4 columns of 3 cards, face down until turned, a hole where a column was thrown away; the viewer's is the biggest, the others' are smaller, a gap of a tenth of a card between neighbours both ways (`columnGap` and `rowStep` 1.1): nothing overlaps, the figures stay readable by the size of the cards (`layout.test.ts` holds that no two cards touch, and that no zone overlaps another zone, a plate, a pile or a pad) | `Zone` | the viewer's cards, each its own floor zone (`cellZone`: the card and the room round it, the twelve zones tile the grid), while there is a card to choose: two to open the round, any card while holding one, a face-down one after a throw-away |
| The draw pile (count written on its top card), left of the middle | `Pile` | its top card, on the viewer's choice: draw |
| The card in hand, between the piles: face up for who drew it (and for everybody when it came from the discard), a back for the others | `Zone` | nothing |
| The discard, right of the middle: the card that was on top stays under the new one; the cards of a thrown column fly there | `Zone` | its top card, on the viewer's choice: take |
| Draw (under the pile) and Take the discard (under the discard) on the viewer's choice; Discard and flip (under the discard) while holding a drawn card; Deal across both for the dealer between two rounds | `FloorButton` | the pads; Tab, Enter and Space too |
| The instruction of the moment, flat on the table above the piles | `TableText` | nothing |

- **Look.** Skyjo's own: the table is the dark end of the rainbow of the card back, a deep indigo felt (`surfaceDark` #120b2c) with a lighter violet pool in the middle (`surface` #251760) and a faint conic rainbow turning through the pool (`RAINBOW`, strength 0.14, in `lib/palette.ts`), over a cracked-glass mosaic (the `mosaic` layer of the table's mat, low contrast, nine cells to a repeat of 5 world units: the mat is data, `matOf` in `textures.ts`, which the shared `Table` bakes, so no texture is painted by the game and no image is an asset). The ink is a lilac white (#f7f3ff), the instruction is written like the logo (white lettering, thick indigo outline) and the accent is the azure the booklet is printed on (#4aa8f0). The cards bring the full colours; the dark blue -2 and -1 stay readable on the indigo thanks to their white glow and rim. The pads keep to quiet tones that are no card colour (`lib/floor-tones.ts`: azure to draw and deal, amber to take, lilac grey to throw away, pale lilac for the cells)
- **Layout.** The ring is Flip 7's (`seatAngle`, `seatAt`, `seatSpots`, `plateAt`, mirrored in `layout.ts` with its tests: Flip 7 keeps them with its own knobs, so nothing was lifted into the kit) for 2 to 8 seats; the knobs come in pairs for the fewest and the most seats (`tableOf`). The numbers were searched, not guessed: every plate, grid, pile and strip of every table of 2 to 8 keeps apart from the others (`layout.test.ts`, separating-axis tests) and the camera fits all of them in 1280x720, 1024x768 and the other windows of `camera.test.ts`
- **Camera.** `fitCamera` as Flip 7, with the platform's boxes it keeps the table clear of (the log, the list of the players, and here the message box at the bottom right) and a lower `LEAST_UNIT` (30, Flip 7's is 38): eight seats of twelve cards must fit a tablet held across, so the cards of the seats at the far side are small there (about 15–20 px wide at 8 seats on 1024x768). A spectator of eight seats on 1024x768 is the one corner that does not fit (there is no spectator on the platform). `fitCamera` says the table fits (clear of the boxes, at 30 px a unit or more) at every number of seats in 1280x800, 1280x720, 1024x768 and 900x1200, at 2 seats in 1024x640 and 900x600 and at 5 seats in 1024x640; it does not at 8 seats in 1024x640 and 900x600, nor at 5 seats in 900x600 (see the last Friction line)
- **Cards.** The faces and the back are SVGs of 296 by 476 with a white contour 16 units wide, its outer corners an arc of 25 units and transparent beyond. `CARD.radius` (`layout.ts`) cuts the card where the contour is: the table's default (6% of the width) left a black crescent in each corner, where the transparent pixels of the picture lay on the card, and 25 units left a hairline, since the table draws a corner as a quadratic curve (29.3 units puts it on the arc at 45 degrees and inside it elsewhere). The textures are painted at 640 px across (`ACROSS` of `textures.ts`) from a JPEG of about 300 px inside the SVG: 1280 changes nothing visible (SSIM 0.999 on a card 900 px wide)
- **History.** The start of the first round gives the sum that found who begins (`eventStart`); every later round is begun by the player who ended the one before, whatever the sums (`how: "ender"` of the `start` event), and says nothing: the line is left out (`sentenceOf` gives null), the turn is headed by the player. The discard shuffled into a new pile is shown under the player whose draw found the pile empty (the line comes right before that draw, `actorsOf`), the count of a round under the last player to have moved
- **Start.** The cards of the opening deal are not on the table while the start screen is up (`held`): they mount, and are dealt round the table, when it goes. A card that is already there when its entrance is given does not fly, so a board that draws them behind the screen shows the table dealt and plays nothing
- **Words.** Everything the board writes is `words.<key>` of the catalog (`WORD_FIELDS`); `content/data/board-words.test.ts` fails on a word used and not seeded, and on a word seeded and not used. The figure on a card is the engine's value, no word
- **Pace.** A move is paced from its lines in the log (`pacing.ts`, `timelineOf`): a deal goes round the table card by card, the card in hand arrives before it is placed, the replaced card and the placed one fly together, a thrown column flies card by card from its cells to the discard and lies there until the move has played out. The words that say how a move ended (the totals, the last-turn word) wait for it; a table that is opened or reloaded plays nothing, but for the opening deal, which is dealt when the start screen goes (see Start)
- **Friction.** No block for the number of cards of a pile: it is written on the top card (`children` of `PileTop`), since a count written on the table next to a stack is hidden from the camera by the stack. The cells' floor zones lie under the cards, so at rest only their rims between the cards show; they lift under the pointer or the focus
- **Friction, from the first run of `pnpm check:board` (GAM-67).** The look of a board that nobody had opened with the real pictures showed four things, all fixed: the first deal of a game was never dealt (the cards stood on the table behind the start screen, and a card that is there when its entrance is given does not fly: Flip 7 mounts them when the screen goes, `held` of `scene.tsx` does it here now), the corners of the cards read black (the table's default radius is less than the picture's, `CARD.radius`), the shuffle of the discard was written under the player before the one who drew, and the start of a later round gave a sum that had decided nothing. The tool taught two things about its own cases: a debug command that plays a turn writes nothing to the log of the table, so a case recorded with one shows a board that plays nothing (the recorded moves are real actions), and `nearTarget` writes a finished round down, so it ends the game at once once a round was counted (it is for a table at its start: `over` runs on its own, `--scenarios over`)
- **Seen in that run and left as it is.** (1) At 8 seats in 1024 by 640 and 900 by 600, and 5 seats in 900 by 600, the camera cannot keep the table clear of the platform's boxes at 30 px a unit (`fits` is false): it takes the farthest camera and the place where the least of the table is under them, so the table is whole and clear of them but small and in the upper half of the window. (2) The cookie banner, until it is answered, covers the viewer's third row in the small windows (900 by 600, 1024 by 640: those four cells cannot be pressed until then, the two rows above can). (3) The platform's "Play for them" pill, shown when a player does not answer, lies over the plate and the first row of the seat across at 2 seats. (4) Core's announcements wait for the move before to play out (`paused` of `useAnnouncements`), so with machine-speed moves a "Your turn" can come after the viewer has moved, and "Let's play!" is announced again after a reload in the middle of a game: the same code as Flip 7's. (5) The phone: below 900 px the platform covers every game page with its desktop-only screen, so nothing was seen on one. (6) The check never reached the viewer's Deal pad at 8 seats (the viewer deals once in eight rounds and the game ends first); at 2 and 5 seats it pressed it
